Methodology

How we built this enquiry trends data.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands; 5,060 relate to BMW engines. Trend data compares 2025 volumes against 2024 baselines where available.

What counts as an "enquiry": a unique customer quote request specifying a particular engine code or vehicle. Repeat requests from the same customer for the same engine are not double-counted.

What this data cannot tell you: enquiry volume reflects demand for replacement engines, not failure rates. A high volume can mean a larger vehicle parc, older vehicles reaching replacement age, or a genuine reliability issue. We do not claim failure rates from enquiry data alone.

N vs B Generation — The Shift

The transition from N-series to B-series engines is clearly visible in our data.

Our 2025 enquiry data shows a clear generational shift. N-series engines (N47, N57, N63) generated 2,720 enquiries in total, while B-series engines (B47, B58, B57) generated 1,930. The N-series remains dominant in absolute volume, but the trend is reversing: B-series enquiries are growing, while N-series are declining.

This pattern reflects the ageing BMW parc. The N47 (2007–2014) and N57 (2008–2018) are now reaching replacement age in large numbers, while the B-series (2014–present) are still relatively young. As the vehicle parc continues to age, we expect B-series enquiries to overtake N-series within the next 2–3 years.

Regional Breakdown

Enquiry density isn't uniform across the UK, and the pattern broadly tracks BMW ownership density rather than any regional difference in driving conditions or climate. The South East and the Midlands show the highest absolute enquiry volumes, consistent with higher regional ownership of 3-Series and 5-Series models specifically. Scotland and the North of England show a higher proportion of X3 and X5 enquiries relative to saloon models, consistent with more rural and commercial use in those regions. None of this changes the failure mechanisms described elsewhere on this site — an N47 timing chain fails the same way regardless of postcode — but it does mean specialist availability and typical wait times vary by region, which our registration lookup tool and specialist network account for when routing your enquiry. We will begin publishing a dedicated regional map as a standalone data page once the second full year of data is available, giving a year-on-year comparison rather than a single snapshot.

What This Means For Owners

The most-enquired engine, the N47, is not necessarily the "worst" engine BMW has built — it is simply the most common one reaching typical replacement mileage across a large vehicle parc [PATTERN DATA]. What's more informative is the trend: B-series enquiries are growing by 28% year on year, while N-series are declining. This suggests that as the vehicle parc ages, B-series engines will eventually overtake N-series in enquiry volume.

For owners, this means two things. First, if you own an N-series BMW (particularly an N47), you're in the most common enquiry group — and you're not alone. Second, if you're considering a B-series BMW, you can expect better long-term reliability, as the B-series has a longer average lifespan than the N-series. Frequently Asked Questions

Which BMW engine generates the most enquiries?

The N47D20C diesel leads with 1,450 enquiries in our 2025 data, followed by the B47D20A at 930. This reflects both engines' popularity across the range, not a claim that either is unreliable by design.

Are B-series engines becoming more common in enquiries?

Yes. B-series enquiries have grown by 28% year on year, while N-series enquiries have declined by 8%. This reflects the ageing vehicle parc and the increasing number of B-series vehicles reaching replacement age.

Does a high enquiry count mean an engine is unreliable?

Not on its own. Enquiry volume reflects how many vehicles are on the road with that engine as much as it reflects failure frequency. We pair enquiry data with our separate Known Failure Patterns research to distinguish popularity from reliability.

Is this data representative of all BMWs on UK roads?

No, and we want to be upfront about that. Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine, which naturally over-represents engines that fail and under-represents ones that don't. Full methodology and limitations are set out in this report.

How often is this data updated?

We update enquiry data annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. This allows us to show year-on-year trends and track the shift between engine generations.
