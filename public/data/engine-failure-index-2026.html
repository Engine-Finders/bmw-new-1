Methodology

What this data is, what it isn't, and how we built it.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands we cover; 5,060 of these relate specifically to BMW engines.

What counts as an "enquiry": a unique customer quote request specifying a particular engine code or vehicle. Repeat requests from the same customer for the same engine are not double-counted.

What this data cannot tell you: our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine — it is not a random sample of every BMW on UK roads. A higher enquiry count can mean an engine is more failure-prone, or simply that more of that engine exist in the parc, or that it's an older engine reaching typical replacement age. We do not claim failure rates against the total vehicle population; only enquiry share within our own dataset.

Refresh cycle: annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. Figures are locked at time of publication and are not silently revised — any correction is logged on our public corrections page rather than edited away.

Regional Breakdown

Enquiry density isn't uniform across the UK, and the pattern broadly tracks BMW ownership density rather than any regional difference in driving conditions or climate. The South East and the Midlands show the highest absolute enquiry volumes, consistent with higher regional ownership of 3-Series and 5-Series models specifically. Scotland and the North of England show a higher proportion of X3 and X5 enquiries relative to saloon models, consistent with more rural and commercial use in those regions. None of this changes the failure mechanisms described elsewhere on this site — an N47 timing chain fails the same way regardless of postcode — but it does mean specialist availability and typical wait times vary by region, which our registration lookup tool and specialist network account for when routing your enquiry. We will begin publishing a dedicated regional map as a standalone data page once the second full year of data is available, giving a year-on-year comparison rather than a single snapshot.

What This Means For Owners

The most-enquired engine, the N47, is not necessarily the "worst" engine BMW has built — it is simply the most common one reaching typical replacement mileage across a large vehicle parc [PATTERN DATA]. What's more informative is the R-to-V column: many engines on this list clear our 70% safety threshold, meaning replacement is financially sound on the typical host vehicle. The one flagged ⚠️ Watch — N47 — is an engine most often fitted to older, lower-value hosts (E90 3-Series, E60 5-Series), where the calculation is closer and worth checking individually using our Repair vs Replace Calculator. For the full breakdown behind any single figure on this page, see N47 Failure Rate, B47 Failure Rate, N57 Failure Rate and B58 Failure Rate.

It's also worth reading rank order alongside the underlying engine population rather than in isolation. The N47 and B47, for example, sit at the top of the list despite the B47 being a newer, more reliable engine — this reflects the sheer volume of N47-powered vehicles still on the road and reaching the age where replacements become necessary. Conversely, the S63's relatively low enquiry volume (410) despite its high replacement cost reflects its niche market position, not engineering superiority. This is the kind of nuance a bare ranking table can hide, and it's why we publish the full per-engine data pages rather than stopping at this summary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which BMW engine generates the most enquiries?

The N47D20C diesel leads with 1,450 enquiries in our 2025 data, followed by the B47D20A at 930. This reflects both engines' popularity across the range, not a claim that either is unreliable by design.

Does a high enquiry count mean an engine is unreliable?

Not on its own. Enquiry volume reflects how many vehicles are on the road with that engine as much as it reflects failure frequency. We pair enquiry data with our separate Known Failure Patterns research to distinguish popularity from reliability.

Is this data representative of all BMWs on UK roads?

No, and we want to be upfront about that. Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine, which naturally over-represents engines that fail and under-represents ones that don't. Full methodology and limitations are set out in this report.

How often is this report updated?

Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. This is our first published edition and establishes the baseline every future report will be measured against.
