Methodology – How We Calculated Average Replacement Costs

Every number on this page comes from real UK supplier quotes and workshop data. Here’s exactly how we built the cost figures.

Source: Proprietary supplier quote data from EnginesMarket.co.uk (our CRM) and direct interviews with 100+ UK engine specialists. Cross‑checked against eBay Motors and PistonHeads classifieds for market validation. All figures are for supply‑only unless stated as fitted. Labour rates are based on independent workshop averages of £65/hr (North England/Midlands). London/South East adds 40–60%.

What counts as a “cost”: We include the typical price ranges for used, reconditioned, and rebuilt engines. These are the three most common supply categories in our enquiry data. Labour hours are estimated per engine, and we calculate a “fitted” range by adding labour at the quoted hourly rate.

Limitations: These are indicative prices. Actual quotes can vary based on mileage, condition, warranty, geographic location, and whether ancillary parts (turbo, injectors, etc.) are included. We always recommend getting at least two independent quotes before committing.

Refresh cycle: This is our first published edition (2026 baseline). We will update annually each February.

Headline Findings – What the Data Tells Us

1. N47 is the cheapest common engine to replace – and the most enquired

With 1,450 requests, the N47D20C has a reconditioned supply cost of £3,200–£5,500 and a fitted total of £3,850–£6,410 [EM-QUOTE]. Used units are even cheaper, at £1,800–£3,200 supply. This makes it the most affordable BMW engine to replace, partly due to high availability.

2. B58 and B47 are mid‑range, costing £4,500–£7,000 fitted

The B58B30 (540 requests) has a reconditioned supply of £3,800–£6,000 and fitted range of £4,450–£6,910 [EM-QUOTE]. The B47D20A sits at similar levels. Both are modern units with good parts support, keeping costs reasonable compared to older V8s.

3. N63 and S63 are the most expensive – up to £15,500 fitted

The hot‑vee V8s (N63 and S63) command the highest prices. N63B44 recon supply is £6,000–£10,000, fitted £7,040–£11,430 [EM-QUOTE]. The S63 is even pricier at £9,000–£14,000 supply, fitted £10,170–£15,560 [EM-QUOTE]. These reflect both the complexity of the engines and the limited number of specialists willing to work on them.

4. Used engines are typically 30‑40% cheaper than reconditioned

Across all engines, the supply cost for a used unit is roughly 60–70% of a reconditioned one. However, used engines carry higher risk of unknown history and shorter remaining life, which is why many owners still opt for reconditioned.

5. Labour adds £520–£1,560 depending on engine complexity

Labour hours range from 8 hours (4‑cylinder diesels) to 24 hours (S63 V8). At £65/hr, this adds £520–£1,560 to the final fitted price. Main dealer labour can be 40–60% higher, so independent specialists are the most cost‑effective route for engine swaps.

What This Means For Owners

If you’ve been quoted an engine replacement, compare it to the typical ranges in this table. For a common N47 in a 3 Series, a reconditioned fitted cost of £5,000 is broadly in line with the market. But if the quote exceeds £7,000, it’s worth getting a second opinion – especially when you can use our calculator to weigh the cost against the car’s value.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost to replace a BMW engine?

Across our dataset, the average reconditioned supply cost is approximately £4,700, with a fitted average of around £5,900. However, this varies widely – from under £4,000 for an N47 to over £12,000 for an S63. The exact cost depends on the engine model, condition (used, recon, rebuilt), and labour rates.

Is it cheaper to buy a used engine or a reconditioned one?

Used engines are typically 30–40% cheaper than reconditioned ones. For example, a used N47 costs £1,800–£3,200 supply, whereas a reconditioned unit is £3,200–£5,500. However, reconditioned engines come with a warranty and known rebuild standards, which can be worth the extra cost if you plan to keep the car long‑term.

How much does labour add to the cost?

Labour hours range from 8 hours (4‑cylinder) to 24 hours (V8). At an independent rate of £65/hr, this adds between £520 and £1,560. Main dealers can charge £100–£150/hr, so the labour cost could be significantly higher.

Why is the N63 so much more expensive than the B58?

The N63 is a hot‑vee V8 design that is complex to work on – accessing components often requires extensive disassembly. It also has a higher failure rate and fewer specialists willing to work on it, which drives up both parts and labour costs. The B58, being a more modern inline‑6, is simpler and benefits from better parts availability.

Are these prices for supply only, or fitted?

The table shows supply‑only costs for used, reconditioned, and rebuilt engines. The fitted range is calculated by adding the labour cost (hours × £65) to the reconditioned supply cost. This gives a realistic fitted price, but we always recommend getting a written quote that includes all parts and labour before committing.
