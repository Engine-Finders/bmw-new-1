Failure Rate in Context

How the S63 compares to other BMW engines.

With 410 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], the S63 is the second most‑requested M‑engine after the N63 (440 enquiries) and ahead of the S55 (fewer than 20). It sits below the N57 (830) and B58 (540) in overall volume, but its position reflects the smaller M‑car parc.

The S63 shares the hot‑vee architecture of the N63, but benefits from more robust internal components and improved cooling. Specialists report that the S63 is significantly more reliable than the N63, with fewer catastrophic failures and a lower incidence of valve stem seal issues.

How Long Do S63 Engines Actually Last?

Typical lifespan and what determines it.

Based on specialist consensus and owner reports, the S63 typically reaches between 70,000 and 120,000 miles before any significant issues arise [PATTERN DATA]. Many well‑maintained examples exceed 150,000 miles without major failure.

The single biggest factor in longevity is maintenance — regular oil changes (every 8,000 miles or less) with the correct BMW LL‑01 spec oil, and avoiding aggressive track use without appropriate cooling upgrades. Engines that have been tuned aggressively (especially with aftermarket software) are at higher risk of timing chain issues.

In our dataset, the S63’s failure mileage distribution aligns with the 70–120k band reported by specialists. The median failure mileage is estimated at around 85,000 miles [PATTERN DATA].

The Verdict: Repair or Scrap?

Applying the 70% BMW scrap threshold.

With a fitted replacement cost often exceeding £11,000–£15,000 (including labour), the 70% scrap threshold means that for many S63‑powered M5/M6/X5M/X6M models worth under £20,000, a full engine failure could be financially borderline. However, the relatively low failure rate of the S63 compared to the N63 means that most owners will never face this decision.

In our Owner Decision Split data, the S63 is flagged as ✅ Safe because its replacement cost is typically well within the vehicle’s value for most examples. The high retained values of M‑cars (often £20,000–£80,000) absorb the replacement cost comfortably.

Our advice: If you own an S63 BMW and it’s worth more than £15,000, a reconditioned replacement is financially justifiable — but always get a specialist inspection first. If the vehicle is worth less than £12,000, consider scrapping or part‑exchanging it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common failure on the BMW S63 engine?

Timing chain tensioner wear is the most frequently reported issue, typically appearing between 70,000 and 120,000 miles. Regular inspection and early replacement of the tensioner can prevent catastrophic chain failure. Oil leaks from valve covers are also common but less severe.

Is the S63 more reliable than the N63?

Yes, the S63 is generally considered more reliable than the N63. It features stronger internal components, improved cooling, and a more robust design. In our 2025 data, the S63 generated 410 enquiries compared to the N63’s 440, despite the S63 being fitted to a smaller number of vehicles.

How much does it cost to replace a BMW S63 engine?

A reconditioned S63 supply‑only costs £9,000–£14,000 [EM-QUOTE]. With R&R labour (18–24 hours at ~£65–£100/hr), a fitted replacement is typically £11,000–£15,000 at an independent specialist. Main dealer costs are significantly higher.

What should I check before buying an S63‑powered BMW?

Check for oil leaks (valve covers), coolant leaks, and service history — regular oil changes with the correct LL‑01 spec are critical. A pre‑purchase inspection by a BMW specialist is strongly recommended, especially for high‑mileage or modified cars.

When should I scrap my S63 BMW instead of repairing it?

BMW’s 70% scrap threshold applies. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of the vehicle’s current market value, scrapping is usually the better financial decision. For a typical F10 M5 worth £15,000–£20,000, an £12,000 engine replacement may be borderline.
