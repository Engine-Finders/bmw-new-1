N47 Failure Rate in Context

With 1,450 enquiries in 2025, the N47 is the most-requested BMW engine — but what does that tell us about its actual failure rate?

Enquiry volume is not the same as failure rate. The N47 was fitted to millions of cars across the BMW range between 2007 and 2014, so the absolute number of enquiries is partly a reflection of population size. However, the known failure pattern — timing chain stretch leading to catastrophic valve-train damage — is real and well-documented. BMW issued Technical Service Bulletins (TSBs) on the issue, and specialists consistently report it as the top reason for N47 engine replacement [EM-INTERVIEW].

In our 2025 data, the N47's 1,450 enquiries place it:

  • Above the B47D20A (930) — the N47's successor, which is significantly more reliable
  • Above the N57D30 (830) — the larger six-cylinder diesel, which has a lower absolute enquiry count
  • Above the B58B30 (540) — a modern petrol engine with far fewer issues

What is more telling is the proportional failure rate when adjusted for vehicle parc size. Based on our specialist interviews and industry estimates, the N47 has a higher proportional failure rate than the B47 and the B58, but is similar to the N57's crankshaft and turbo issues [PATTERN DATA]. The N47's timing chain problem is particularly prevalent in pre-2011 engines with the older tensioner design.

N47 Mileage at Failure Distribution

When does an N47 typically fail? Our data suggests a cluster between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, with a notable peak around 80,000–90,000 miles.

This distribution is based on our [EM-INTERVIEW] data from specialists and [PATTERN DATA] from forum and TSB cross-reference. The timing chain tensioner is the primary culprit — it is a known weak point that tends to fail within this mileage band, especially on cars that have not had regular oil changes.

What the numbers show:

  • Pre-2011 N47 engines show a higher failure rate in this mileage band, consistent with the TSB on the tensioner design (BMW TSB SI B 11 03 14).
  • Post-2011 units with the revised tensioner and updated guides appear to survive longer — though we have seen failures beyond 120k as well.
  • Oil change history is the single biggest differentiator. Cars with 10,000-mile oil changes (or shorter) are much less likely to suffer timing chain failure than those on BMW's long-life 18,000-mile service schedule.

If your N47 is at 70,000–90,000 miles and you hear a rattle on cold start, that is the moment to act — not after the chain has slipped. A specialist can check tensioner wear during an oil service, and a £150–£300 inspection is far cheaper than a £3,200–£5,500 recon engine.

Most Common Failure Modes

While timing chain stretch is the headline issue, the N47 has other known weak points.

  • Timing chain stretch (primary): The rear-mounted chain and tensioner design is prone to wear, leading to rattling on cold start. If ignored, the chain can jump teeth, destroying the valve train. [PATTERN DATA]
  • Turbocharger wear: Oil supply issues can lead to turbo failure, especially on higher-mileage units. [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Injector leakage: Diesel injectors can fail, causing rough running, black smoke, and fuel dilution of oil. [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • DPF/EGR clogging: Common on urban-driven cars, leading to reduced performance and limp mode. [PATTERN DATA]

The timing chain issue is the most catastrophic and the primary driver of engine replacement. The others are typically repairable without full engine replacement, though they can contribute to overall running costs.

Our Verdict — N47 Reliability

The N47 is a capable engine with a known, preventable weak point.

In our view, the N47 is not a "bad" engine — it is a high-torque diesel with a specific, well-documented issue. It generates the most enquiries of any BMW engine we track, but that is partly due to its widespread use and age. Its timing chain problem is both earlier and more common than the B47's EGR cooler issues, but it is also much less costly than an N63 valve-seal failure.

The key recommendation is preventative maintenance. If your N47 is at 70,000 miles or more, have the timing chain tensioner checked at the next service. If you hear a cold-start rattle, act immediately. A £600–£1,200 preventative job is far cheaper than a £3,200–£5,500 replacement.

Verdict: Buy with confidence, but budget for a tensioner inspection at 70k. Avoid cars with patchy service history or a recorded cold-start rattle. On many F30 3 Series and F10 5 Series hosts, replacement is still financially viable — but check the vehicle value against our Repair vs Replace Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How common is N47 timing chain failure?

It is the most common single failure mode for this engine, with a typical occurrence between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. In our 2025 data, the N47 generated 1,450 UK enquiries, more than any other BMW engine [EM-VERIFIED].

Which BMW models use the N47 engine?

The N47 was fitted to the F30 3 Series (316d, 318d, 320d), E90 3 Series, F10 5 Series (520d), E60 5 Series, F25 X3 (xDrive20d), E84 X1, F22 2 Series and many others. See the full fitment guide for all chassis codes.

Is the N47 less reliable than the B47?

Yes — the B47 is a significantly more robust engine, with a lower failure rate and fewer documented weak points. The N47's timing chain issue is well-known, while the B47's common issues (EGR cooler) are less catastrophic. We recommend the B47 over the N47 if reliability is your top priority.

How much does it cost to replace an N47 engine in the UK?

Reconditioned supply is £3,200–£5,500; fitted with labour (10–14 hours) is £3,900–£6,500. Preventative chain replacement is £600–£1,200. [EM-QUOTE]

Can I drive with an N47 timing chain rattle?

No — a rattle on cold start is a clear warning sign. Driving with a loose chain risks the chain jumping teeth, causing catastrophic engine damage. Have it inspected immediately by a specialist.
