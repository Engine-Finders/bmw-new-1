N57 Failure Rate in Context

With 830 enquiries in 2025, the N57 is the eighth most-requested BMW engine — but what does that tell us about its actual failure rate?

Enquiry volume is not the same as failure rate. The N57 was fitted to a wide range of BMW models between 2008 and 2019, including the 3 Series (E90/F30), 5 Series (F10), X3 (F25), X5 (E70/F15), and many others. Its production volume is substantial, and it is a very common engine in the UK. The fact that it generates 830 enquiries — more than the B58 (540) and N63 (440) — reflects both its popularity and its age [EM-VERIFIED]. However, specialists report that the N57 is generally considered less reliable than its successor, the B57, but more reliable than the N47 diesel in terms of catastrophic timing chain failures (though the N57 has its own chain issues) [EM-INTERVIEW].

The N57's main issues — timing chain stretch, turbocharger wear, and oil pump failure — are well-documented and can be expensive to fix. However, many of these failures are preventable with regular maintenance, and the engine itself is robust when cared for. In our data, the N57's 830 enquiries place it behind the N47 (1,450) and B47 (930), but ahead of the B58 (540) and N63 (440) [EM-VERIFIED].

N57 Mileage at Failure Distribution

Based on specialist interviews and owner reports, the most common failures on the N57 occur between 80,000 and 150,000 miles.

This distribution is derived from [EM-INTERVIEW] data and [PATTERN DATA] from forums and service records. The N57's timing chain is similar to the N47's in that it is rear-mounted, making it labour-intensive to replace. Turbocharger issues often arise from oil supply problems, and oil pump failures can be catastrophic if not caught early.

Key mileage points:

  • 80,000–100,000 miles: Timing chain stretch begins to appear; cold-start rattle is the early warning sign. Turbo wastegate rattle may also start.
  • 100,000–120,000 miles: Turbocharger wear and oil pump failures become more common, especially in cars with neglected oil changes.
  • 120,000+ miles: DPF and EGR issues in urban-driven cars; crankshaft bearing wear reported in some higher-mileage units.

Preventative maintenance, including regular oil changes (every 8,000–10,000 miles) and chain inspection, is crucial. Many specialists recommend a timing chain inspection at 80,000 miles as a cost-effective measure [EM-INTERVIEW].

Most Common Failure Modes

The N57 has several known weak points, some of which can lead to catastrophic engine failure.

  • Timing chain stretch (most common): The rear-mounted chain and tensioner can stretch, causing rattling on cold start. If ignored, the chain can jump teeth, destroying the valve train. [PATTERN DATA] Repair cost: chain replacement £1,500–£2,500; full engine £4,500–£7,000 [EM-QUOTE].
  • Turbocharger wear: Oil supply issues can lead to turbo failure, especially on higher-mileage units. Typical cost: £1,200–£2,500 [EM-QUOTE].
  • Oil pump failure: The oil pump can fail, leading to oil starvation and engine seizure. Regular oil changes are critical. Repair cost: £1,500–£3,000 [ESTIMATED].
  • Crankshaft bearing issues: Reported in some higher-mileage units, though less common than the other issues. [PATTERN DATA]

Unlike the N47, which is often a write-off when the chain fails, the N57's chain replacement is expensive but often still viable on higher-value hosts. However, a full engine replacement is costly, so preventative chain inspection is strongly recommended.

Our Verdict — N57 Reliability

The N57 is a capable engine with several known, costly weak points.

In our view, the N57 is not a "bad" engine — it is a powerful six-cylinder diesel with some well-documented issues. It generates 830 enquiries, placing it in the middle of the pack. Its timing chain and turbo issues are serious and can be expensive, but they are often preventable with regular maintenance and proactive inspection.

The key recommendation is preventative maintenance. If your N57 is at 80,000 miles or more, have the timing chain and tensioner inspected. If you hear a cold-start rattle, act immediately. A £1,500–£2,500 chain replacement is far cheaper than a £4,500–£7,000 recon engine. Also, use premium oil and adhere to shorter oil change intervals to minimise turbo and oil pump wear.

Verdict: A good engine with a few manageable but costly issues. Buy with confidence, but budget for chain and turbo work around 80k–100k. Avoid cars with a history of neglected oil changes or a recorded cold-start rattle.

Frequently Asked Questions

How common is N57 timing chain failure?

It is one of the most common issues, typically occurring between 80,000 and 150,000 miles. In our 2025 data, the N57 generated 830 UK enquiries, the eighth highest among BMW engines [EM-VERIFIED].

Which BMW models use the N57 engine?

The N57 was fitted to the E90/F30 3 Series (330d, 335d), F10 5 Series (530d, 535d), F25 X3 (xDrive30d), E70/F15 X5 (xDrive30d, xDrive40d), and many others. See the full fitment guide for all chassis codes.

Is the N57 more reliable than the N47?

In terms of catastrophic failure, the N57's timing chain issue is less common than the N47's, but it is still a serious and expensive problem. The N57 also suffers from turbo and oil pump failures, which are less common on the N47. Overall, many specialists consider the N57 slightly more reliable than the N47, but not by a large margin.

How much does it cost to replace an N57 engine in the UK?

Reconditioned supply is £4,500–£7,000 [EM-QUOTE]; fitted with labour (12–16 hours) would be £5,500–£8,500. Preventative chain replacement is £1,500–£2,500 [EM-QUOTE].

Can I drive with an N57 timing chain rattle?

No — a rattle on cold start is a clear warning sign. Driving with a loose chain risks the chain jumping teeth, causing catastrophic engine damage. Have it inspected immediately by a specialist.
