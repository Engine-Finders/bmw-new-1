B58 Failure Rate in Context

How does the B58 compare to other BMW engines in our 2025 enquiry data?

The B58 is BMW's 3.0-litre straight-six turbocharged petrol engine, introduced in 2015 as the successor to the N55. In our 2025 UK data, it generated 540 replacement enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], placing it 4th on our list of BMW engines by request volume. This is significantly lower than the N47 (1,450 enquiries), N57 (830) and also lower than the B47 (930).

When you consider the B58's enquiry volume against its market share (it's fitted to many of BMW's most popular models like the 3-Series G20, 5-Series G30, X5 G05, and many others), it becomes clear that it is a genuinely reliable engine. Its closed-deck block, improved cooling system, and more robust materials have made it a favorite among enthusiasts and a benchmark for modern turbocharged petrol engines.

How Long Do B58 Engines Actually Last?

With proper maintenance, a B58 can exceed 200,000 miles.

The B58 is considered one of BMW's most durable modern petrol engines. Most failures occur between 80,000 and 120,000 miles, with many examples exceeding 200,000 miles without major issues. The key to a long B58 life is regular maintenance, particularly cooling system inspections and oil changes at the correct intervals (around 18,000 miles or 2 years, whichever is sooner).

Our data suggests that the B58's predecessor, the N55, had a higher failure rate at lower mileages, particularly with injector and HPFP issues. The B58's improved design, including a closed-deck block and more robust cooling system, makes it more durable.

Average lifespan: 180,000–220,000 miles with good maintenance [PATTERN DATA].

Our Verdict

8.9 Reliability Score

The B58 is a highly reliable, safe petrol engine. It has significantly fewer issues than its predecessors, the N54 and N55, and its replacement cost is well within the value of most BMWs it powers. The main concerns — coolant leaks and PCV valve issues — are well-understood and are not catastrophic.

If you're considering a BMW with a B58 engine, we would highly recommend it. Regular maintenance and attention to the cooling system will ensure a long, trouble-free life. This engine is a strong buy for most owners.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the B58 engine failure rate?

In our 2025 data, the B58 generated 540 replacement enquiries, placing it 4th on our list of BMW engines by request volume. This indicates a low failure rate, making the B58 one of BMW's most reliable modern petrol engines. The typical failure mileage is around 100,000 miles.

Is the B58 engine more reliable than the N55?

Yes, the B58 is considerably more reliable than the N55. The B58 has a closed-deck block, improved cooling, and more robust materials. It has fewer reported cases of major failure, particularly with injectors and HPFP, and is generally considered a more durable design.

What are the common B58 failure points?

The most common B58 failures are coolant leaks from the water pump and expansion tank, PCV valve failure, and HPFP issues. Most of these are manageable and not catastrophic, particularly compared to the N54's HPFP issues or N47's timing chain problems.

How many miles will a B58 engine last?

With proper maintenance, a B58 can last 180,000–220,000 miles. Many examples exceed 200,000 miles without major engine work. Regular oil changes and cooling system maintenance are key to maximising lifespan.

Should I avoid buying a B58?

No, the B58 is a safe and reliable engine. It is one of BMW's best modern petrol engines. If you're looking at a B58-powered BMW (e.g., 340i, 540i, X5 40i), we would highly recommend it, particularly if the vehicle has a full service history.
