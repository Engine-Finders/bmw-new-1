M47 Failure Rate in Context

How does the M47 compare to other BMW engines in our 2025 enquiry data?

The M47 is BMW's 2.0-litre four-cylinder diesel engine, produced from 1998 to 2007. It was replaced by the N47 in 2007. In our 2025 UK data, the M47 — primarily represented by the N47D20C variant — generated 1,450 replacement enquiries [EM-VERIFIED], placing it at the top of our list of BMW engines by request volume. This is significantly higher than the B47 (930 enquiries) and N57 (830 enquiries).

When you consider the M47's enquiry volume against its market share (it was fitted to millions of BMWs across the 3-Series, 5-Series, X3 and 1-Series), it becomes clear that the N47's timing chain weakness is a major contributing factor. The M47 itself is more reliable; the N47's rear-mounted timing chain design is the primary issue [PATTERN DATA].

How Long Do M47 Engines Actually Last?

With proper maintenance, an M47 can exceed 150,000 miles, but the N47's timing chain limits its typical lifespan.

The M47 is considered a durable diesel engine. Many examples exceed 200,000 miles without major issues. However, the N47 variant, which is the most common M47 in our dataset, has a known timing chain weakness that typically manifests between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. The key to a long N47 life is timing chain inspection and replacement before failure, which is far cheaper than a full engine replacement.

Our data suggests that the N47's predecessor, the M47, has a longer average lifespan because it lacks the rear-mounted timing chain design. The N47's improved fuel economy and emissions came at the cost of timing chain accessibility and durability [PATTERN DATA].

Average lifespan: 120,000–160,000 miles with good maintenance [PATTERN DATA].

Our Verdict

6.5 Reliability Score

The M47 is a reliable engine, but the N47 variant has a significant weakness. The M47 itself is robust and long-lived, but the N47's rear-mounted timing chain design makes it prone to catastrophic failure between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. The main concern is timing chain stretch, which can destroy the engine if ignored. BMW's recall on some N47 engines has addressed some issues, but many remain on the road today.

If you're considering a BMW with an M47 or N47 engine, we would recommend checking the timing chain history. A car with a full service history and a documented timing chain replacement is a safer buy. This engine is a cautious buy for most owners.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the M47 engine failure rate?

In our 2025 data, the M47/N47 generated 1,450 replacement enquiries, making it the most-enquired BMW engine. This reflects its popularity across a wide range of BMW models as well as its known timing chain weakness. The typical failure mileage is between 80,000 and 120,000 miles.

Is the M47 more reliable than the N47?

Yes, the M47 is generally more reliable than the N47. The M47 has a front-mounted timing chain, whereas the N47's rear-mounted chain is a known weak point. The N47 also has more reported cases of catastrophic failure due to timing chain stretch.

What are the common M47 failure points?

The most common M47/N47 failure is timing chain stretch and tensioner failure. Other issues include swirl flap failure, injector failure and turbocharger wear. Timing chain issues are the most serious and can cause catastrophic engine failure.

How many miles will an M47 engine last?

With proper maintenance, an M47 can last 120,000–160,000 miles. The N47 variant may fail earlier due to timing chain issues. Regular timing chain inspections are key to maximising lifespan.

Should I avoid buying an N47?

Not necessarily, but you should be cautious. An N47 with a documented timing chain replacement is a safer buy. If there's no record of timing chain work, budget for a replacement or inspection. The N47 is a common and well-understood engine, so many specialists can work on it.
