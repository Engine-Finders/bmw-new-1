Methodology – How We Calculated Average Mileage at Failure

Every number on this page is built from real UK enquiry data and specialist interviews. Here’s exactly what we counted and what we didn’t.

Source: Internal CRM quote‑request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, full year 2025. Total 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands; for this report we isolate BMW‑specific enquiries – over 9,000 individual cases.

What counts as a "failure": any enquiry that resulted in a quote request for an engine replacement, rebuild, or major component (timing chain, turbo, crankshaft, etc.). Routine servicing and minor repairs are excluded.

How we determine mileage: customers are asked to provide the current mileage of their vehicle at the time of enquiry. This figure is recorded in our CRM and used to calculate the mileage at which the failure occurred. We also cross‑check with specialist workshop records where available.

Limitations: Our dataset is enquiry‑based, not a random sample of the entire BMW population. It over‑represents vehicles that have already failed and under‑represents trouble‑free examples. Therefore, the average mileage we report is the average mileage at which owners contact us for a replacement, not the average lifespan of a BMW engine. We always pair this with qualitative pattern data from specialists to give a fuller picture.

Refresh cycle: This is our first published edition (2026 baseline). We will update annually each February.

Headline Findings – What the Data Tells Us

1. N47 Family – The Most‑Enquired, Fails Between 60k–120k

The N47D20C (1,450 requests) is the most common BMW engine in our dataset. Its typical failure mileage is 60,000–120,000 miles, with timing chain stretch being the dominant issue [PATTERN DATA]. Many cases occur around 80k–100k, and cold‑start rattle is the earliest warning sign.

2. B58 Petrol – Robust but Still Fails in the 60k–120k Band

The B58B30 (540 requests) is widely regarded as reliable, but our data shows failures (mainly coolant leaks, PCV, and water pump) typically occur between 60k and 120k miles [PATTERN DATA]. Many vehicles exceed 100k before any major issue.

3. N63 V8 – Earliest Failures, 60k–100k

The N63B44 (440 requests) has the tightest mileage window in our dataset – most enquiries come from vehicles with 60,000–100,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. Hot‑vee design issues (valve stem seals, turbo coolant lines) are the primary culprits.

4. B57 Diesel – More Durable, Often Beyond 120k

The B57D30 (460 requests) shows a wider spread, with many failures not occurring until well over 100k, and some reaching 180k before needing attention [PATTERN DATA]. It is considered significantly more durable than its predecessor, the N57.

5. Overall Average – 85,000 Miles

When we weight the mileage ranges by enquiry volume, the overall average mileage at which BMW owners request a replacement is approximately 85,000 miles [THIRD-PARTY – derived from ranges]. This figure is a benchmark, not a guarantee – individual results vary widely based on maintenance and driving conditions.

These findings are based on qualitative pattern data [PATTERN DATA] and enquiry volumes [EM-VERIFIED]. For a deeper dive into specific engine failure rates, see our Failure Rate by Engine page.

What This Means For Owners

If your BMW is approaching the typical failure mileage for its engine family, it's worth being proactive. For example, if you own an N47‑powered 3 Series with 80,000 miles, you should listen for cold‑start rattle and consider having the timing chain inspected [PATTERN DATA]. For B58 owners, watch for coolant loss and PCV symptoms – addressing these early can prevent a full engine replacement.

Remember: these are average figures. Many BMW engines exceed these mileages with proper maintenance. Regular servicing, using the correct oil spec, and avoiding repeated short trips can push the failure point significantly higher – our Technical Reference pages provide specific oil and service specs for each engine.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average mileage at which a BMW engine fails?

Across all BMW engines in our 2025 dataset, the overall average is approximately 85,000 miles. However, this varies significantly by engine – N47 typically fails between 60k–120k, while B57 often exceeds 120k. Individual results depend on maintenance, driving style, and service history.

Is the N47 really that unreliable?

The N47 is not inherently "unreliable" – it's a high‑volume engine that powers many BMWs, and our data shows it fails at a typical mileage of 60k–120k. The key is early detection of timing chain rattle; catching it early can prevent catastrophic engine failure. See our N47 Timing Chain Failure page for details.

Does the B58 engine last longer than the N55?

Our preliminary data suggests the B58 is more robust than the N55, with many B58s exceeding 100k miles without major issues. The N55 had a typical failure mileage of around 80k–120k, while the B58 often pushes beyond 120k. However, we need more data to draw a definitive conclusion – we will have clearer figures in future editions.

Should I sell my BMW before it reaches the average failure mileage?

That depends on the engine, the car's condition, and your personal situation. If you own an N47 with 90,000 miles and no symptoms, you might be fine for another 20k miles. But if you're risk‑averse, selling before 100k could be wise. Use our Repair vs Replace Framework to weigh the costs of replacing the engine against the car's value.

Why is the N63's failure mileage so much lower than the B58?

The N63's hot‑vee design generates significant heat, leading to premature wear on valve stem seals and coolant system components. It's a more complex and stressed engine, so failures occur at lower mileages. The B58, being a later design, addressed many of these issues, resulting in greater longevity.
