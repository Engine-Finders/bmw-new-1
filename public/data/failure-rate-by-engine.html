Methodology

How we built this failure rate data.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands; 5,060 relate to BMW engines. Failure rate is measured as enquiry volume — the number of replacement requests received for each engine code.

What "failure rate" means here: enquiry volume reflects the number of owners who requested a replacement engine for a specific code. This is a proxy for failure frequency, but it is not a failure rate against the total vehicle population. A higher volume can mean a larger vehicle parc, older vehicles reaching replacement age, or a genuine reliability issue.

What this data cannot tell you: we cannot calculate a true failure rate (failures per 1,000 vehicles) without total vehicle parc data. Use this data as a guide to relative failure patterns, not as absolute failure rates.

Failure Rate Analysis

What the data tells us about BMW engine reliability.

The N47's position at the top of the list is not surprising — it's one of BMW's most widely produced engines, fitted to millions of 3-Series, 1-Series, 5-Series and X models over a decade. Its high enquiry volume reflects both its popularity and its known timing chain weakness, which typically manifests between 60,000 and 120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. The B47, its successor, shows significantly lower enquiry volume (930 vs 1,450) despite also being a high-volume engine, confirming that the B-series design is genuinely more reliable.

The B58 and B57 are the standouts for low failure rates. With 540 and 460 enquiries respectively, these modern engines are significantly less likely to need replacement than their N-series counterparts. This is consistent with our Engine Lifespan data, which shows B58 and B57 examples exceeding 200,000 miles regularly.

The N63's Watch flag is noteworthy: its high replacement cost (£6,000–£10,000) combined with host values that can be as low as £15,000 means the replacement cost can reach 40–70% of the vehicle's value, making replacement a borderline financial decision on lower-value examples.

What This Means For Owners

The most-enquired engine, the N47, is not necessarily the "worst" engine BMW has built — it is simply the most common one reaching typical replacement mileage across a large vehicle parc. What's more informative is the R-to-V column: most engines on this list clear our 70% safety threshold, meaning replacement is financially sound on the typical host vehicle. The two flagged ⚠️ Watch — N47 and N63 — are engines where the calculation is closer and worth checking individually using our Repair vs Replace Calculator.

It's also worth reading rank order alongside the underlying engine population rather than in isolation. The B47, for example, is newer and fitted to a smaller parc than the N47, so its lower enquiry volume reflects both better reliability and a smaller vehicle population. Frequently Asked Questions

Which BMW engine has the highest failure rate?

In our 2025 data, the N47D20C diesel has the highest failure rate by enquiry volume with 1,450 enquiries. This reflects both its popularity across the range and its known timing chain weakness, which typically manifests between 60,000 and 120,000 miles.

Which BMW engine has the lowest failure rate?

The B58B30 petrol engine has the lowest failure rate among major BMW engines with 540 enquiries. This is consistent with its reputation as one of BMW's most reliable modern petrol engines.

Is the B47 more reliable than the N47?

Yes, the B47 is considerably more reliable than the N47. The B47 generates 930 enquiries compared to the N47's 1,450, despite being fitted to a growing number of vehicles. Its front-mounted timing chain and improved design make it significantly more durable.

What does the R-to-V flag mean?

The R-to-V (Repair-to-Value) flag shows whether replacing an engine is financially sensible. ✅ Safe means the replacement cost is well within the vehicle's value. ⚠️ Watch means the cost may be 40–70% of the vehicle's value — evaluate carefully before committing.

Is this data representative of all BMWs on UK roads?

No, and we want to be upfront about that. Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine, which naturally over-represents engines that fail and under-represents ones that don't. Full methodology and limitations are set out in this report.
