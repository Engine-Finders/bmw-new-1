The Verdict

For most buyers, the S63 is the stronger choice — it generates only slightly fewer enquiries (410 vs 440) despite being fitted to far fewer cars, which speaks to its desirability. The S63 is more reliable, more powerful, and built to a higher standard. The N63 is cheaper to buy, but its valve stem seal issues can be catastrophic. If you can afford the S63, buy it. If you're on a budget, the N63 can be a bargain — but only if you're prepared for maintenance.

N63 Deep Dive

The N63 was BMW's first hot‑vee V8, with the turbochargers mounted inside the V of the engine. Found in the 550i, 750i, X5 50i, and X6 50i, it offered impressive power and torque for its time. Its hot‑vee design, while space‑efficient, creates significant heat issues — valve stem seals can fail, leading to oil consumption and blue smoke, and the turbo coolant lines can leak. A valve stem seal replacement can cost £3,000–£6,000 [EM-QUOTE]. At £6,000–£15,000, the N63 is a cheap entry point, but you're buying an engine with known risks. Full specs, fitment and reliability rating live on the N63 Knowledge Centre.

S63 Deep Dive

The S63 is the M‑division version of the N63, featuring forged internals, higher boost, and a more robust cooling system. Found in the M5, M6, X5M, and X6M, it delivers up to 617 bhp in the latest iterations. Its main weaknesses are timing chain tensioner wear and oil leaks from the valve covers, both of which are less catastrophic than the N63's valve stem seal issues. The S63 is a more reliable engine overall, but its higher performance comes with higher running costs — fuel consumption is worse, and servicing is more expensive. Full specs and fitment on the S63 Knowledge Centre.

Five-Year Ownership Cost

The practical cost difference between these two engines over five years is driven by three factors: depreciation, running costs, and the probability of a major engine failure. The S63 will cost more to buy but will retain more of its value — a well‑maintained F10 M5 will still be worth £15,000+ after five years, while an F10 550i will have dropped to around £5,000. Running costs are higher on the S63 — it's thirstier and more expensive to service, but the probability of a major failure is lower. A valve stem seal failure on the N63 can be a £3,000–£6,000 repair, which can write off a low‑value car. The S63's higher value makes a £9,000+ engine replacement a more viable proposition.

Which Should You Buy?

🚙

Buying a Daily Driver

If you can afford the S63, buy it — it's more reliable and more powerful. The N63 is a gamble; only buy it if you have a maintenance budget and are prepared for potential issues.

🏁

Buying a Weekend Car or Track Toy

S63, decisively — the M‑division engineering, the power, and the sound make it the only choice for a performance car. The N63 is a cruiser, not a track car.

💰

Prioritising Lowest Running Cost

Neither — V8s are expensive to run. If you must choose, the N63 is cheaper to buy and insure, but the S63 is more reliable. The N63's fuel consumption is similar, but its repair costs can be higher.

🔁

Buying to Keep Long-Term

S63, decisively. The N63 is a ticking time bomb if the valve stem seals haven't been addressed. The S63 is built to last and will be a far more reliable long‑term companion.

One point worth being explicit about: the N63 is not a bad engine — it's actually quite good when maintained. But the hot‑vee design creates heat issues that can be expensive to fix. If you're considering an N63 car, factor in the cost of a valve stem seal replacement or look for one that has already had it done. The S63, on the other hand, is a significantly better engine in every measurable way. The decision genuinely comes down to budget and risk appetite.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the S63 better than the N63?

For most buyers, yes — the S63 is more reliable, more powerful, and built to a higher standard. The N63 is cheaper, but its valve stem seal issues make it a risk. The S63 is the better engine for performance buyers; the N63 is only worth it if you're prepared for maintenance.

Which BMW V8 is more reliable, N63 or S63?

The S63 is significantly more reliable. The N63 has well‑documented valve stem seal failures and hot‑vee heat issues. The S63's forged internals and improved cooling make it a much more robust engine.

Is the N63 cheaper to run than the S63?

No — the N63 is similar on fuel but has a higher risk of a catastrophic failure. The S63 is more expensive to service and insure, but the probability of a major failure is lower. Overall, the S63 is the lower‑risk proposition.

Which is better, N63 550i or S63 M5?

The S63 M5 is the better car — it's more powerful, more reliable, and more special. The N63 550i is a cruiser; the M5 is a super saloon. Buy the M5 if you can afford it.

Which is easier to replace when it fails, N63 or S63?

Both are expensive, but the S63's higher host value makes replacement more financially viable. The N63's lower value often makes a £6,000+ engine replacement uneconomical, while the S63's £15,000+ value makes it a more sensible proposition.
