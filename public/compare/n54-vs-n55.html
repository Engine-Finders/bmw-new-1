The Verdict

For most buyers, the N55 is the stronger choice. It generates more enquiries in our data (using the F30 335i proxy) because it's a more modern, reliable engine. The N54, while cheaper to purchase, can easily become a money pit — a HPFP or injector failure on a £3,000 car is a scrap threshold proposition. That said, the N54 is not a bad engine — it's actually very good when maintained, and its tuning potential is legendary. But for a worry‑free daily, the N55 is the clear winner.

N54 Deep Dive

The N54 was BMW's first mass‑market turbocharged petrol engine, replacing the naturally aspirated N52 in many applications. Found in the E90 335i, E60 535i, and E82 135i, it offered impressive power and tunability for its time. Its twin‑turbo design delivers a punchy, linear power delivery that still feels modern today. Its main weaknesses are the high‑pressure fuel pump (HPFP) — which can fail without warning — and injector failure, which can cause misfires and damage the engine. A full set of injectors can cost over £1,000 [EM-QUOTE]. At £3,000–£8,000, the N54 is a cheap entry point, but you're buying an engine with known risks. Full specs, fitment and reliability rating live on the N54 Knowledge Centre.

N55 Deep Dive

The N55 is the successor to the N54, featuring a single twin‑scroll turbocharger, Valvetronic variable valve lift, and a more robust engine management system. Found in the F30 335i, F10 535i, and F22 M235i, it offers better low‑end torque, improved fuel economy, and significantly better reliability than the N54. The only notable issues are VANOS solenoid failures and oil leaks from the valve cover, both of which are relatively inexpensive to fix compared to the N54's fuel system problems. The N55 is a much better daily driver, with a more refined character and fewer worries. Full specs and fitment on the N55 Knowledge Centre.

Five-Year Ownership Cost

The practical cost difference between these two engines over five years is driven by three factors: depreciation, running costs, and the probability of a major engine failure. The N55 will cost more to buy but will retain more of its value — a well‑maintained F30 335i will still be worth £5,000+ after five years, while an E90 335i will have dropped to around £2,000. Running costs are better on the N55 — the improved efficiency delivers 35+ mpg on a motorway run, while the N54 struggles to hit 30 mpg. The probability of a major failure is substantially higher on the N54, with HPFP and injector failures a real risk. A failure can write off the car on lower‑value examples. The N55, while not immune to issues, is a much safer bet.

Which Should You Buy?

🚙

Buying a Daily Driver

Go N55 — it's more efficient, more refined, and more modern. The F30 chassis is a better daily, and the N55's extra torque and reliability make it a more effortless drive.

🏁

Buying a Weekend Car or Project

N54, but only if you're prepared for maintenance. A cheap E90 335i with a few known fixes can be a monster on a budget. Just budget for HPFP and injectors upfront.

💰

Prioritising Lowest Running Cost

N55 — it's more economical to run, and a major failure is far less likely to write off the car, thanks to higher retained values. The N54's fuel consumption and repair costs will hurt your wallet.

🔁

Buying to Keep Long-Term

N55, decisively. The N54 is a ticking time bomb if the HPFP or injectors haven't been addressed. The N55 is built to last and will be a far more reliable long‑term companion.

One point worth being explicit about: the N54 is not a bad engine — it's actually quite good when maintained. But the fuel system issues are a genuine design flaw that can be expensive to fix. If you're considering an N54 car, factor in the cost of a HPFP and injector replacement or look for one that has already had it done. The N55, on the other hand, is a significantly better engine in every measurable way. The decision genuinely comes down to budget and risk appetite.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the N55 better than the N54?

For most buyers, yes — the N55 is more reliable, more efficient, and more refined. The N54 is the cheaper, more tuneable option, but its fuel system issues make it a risk. The N55 is the better daily driver; the N54 is only worth it if you're prepared for maintenance.

Which BMW turbo 6‑cylinder is more reliable, N54 or N55?

The N55 is significantly more reliable. The N54 has well‑documented HPFP and injector failures that can be expensive to fix. The N55's fuel system is much more robust, and the engine has a stronger reputation overall.

Is the N54 cheaper to run than the N55?

No — the N54 is thirstier (25–30 mpg vs 30–35 mpg) and has a higher risk of a catastrophic failure. The N55 is more fuel‑efficient and has lower running costs overall, though it can have more expensive sensor failures (e.g., VANOS solenoids).

Which is better, N54 335i or N55 335i?

The N55 335i is the better car — it's more reliable, more efficient, and more refined. The N54 335i is a classic, but it's now an old engine. Buy the N55 for daily use, the N54 only if you get a bargain and are prepared for maintenance.

Which is easier to replace when it fails, N54 or N55?

Both are relatively straightforward, but the N54's lower host value makes replacement less financially viable. The N55's higher retained value makes a £3,000+ engine replacement a more sensible proposition.
