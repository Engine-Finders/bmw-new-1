The Verdict

The B58 is the better engine for most buyers. It's more reliable, more refined, and cheaper to fix on a probability-weighted basis. The N55 is still a great engine with a huge aftermarket, but its valve cover oil leaks and water pump failures are well-documented and can be costly. The B58's issues—coolant leaks from plastic fittings and PCV valve wear—are less severe and often cheaper to repair. If you're buying a used BMW with a turbo straight-six, the B58 is the smart, safe choice. That said, if you're a tuner or on a tight budget, a well-maintained N55 can still be a fantastic engine.

N55 Deep Dive

The N55 is BMW's single-turbo 3.0-litre straight-six, produced from 2009 to 2015. It replaced the twin-turbo N54 and was fitted to a huge range of models, including the 1 Series, 3 Series (F30), 5 Series (F10), X3, X5, and many others. It's the most-enquired six-cylinder engine in our 2025 data (830 requests [EM-VERIFIED]), reflecting its popularity and the fact that many owners are looking for replacements or repairs. The main issues are valve cover oil leaks (which can cause burning oil smells and smoke) and water pump failure (which can lead to overheating). These are well-understood and generally not catastrophic, but they can be expensive to fix—especially the valve cover, which requires significant labour. Despite these, the N55 is a capable and tuneable engine, and with regular maintenance, many have covered over 150,000 miles. Full specifications and fitment are on our N55 Knowledge Centre.

B58 Deep Dive

The B58 is the successor to the N55, introduced in 2015 and still in production today. It's a more modern, refined, and reliable 3.0-litre turbo straight-six, with a redesigned closed-deck block, improved cooling, and better fuel injection. Our data shows 540 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], and it's the preferred choice for buyers seeking long-term reliability. The main documented issues are coolant leaks from plastic fittings (expansion tank, water pump, and thermostat housing) and PCV valve failure—both of which are relatively inexpensive to fix (£400–£1,000) compared to the N55's issues. The B58 is also quieter, smoother, and more fuel-efficient, making it the superior engine in almost every way. Full specs and fitment are on the B58 Knowledge Centre.

Five-Year Ownership Costs

Over a typical five-year ownership period, the B58 is cheaper to own despite a higher purchase price. The N55's lower initial cost is offset by a higher probability of a major repair (valve cover and water pump). The B58's issues are less severe and cheaper to fix. Additionally, the B58's improved fuel economy (by about 5-10%) and lower servicing costs (fewer oil changes due to better oil degradation) add up. In our probability-weighted analysis, the B58 comes out ahead by roughly £500–£1,000 over five years, even before accounting for peace of mind. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the B58 more reliable than the N55?

Yes, significantly. The B58's closed-deck block, improved cooling, and better materials make it more durable. The N55's valve cover oil leaks and water pump failures are well-documented and can be expensive to fix.

Which is cheaper to replace, B58 or N55?

The B58 is cheaper to replace, at £3,800–£6,000 supply only versus £4,500–£7,000 for the N55, reflecting its more modern design and lower demand (540 vs 830 enquiries).

Is the N55 a bad buy?

No. If you find a low-mileage N55 with a documented service history, it can be a great bargain. Just factor in the risk and budget for a valve cover and water pump inspection.

Which engine has better fuel economy?

The B58 is more fuel-efficient, typically offering 5-10% better real-world MPG due to improved injection technology and lower internal friction.

Which is more tuneable?

The N55 has a huge aftermarket and is easier to tune, but the B58 can also achieve significant power gains with the right modifications.

Which engine sounds better?

Subjective, but many enthusiasts prefer the N55's rawer sound, while the B58 is smoother and more refined.
