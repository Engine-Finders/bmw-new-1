The Verdict

The 2 Series takes the win for most drivers. It offers a more engaging driving experience, better styling, and on the coupe (F22) and Active Tourer (F45), the availability of the newer, more reliable B47 diesel engine. The 1 Series is still a great car — practical, spacious, and affordable — but the 2 Series simply offers more of what BMW buyers want: driving pleasure and a premium feel. That said, if you need five doors and a bigger boot, the 1 Series is the obvious choice. Both are excellent used buys, but the 2 Series edges ahead on driving dynamics and value retention.

1 Series Deep Dive (F20/F21)

The 1 Series is BMW's entry-level hatchback, offered as a five-door (F20) or three-door (F21). It's practical, with a decent boot and five-door option, and it's popular — our data shows 344 enquiries for the F20 in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]. Early models (pre‑2015) use the N47D20C diesel, which has a notorious timing chain stretch risk — see our N47 Timing Chain Failure page for details. Later models (2015‑on) switch to the B47D20A, which is significantly more reliable and cheaper to replace. The 1 Series is also available with petrol engines (N20, B48), but the diesels dominate enquiry volume. It's a great all‑rounder, but the driving experience is less sharp than the 2 Series due to the hatchback's higher centre of gravity and softer suspension. Full specs and reliability on the N47 Knowledge Centre and B47 Knowledge Centre.

2 Series Deep Dive (F22/F45)

The 2 Series is BMW's small coupe (F22) and convertible (F23), plus the more practical Active Tourer (F45) MPV. The coupe is the driver's choice — lighter, lower, and sharper than the 1 Series, with a chassis that rewards spirited driving. Enquiry volumes for the F22 are 147 in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting a more niche audience. Like the 1 Series, early 2 Series use the N47D20C with its timing chain risk, but later models (2015‑on) get the B47D20A, which is more reliable. The F45 Active Tourer is a different proposition — a taller, more practical MPV with front‑wheel drive (unlike the rear‑drive 1 Series and 2 Series coupe) — and it exclusively uses the B47 or B48 engines, which are generally robust. Our B47 vs N47 comparison covers the engine differences in more detail. If you want a sporty, stylish compact that retains value well and offers a great driving experience, the 2 Series is hard to beat.

Five-Year Ownership Costs

The biggest cost difference between these two isn't fuel or servicing — it's depreciation and the probability of a major engine failure. The 1 Series is cheaper to buy used, and parts are widely available, but the N47 timing chain risk on pre‑2015 models can be a £650‑£1,800 bill [PATTERN DATA]. The 2 Series holds its value better (coupe styling always does), but it costs more upfront. On average, the B47‑equipped models of both are reliable, but the 2 Series' lower weight and sportier setup means slightly higher tyre and brake wear. Overall, the 2 Series costs a bit more to own over five years — but the driving experience and value retention make it worthwhile for many.

The 2 Series is the more exciting car; the 1 Series is the more sensible one. Both are excellent used buys, but whichever you choose, avoid pre‑2015 N47 engines unless you have a full timing chain history.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the BMW 1 Series or 2 Series more reliable?

It depends on the engine. Both are reliable with the B47 diesel (post‑2015) or B48 petrol. The N47 in pre‑2015 models of both has a timing chain risk, so avoid it unless the chain has been done.

Which is cheaper to run: 1 Series or 2 Series?

The 1 Series is cheaper to buy and generally cheaper to service, as parts are more common. The 2 Series has slightly higher tyre and brake wear due to its sportier setup, but fuel economy is similar.

Is the 2 Series just a 1 Series with a different body?

Mostly, yes — they share the same platform and engines. The 2 Series coupe is lower, lighter, and has a wider track, giving it sharper handling and a more planted feel.

Which BMW holds its value better?

The 2 Series coupe and convertible generally hold value better than the 1 Series hatchback, due to coupe styling and desirability.
