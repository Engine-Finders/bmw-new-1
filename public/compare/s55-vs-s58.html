The Verdict

For most buyers, the S58 is the stronger choice. It generates more enquiries in our data not because it fails more often, but because it's fitted to more modern and higher-volume M cars — and when it does need replacing, the cost is lower and the supply is more readily available. The S58 is also considered the more reliable engine, with no major known weak point equivalent to the S55's crank hub risk. That said, the S55 remains a brilliant engine and a genuinely compelling choice if you're looking at the F80/F82 chassis on a tighter budget — just factor in the crank hub preventative work early on. Both engines are comfortably ✅ Safe against typical host values in their respective chassis, though the 70% BMW threshold means the economics are tighter on older, lower-value F80 examples.

S55 Deep Dive

The S55 is the twin-turbo straight-six that powered the F80 M3 and F82 M4 from 2014 to 2020. It's a high-performance engine with a well-documented crank hub failure risk, typically at higher mileages or on modified cars. Full specs, fitment and reliability rating live on the S55 Knowledge Centre. The S55 generated 410 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], and its reconditioned supply cost ranges from £6,500 to £9,500 [EM-QUOTE]. Labour for R&R is 12–16 hours at independent rates. The engine's main advantage is the value proposition — F80/F82 cars are now a used bargain, making the S55 an attractive entry point into M ownership.

The S55 failure rate data shows that while the crank hub issue is real, it's not as common as forum lore suggests — and preventative work is relatively straightforward for a specialist. The bottom line: the S55 is a great engine, but you need to budget for the ownership cost of that preventative work if you're buying one.

S58 Deep Dive

The S58 is the newer twin-turbo straight-six, used in the G80 M3, G82 M4, G87 M2, and X3 M/X4 M from 2020 onward. It's a more powerful and more reliable engine than the S55 — with no major known failure mode at scale. Full specs and fitment are on the S58 Knowledge Centre. The S58 generated 540 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], and its reconditioned supply cost is £5,000–£8,000 [EM-QUOTE]. Labour for R&R is 10–14 hours — shorter than the S55's 12–16 hours, reflecting better access and design improvements.

The S58 is also the engine with better failure rate data — it's simply more robust. The bottom line: if you can afford the newer car, the S58 is the smarter buy, both from a reliability and a replacement-cost perspective.

Five-Year Ownership Cost

Purchase price aside, the practical cost difference between these two engines over a typical five-year ownership window comes down to three things: fuel economy, insurance (which is higher on the S58), and the probability-weighted cost of a major failure. The S58's higher power is balanced by its more modern engine management, which delivers similar real-world economy to the S55. Insurance is notably higher on the S58, particularly for G80/G82 cars, which are newer and more expensive. The probability-weighted cost of failure is where the S58 wins decisively — it has no major known weak point, while the S55's crank hub risk, even with preventative work, adds a non-trivial cost over the ownership period. Overall, the S58 is the cheaper engine to own, despite the higher insurance costs.

Buying to Keep Long-Term

If you plan to keep the car past 100,000 miles, the S58 is the safer long-term bet. The S55 can go the distance with preventative work, but the S58 is simply more robust from the factory.

One point worth being explicit about: neither engine is "the unreliable one" in any simple sense. Both are capable of covering well over 100,000 miles when maintained correctly. The decision genuinely comes down to budget and chassis preference — an F80 M3 is a brilliant car, and the S55 is a brilliant engine. The S58 is better, but it costs more to get into. The repair vs replace framework applies equally to both: if the lowest viable replacement cost exceeds approximately 70% of the vehicle's current market value, evaluate scrap value before committing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the S58 better than the S55?

Yes, for most buyers — the S58 is more powerful, more reliable, and cheaper to replace than the S55. The S55 is still a great engine in the F80/F82 chassis, but the S58 is the better engine.

Is the S58 more reliable than the S55?

Yes — the S58 has no major known failure mode at scale, while the S55 has a well-documented crank hub failure risk. The S58 is considered a more robust engine overall.

Which engine is cheaper to replace, S55 or S58?

The S58 is cheaper — £5,000–£8,000 reconditioned supply versus £6,500–£9,500 for the S55. Labour hours are also lower on the S58 (10–14 hrs vs 12–16 hrs).

Is the S55 worth buying in 2026?

Yes — if you're looking at the F80 M3 or F82 M4, the S55 is still a brilliant engine. Just budget for the crank hub preventative work early on, and you'll have a great M car experience for a relatively low entry price.

Which engine has better tuning potential?

Both are highly tunable, but the S58 has more headroom due to its stronger bottom end and better cooling. However, tuning any M car significantly increases the risk of major failure — proceed with caution.

Is the S58 cheaper to maintain than the S55?

Yes — the S58 has fewer known issues, cheaper parts, and lower labour hours for major work. It's the more cost-effective engine to maintain over a five-year ownership period.
