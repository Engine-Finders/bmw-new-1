The Verdict

The 5 Series takes the win for most buyers. It's the sweet spot in BMW's range: spacious, comfortable, and sporting enough to be a driver's car, yet practical and affordable to run. The 7 Series is a step up in luxury and presence, but the extra cost, size, and complexity are hard to justify unless you regularly carry VIP passengers or simply want the ultimate BMW. For the majority of buyers, the 5 Series offers 90% of the 7 Series experience at 60% of the price.

5 Series Deep Dive (F10 / G30)

The 5 Series is BMW's executive saloon, available as a saloon (F10, G30) and estate (F11). It's the best-selling large BMW in the UK, and our data reflects that: 300 enquiries for the F10 and 177 for the G30 in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED]. Engine options range from economical four-cylinder diesels (N47, B47) to smooth six-cylinders (N57, B57) and even V8s in the M550i. Early diesels (pre‑2015) have the N47 timing chain risk — see our N47 Timing Chain Failure page — while later B47 and B57 engines are much more reliable. The 5 Series is a brilliant all‑rounder: comfortable, spacious, and engaging to drive. It's the car that defined the executive segment. Full specs and reliability on the N47, B47, N57, and B57 Knowledge Centres.

7 Series Deep Dive (F01 / G11)

The 7 Series is BMW's flagship limousine, designed for luxury and chauffeur-driven comfort. It's larger, heavier, and more technologically advanced than the 5 Series, with features like air suspension, rear-seat entertainment, and optional long wheelbase. Enquiry volumes are lower: 150 for the G11 and 56 for the F01 in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting its niche status. Engines are predominantly six-cylinder diesels (N57, B57) and V8 petrols (N63, B58) — the N63 is notorious for valve stem seal issues, detailed on our N63 Oil Consumption page. The 7 Series offers a ride quality that's unmatched in the BMW range, but it comes at a cost: higher purchase price, higher running costs, and steeper depreciation. If you want the best that BMW can offer, the 7 Series is it.

Five-Year Ownership Costs

The cost gap between these two is significant. The 5 Series is cheaper to buy, cheaper to service, and cheaper to insure. The 7 Series' higher purchase price and greater depreciation are the main drivers of higher total cost of ownership. However, the 7 Series offers a more luxurious experience and holds a certain prestige that the 5 Series can't match. Over five years, the 5 Series typically costs thousands less, making it the smarter financial choice for most.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the BMW 5 Series or 7 Series more reliable?

It depends on the engine. Both are reliable with the B57 diesel (post‑2015) or B58 petrol. The N47 and N57 in pre‑2015 models have timing chain risks, while the N63 V8 has valve seal issues. Avoid those unless well-maintained.

Which is cheaper to run: 5 Series or 7 Series?

The 5 Series is cheaper to buy, service, and insure. The 7 Series has higher fuel consumption, more expensive parts, and steeper depreciation. Overall, the 5 Series is significantly cheaper to own over five years.

Is the 7 Series worth the extra money?

If you value supreme comfort, rear-seat space, and status, yes. For most buyers, the 5 Series offers excellent value and 90% of the 7 Series' luxury at a much lower cost.

Which holds its value better?

The 5 Series typically holds its value better than the 7 Series, which depreciates faster due to higher initial prices and demand in the used market.
