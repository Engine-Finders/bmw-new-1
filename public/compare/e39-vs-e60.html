The Verdict

The E60 is the better choice for most buyers. It's more modern, has better engine options (especially the N52 petrol and N57 diesel), and is significantly cheaper to maintain due to better parts availability. The E39 is a classic—it has the best steering feel of any 5 Series and a timeless design—but its age means rust, failing electronics, and parts that are increasingly hard to find. If you're looking for a daily driver, the E60 is the smarter, cheaper, and more reliable option. The E39 is a weekend car for enthusiasts who value driving feel over practicality.

E39 Deep Dive (1995–2003)

The E39 is often considered the best 5 Series ever made. Its hydraulic steering is unmatched, its build quality is legendary, and its design has aged beautifully. Engines include the M54 straight‑six petrol (2.2–3.0L) and the M57 diesel (2.5–3.0L). The M54 is known for cooling system failures—water pumps, expansion tanks, and thermostats are common weak points [PATTERN DATA]. The M57 diesel is robust but suffers from injector seal leaks and swirl flap issues. Our data shows only 33 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting its age and dwindling numbers. Parts are getting harder to find, and rust is a serious issue on UK cars. The E39 is a fantastic car for enthusiasts, but it's not a cheap daily driver anymore. Full specs on our M54 and M57 Knowledge Centres.

E60 Deep Dive (2003–2010)

The E60 introduced iDrive, a polarising design, and a new generation of engines. The N52 petrol (3.0L) is smooth and reliable, though it can suffer from valve stem seal wear and water pump failure [PATTERN DATA]. The N47 diesel (2.0L) has the timing chain issue—see N47 Timing Chain Failure—while the N57 (3.0L) is more robust but can have injector and turbo issues. Enquiry volumes are higher at 226 in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting its wider availability and lower running costs. The E60 is more practical, easier to maintain, and has better parts support. It's not as tactile as the E39, but it's a better everyday car. Full specs on our N52 and N57 Knowledge Centres.

Five-Year Ownership Costs

The E60 is significantly cheaper to own over five years. The E39's age means more frequent repairs, harder-to-find parts (which cost more), and rust issues. The E60's engines are more modern, parts are widely available, and repairs are better understood. While the E39's purchase price is lower, its total cost of ownership over five years can be higher. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the E39 or E60 more reliable?

The E60 is more reliable overall, thanks to newer engine designs and better parts availability. The E39 is getting old—its cooling systems and electronics are common failure points, and rust is a major issue on UK cars.

Which is cheaper to run, E39 or E60?

The E60 is cheaper to run. Parts are more available, and the engines (especially the N57 diesel and N52 petrol) are more fuel-efficient and reliable.

Is the E39 worth buying in 2025?

Yes, if you're an enthusiast looking for a classic with excellent driving feel. But it's no longer a cheap daily—budget for maintenance and rust repair.

Which has better steering feel?

The E39's hydraulic steering is widely regarded as the best of any 5 Series. The E60's electric steering is lighter and less communicative.

Do E60s have timing chain issues?

Yes, the N47 diesel (2.0L) in early E60s has the timing chain issue. The N52 petrol and N57 diesel are generally more robust.

Which holds its value better?

Clean E39s are rising in value as classics, but the E60 depreciates more like a modern car. For long-term value, the E39 is better—but you'll pay for it in maintenance.
