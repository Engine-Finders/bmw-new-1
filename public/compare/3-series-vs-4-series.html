The Verdict

The 3 Series takes the win for most buyers. It's the practical, affordable choice with four doors, a bigger boot, and a huge range of engines. The 4 Series is the style choice — lower, wider, and sleeker, with a more engaging driving experience. But for most people, the 3 Series' extra practicality and lower purchase price make it the smarter buy. That said, if you want a coupe or convertible and you're willing to pay the premium, the 4 Series is a brilliant car.

3 Series Deep Dive (F30/F31)

The 3 Series is BMW's best-selling model for good reason. It's practical, comfortable, and offers a wide range of engines from economical diesels to punchy six‑cylinders. Our data shows 380 enquiries for the F30 in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], making it one of the most‑enquired models in the BMW range. Early models (pre‑2015) use the N47D20C diesel with its notorious timing chain stretch risk — see our N47 Timing Chain Failure page. Later models (2015‑on) get the B47D20A or B48B20 petrol, both of which are significantly more reliable. The 3 Series is also available as a Touring (F31) estate, adding even more practicality. It's a great all‑rounder — comfortable, spacious, and affordable. Full specs and reliability on the N47 Knowledge Centre and B47 Knowledge Centre.

4 Series Deep Dive (F32/F33/F36)

The 4 Series is BMW's sleek coupe and convertible, plus the more practical Gran Coupe (F36) — a five‑door liftback that combines coupe styling with hatchback practicality. Enquiry volumes for the F32 are 120 in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting a more niche audience. Like the 3 Series, early models use the N47D20C with its timing chain risk, but later models (2015‑on) get the B47D20A or B48B20, which are more reliable. The 4 Series is lower, wider, and sportier than the 3 Series, with sharper handling and a more premium feel. It holds its value better than the 3 Series, but it's more expensive to buy and insure. Our B47 vs N47 comparison covers the engine differences in more detail. If you want a stylish, sporty BMW that stands out, the 4 Series is hard to beat.

Five-Year Ownership Costs

The biggest cost difference between these two isn't fuel or servicing — it's depreciation and the probability of a major engine failure. The 3 Series is cheaper to buy used, and parts are widely available, but the N47 timing chain risk on pre‑2015 models can be a £650‑£1,800 bill [PATTERN DATA]. The 4 Series holds its value better (coupe/convertible styling always does), but it costs more upfront and typically has higher insurance premiums. On average, the 3 Series costs less to own over five years, but the 4 Series' value retention can offset some of that difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the BMW 3 Series or 4 Series more reliable?

It depends on the engine. Both are reliable with the B47 diesel (post‑2015) or B48 petrol. The N47 in pre‑2015 models of both has a timing chain risk, so avoid it unless the chain has been done.

Which is cheaper to run: 3 Series or 4 Series?

The 3 Series is cheaper to buy and generally cheaper to service, as parts are more common. The 4 Series has slightly higher tyre and brake wear due to its sportier setup, but fuel economy is similar.

Is the 4 Series just a 3 Series with a different body?

Mostly, yes — they share the same platform and engines. The 4 Series is lower, wider, and has a sportier suspension setup, giving it sharper handling and a more planted feel.

Which BMW holds its value better?

The 4 Series coupe and convertible generally hold value better than the 3 Series saloon, due to coupe styling and desirability.
