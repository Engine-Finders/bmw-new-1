The Verdict

For most buyers, the X3 is the stronger choice. It's more affordable to buy, cheaper to run, and its smaller size makes it far more city-friendly. The X5 is a magnificent vehicle, but its advantages only really come into play if you regularly carry five adults, tow heavy loads, or simply want the ultimate in BMW SUV luxury. Our enquiry data shows the X3 is significantly more popular, but that's partly due to its broader appeal. The 70% BMW scrap threshold means that on older, lower-value examples, the X5's higher replacement cost can push it closer to the watch threshold, whereas the X3 remains comfortably safe on most examples [PATTERN DATA].

X3 Deep Dive

The BMW X3 is the brand's best‑selling SUV, available in three generations (E83, F25, G01). The F25 (2011–2017) is the most‑enquired generation in our data, with 316 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], while the G01 (2018–present) adds another 182 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED]. Engines range from the N47 and B47 four‑cylinder diesels to the N57 and B57 six‑cylinder diesels, and petrol options including the N20 and B48. The X3 is renowned for its balance of comfort, handling, and practicality — rear seats fold 40:20:40, and the boot offers 550 litres. The N47 Knowledge Centre covers the diesel powertrains, while the N47 failure rate data shows the X3 is a common host. For most families, the X3 is the sensible, capable choice.

X5 Deep Dive

The X5 is BMW's flagship SUV, available in four generations (E53, E70, F15, G05). The F15 (2013–2018) generated 288 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], while the older E70 (2007–2013) added 218 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED]. The X5 is larger, heavier, and more luxurious than the X3, with typical engines being the N57 six‑cylinder diesel, the B57, and the V8 petrol N63 and S63. Replacement costs are significantly higher — a reconditioned N57 supply is £4,500–£7,000 [EM-QUOTE], and the B57 runs £5,000–£8,000 [EM-QUOTE]. Labour hours are also longer at 12–16 hours. The X5's real strength is its interior space, towing capacity (up to 3.5 tonnes) and long‑distance comfort. For those who need it, there's no substitute — but it comes at a cost. Our N57 failure rate data shows it's a robust engine, but the higher repair costs mean the repair vs replace framework becomes more critical on older examples.

Five‑Year Ownership Cost

Over five years, the X3 is significantly cheaper to own than the X5. The X3's smaller engines deliver better fuel economy — typically 45–55 mpg for the diesels versus 35–45 mpg for the X5's six‑cylinder diesels. Insurance groups are also lower for the X3, and servicing costs are reduced due to smaller engines and less complex systems. The biggest difference is in the probability-weighted cost of major failure: the X3's N47/B47 replacement is £3,200–£5,500, while the X5's N57/B57 is £4,500–£8,000. Additionally, the X5's more complex suspension and drivetrain (often with air suspension and xDrive) add potential repair costs that aren't present on the X3. Overall, the X3 will save a typical owner several thousand pounds over five years, making it the more cost‑effective choice for the vast majority of buyers.

One point worth being explicit about: neither model is inherently more reliable — they share similar engine families and build quality. The decision genuinely comes down to your lifestyle and budget. If you don't need the X5's extra space and towing ability, the X3 will serve you just as well for thousands less. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the X3 better than the X5?

For most buyers, yes — the X3 is more affordable, more fuel-efficient, and cheaper to maintain, while still offering excellent space and comfort. The X5 is only better if you need its extra size and towing capacity.

Which is more reliable, X3 or X5?

They are equally reliable — they share similar engine families (N47/B47 vs N57/B57) and build quality. Reliability depends more on the specific engine and maintenance history than the model.

Is the X5 cheaper to insure than the X3?

No — the X5 typically falls into higher insurance groups due to its larger engine and higher value, so it's generally more expensive to insure.

Which has more boot space, X3 or X5?

The X5 has significantly more boot space — around 650 litres vs the X3's 550 litres, and the X5 offers more rear passenger space and headroom.

Which is cheaper to run, X3 or X5?

The X3 is cheaper to run in every respect — fuel, insurance, servicing, and replacement parts. The X5's larger engines and heavier weight increase consumption and wear.

Is the X5 worth the extra money?

Only if you regularly need the extra space, towing capability, or the more premium interior. For everyday use, the X3 offers better value for money.
