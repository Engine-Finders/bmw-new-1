Frequently Asked Questions — BMW Economics

What is the 70% rule for BMW engine replacement?

The 70% rule is our repair-to-value threshold for BMW: if a repair or replacement engine costs more than 70% of your BMW's current market value, the honest verdict is usually to scrap or sell for parts rather than repair. It's a guide, not an absolute cut-off — we always show the full working so you can judge your own case.

Why is BMW's threshold 70% and not 60% like Land Rover?

70% reflects the typical parts and labour baseline for BMW engines in our enquiry data. Land Rover and Jaguar sit at 60% in our data, reflecting a lower typical cost baseline for those brands. The BMW figure is reviewed against real enquiry outcomes rather than fixed arbitrarily.

Is a reconditioned BMW engine always cheaper than a used one?

No. A used engine is usually the cheapest supply-and-fit option but carries more uncertainty over remaining life and history. A reconditioned engine costs more but comes with a warranty and a known rebuild standard. Which is worth it depends on how long you plan to keep the car.

Does the 70% rule apply the same way to M cars and standard BMWs?

The rule itself is constant, but the answer it produces varies enormously. An M car's higher repair costs are offset by higher retained values — the ratio often clears the threshold comfortably. A higher-mileage 1 Series near the bottom of its value curve fails the same threshold at a much lower repair cost.
