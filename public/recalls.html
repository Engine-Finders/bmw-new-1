How to Check Your BMW for Outstanding Recalls

Every BMW has a unique 17‑digit VIN. You can use it to check for open recalls in seconds.

  1. Find your VIN – on the driver’s side dashboard (visible through the windscreen) or on the driver’s door pillar.
  2. Use our VIN Decoder to see if your car has any outstanding recall campaigns.
  3. If a recall is found, contact your nearest BMW specialist or main dealer – repairs are carried out free of charge.

What a Recall Does (and Does Not) Cover

A recall is a manufacturer-led campaign to fix a safety or emissions defect. It is always free to the owner, regardless of mileage or age, provided the car is within the recall VIN range.

  • Covered: Safety-critical faults (airbags, brakes, steering), emissions compliance issues, and certain engine components (EGR coolers, timing chain tensioners where a recall exists).
  • Not covered: Wear and tear, neglect, or known mechanical failures that are not subject to a formal recall – e.g., N47 timing chain stretch is a known failure, but not a recall. See our Engine Failure Index for those.

If you’re unsure whether your symptom is a recall or a common fault, browse our symptom guides or use the Diagnostic Wizard.

Tip: Always check for outstanding recalls before paying for a repair – you could be eligible for a free fix.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if my BMW has an outstanding recall?

Use our VIN Decoder – enter your 17‑digit VIN and we’ll show you any open campaigns. You can also check the official BMW UK recall portal or the DVSA website.

What’s the difference between a recall and a known failure?

A recall is a manufacturer-mandated fix for a safety or emissions defect, carried out free of charge. A known failure is a common mechanical issue that is not covered by a recall – e.g., N47 timing chain stretch – and repair costs are the owner’s responsibility.

Do I have to pay for a recall repair?

No. If your car is within the affected VIN range, the repair is free at any BMW dealer or authorised specialist. This includes parts and labour.

How long does a recall repair take?

It varies, but most are completed in a few hours. Some complex campaigns (e.g., EGR cooler replacement) may require a full day. Your dealer will give a timeframe when you book.

Can a recall fix a failed engine?

Only if the failure is directly caused by the recall defect. For example, a recall for an EGR cooler that leaks coolant into the engine may cover the cooler and associated damage, but not other wear items. Always check the specific campaign details.
