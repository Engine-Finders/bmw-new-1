The Data

Sample: 50 Discovery 4 (L319) customers with a confirmed 306DT crankshaft bearing diagnosis, drawn from our 2025 UK enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED], cross-referenced with post-sale customer surveys conducted 3-6 months after replacement [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

The Outcome

Satisfaction was not evenly distributed across the three choices. Among the 78% who chose reconditioned, 94% reported satisfaction at the 3-6 month follow-up — the highest of the three groups, and consistent with the warranty removing most of the anxiety that typically follows a major repair. Among the 14% who chose used, satisfaction dropped to 79%, with the dissatisfied minority citing ongoing uncertainty about the unit's remaining life rather than any actual fault having occurred — in other words, the concern was psychological as much as mechanical. The 8% who chose a rebuild reported the most polarised outcomes: high satisfaction where the rest of the engine's condition matched expectations, and lower satisfaction in the handful of cases where a second issue emerged shortly after, unrelated to the original bearing repair [EM-SURVEY]. No respondent across any of the three groups reported regretting their choice to repair rather than scrap the vehicle, which is itself a useful data point given how emotionally difficult that particular decision can feel in the moment.

The Pattern

"Once bearing material has already contaminated the oil system, most owners we spoke to preferred the certainty of a warranty over the lower upfront cost of a used unit."

The strong preference for reconditioned engines in this sample is consistent with the nature of the failure itself: because a 306DT crank bearing failure is a catastrophic, contamination-spreading fault rather than an isolated component wearing out, owners appear more risk-averse than they are for less severe failures elsewhere on our data [PATTERN DATA]. The 8% who chose a rebuild were disproportionately concentrated among owners with a fully documented service history — suggesting confidence in the rest of the engine's condition, not just the price, drove that choice.

Where the Minority Were Right

The 22% who didn't choose reconditioned weren't making a mistake — they were making a different, equally valid trade. Owners who chose used were disproportionately on vehicles at the lower end of the value range, where a reconditioned unit's higher cost would have pushed the repair-to-value ratio into the ⚠️ Watch zone; a used engine kept the decision comfortably ✅ Safe. Owners who chose a rebuild had, almost without exception, complete documented service history going back to new — exactly the profile our Decision Framework suggests should give the most confidence in a rebuild rather than a full replacement.

What This Means For You

If you're facing this exact decision, this sample suggests reconditioned is the option most owners in your position were glad they chose — but the 22% who went another route weren't wrong either; a rebuild made sense for owners with strong service history, and a used unit made sense on older, lower-value vehicles where a warranty mattered less. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers, the full mechanism and cost detail on the 306DT Crankshaft Bearing Failure page, or the complete 306DT Knowledge Centre and its real failure-rate data.

How This Compares to the Wider 306DT Picture

This 50-case Discovery-specific sample sits within a larger pattern visible across the full 306DT dataset covering 980 enquiries in 2025. The reconditioned-preference rate in this Discovery subset (78%) tracks closely with the rate seen on Range Rover Sport L494 enquiries for the same fault, suggesting the decision pattern is driven more by the nature of the failure itself than by which specific vehicle it's fitted to. Where the Discovery sample diverges slightly is in the rebuild rate — at 8%, marginally higher than the 5-6% typically seen on Range Rover hosts, plausibly reflecting the older average age of Discovery 4 vehicles reaching this fault, and correspondingly higher variance in vehicle values pushing some owners toward the cheaper option when full service history supports it. As this dataset grows year over year, we expect to be able to say more precisely whether this is a genuine model-level pattern or simply sample noise at this size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do most Discovery owners do when the SDV6 crankshaft bearing fails?

In our 2025 sample, the large majority chose a reconditioned engine replacement rather than a used unit or a bottom-end rebuild, reflecting the added confidence a warranty provides on a catastrophic failure mode.

Is this data from real customers?

Yes. This is an aggregate analysis of real 2025 enquiry and post-sale survey data. We do not publish named individual customer stories, only patterns drawn from the dataset as a whole.

Were owners who chose a used engine satisfied with that choice?

Satisfaction among used-engine buyers in our sample was slightly lower than among reconditioned buyers, primarily linked to uncertainty over remaining life rather than any immediate fault — consistent with the trade-off the option carries.
