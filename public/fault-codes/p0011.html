What P0011 Actually Means

In BMW's VANOS system, the ECU controls the camshaft position using variable valve timing. P0011 is logged when the intake camshaft timing is over-advanced — meaning the camshaft is too far advanced relative to the crankshaft position. This code specifically applies to the intake camshaft on Bank 1 (the cylinder bank containing cylinder 1).

Crucially, P0011 describes a measured timing error, not a specific component failure. The ECU compares the actual camshaft position (via the camshaft position sensor) against the target position it commanded. When the actual position deviates too far, the code is set. The cause could be mechanical (stretched timing chain, worn VANOS unit), hydraulic (low oil pressure, dirty solenoid), or electrical (failing sensor). This distinction is the difference between a £90 solenoid clean and a £2,500 timing chain job. This code appears across the full range of BMW VANOS-equipped engines, from the N47 diesel to the N57 six-cylinder, and the diagnostic approach is similar regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, usually amber
  • Reduced power or limp mode — especially noticeable under acceleration
  • Rough idle or misfire, particularly on cold start
  • Poor acceleration or hesitation when pressing the throttle
  • Rattle or ticking noise from the top of the engine on cold start — often a sign of timing chain stretch on N47/N57 engines
  • Reduced fuel economy and increased emissions

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check oil level and condition

VANOS uses oil pressure to function. If the oil is low or overdue, change it first. A meaningful share of P0011 cases resolve with a simple oil and filter change.

2

Swap VANOS solenoids

Swap the intake and exhaust solenoids. If the fault moves to P0012 or P0015, the solenoid is the issue. A £90 clean or £150 replacement often fixes it.

3

Live-data the camshaft

A specialist compares commanded vs actual camshaft position on live data. A consistent offset at idle points to mechanical stretch or VANOS unit wear.

4

Check timing chain stretch

On N47/N57 engines, check chain stretch using BMW diagnostic software. If stretch exceeds specification, the chain and tensioner need replacement.

5

Only then consider the VANOS unit

If steps 1–4 are clear, the VANOS actuator itself may be worn internally. This is the most expensive non-chain repair.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — short journeys to get it diagnosed. P0011 on its own is rarely engine-destroying in the short term. But if the car has entered limp mode, motorway driving becomes unsafe because you cannot accelerate normally. And if the underlying cause is timing chain stretch (common on N47/N57), continued driving risks catastrophic engine failure when the chain snaps. If you hear a cold-start rattle, get it checked immediately — don't wait. For the vast majority of cases (solenoid or oil-related), the car is safe to drive gently to a garage. Get it looked at within days, not weeks.

Related Fault Codes

P0011 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0012 — Camshaft Timing Over-Retarded

The mirror image. If both are logged intermittently, suspect the solenoid or oil pressure rather than timing chain stretch.

P0016 — Camshaft/Crankshaft Position Correlation

Strongly suggests timing chain stretch or a skipped tooth. Often accompanies P0011 on N47/N57 engines with chain wear.

P0021 — Bank 2 Camshaft Timing Over-Advanced

Same issue on the other bank of a V-engine. If both banks are over-advanced, suspect a global timing issue rather than a single solenoid.

Cold-start rattle

Not a code, but a critical symptom. Rattling on start-up alongside P0011 is a red flag for timing chain wear. See our timing chain diagnosis guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does P0011 mean on a BMW?

P0011 means the intake camshaft timing is over-advanced relative to the crankshaft. It indicates a problem with the VANOS system — usually a solenoid, oil pressure issue, or timing chain stretch.

Can I drive with P0011 on my BMW?

Short distances to a garage are usually OK, but avoid hard acceleration and motorway driving. If the car is in limp mode, stop immediately and get it towed. If you hear a cold-start rattle, do not drive it — timing chain failure is imminent.

How much does it cost to fix P0011 on a BMW?

From £90 for an oil change to £2,500 for a timing chain replacement. Most cases are solenoids or oil-related, costing £120–£300. Always diagnose cheapest-first — see the diagnostic order above.

Does P0011 always mean the timing chain has failed?

No. Across the ranked causes, timing chain stretch is only medium likelihood — and on N47/N57 engines, it becomes more likely with mileage and cold-start rattle. But a simple clogged solenoid or old oil is far more common and far cheaper to fix.

What is the 70% scrap threshold for BMW?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If a timing chain replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be wiser to scrap or sell the car. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.

Is there a BMW recall for P0011 or timing chain issues?

BMW has issued recalls and extended warranties on timing chains for certain N47 engines. Check with your dealer using your VIN to see if any recall applies to your vehicle.
