P0299 indicates the engine control unit has detected that boost pressure from the turbocharger is significantly below the expected level. On most BMWs this is a boost leak or control issue – not a failed turbocharger – and it should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

What P0299 Actually Means on a BMW

The DME (engine control unit) uses a boost pressure sensor (often a MAP sensor) to monitor the pressure generated by the turbocharger. It compares the actual boost to a target value based on engine speed, load, and other parameters. P0299 is logged when the actual boost pressure falls significantly below the target – typically by 200–300 millibars or more. This is a performance code, not a component failure code. The underboost could be caused by a boost leak (split hose, loose clamp, failed intercooler), a faulty wastegate or actuator, a blocked air filter, or – least commonly – a failed turbocharger. The turbocharger itself is rarely the root cause; it is almost always a leak or control issue. This code appears across the full range of BMW turbocharged petrol and diesel engines, from the N47 to the B58, and the diagnostic approach is similar regardless of engine type.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated, often amber
  • Limp mode or reduced power – the ECU cuts boost to protect the engine
  • Noticeable lack of power, especially under acceleration or on motorway inclines
  • Whistling or hissing sound from the engine bay under load – a classic boost leak symptom
  • Increased fuel consumption – the engine works harder to compensate

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Visual inspection of boost hoses and intercooler

Check all boost pipes and intercooler for splits, cracks, or loose clips. A simple visual check often finds the fault.

2

Check boost pressure sensor readings

Using a diagnostic tool, compare actual boost to atmospheric pressure at key-on, engine off. If it reads incorrectly, the sensor may be faulty.

3

Check air filter condition

A blocked filter can restrict airflow and cause underboost. Replace if dirty.

4

Perform boost leak test (smoke test)

If visual inspection is clear, a smoke test will reveal hidden leaks in the intake system.

5

Check wastegate actuator and control solenoid

Ensure the actuator moves freely and the solenoid operates correctly.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes – short journeys to get it diagnosed, but avoid heavy throttle. P0299 on its own is not an immediate engine‑destroying fault, but continued driving with low boost can cause the engine to run richer, potentially damaging the catalytic converter and increasing fuel consumption. If the car has entered limp mode, motorway driving is unsafe due to lack of power. Get it looked at within a few days – a small boost leak can often be fixed cheaply, but ignoring it can lead to turbo damage if the turbo overspeeds trying to compensate.

Related Fault Codes

P0299 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0234 — Turbo Overboost

The opposite condition – if both appear intermittently, suspect the sensor or boost control solenoid.

P0236 — Boost Sensor Range/Performance

Points to a sensor circuit issue, often cheaper to fix than a turbo problem.

P0033 — Turbo Bypass Valve Control

Relates to the diverter valve; can co-occur with boost control issues.

P0300 — Random Misfire

Can occur if low boost causes lean running or misfires.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0299 on a BMW?

For short journeys to a garage, generally yes – but not for normal use, and not on motorways if the vehicle has entered limp mode, because you won't be able to accelerate safely. Prolonged driving with underboost can damage the catalytic converter and increase fuel consumption.

Does P0299 always mean the turbo has failed?

No. Across the six ranked causes, turbo failure is the least common. A boost leak or a faulty sensor are both far more likely and cost a fraction as much.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the DME detects underboost again, usually during a full‑throttle run. Clearing it also removes freeze‑frame data that helps pinpoint the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent BMW specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.

Is P0299 more common on diesel or petrol BMWs?

It appears on both, but diesel engines with variable geometry turbos are more prone to actuator and boost control issues. However, petrol engines suffer from boost hose splits just as often.
