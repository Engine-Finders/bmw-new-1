P0016 on a BMW means the engine's DME has detected that the crankshaft and camshaft positions are out of sync. On most BMWs this is a correlation fault — the timing is slightly off, not a sudden failure — and it should be diagnosed cheapest-first.

  • Most common real cause: a failing VANOS solenoid or actuator — typically £150–£350 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: timing chain stretch or guide wear, £1,200–£2,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside P0012 (camshaft over‑retarded) or P0021 (over‑advanced)
  • Frequently appears on N47 and N20 engines with timing chain wear

What P0016 Actually Means on a BMW

In BMW engines, the crankshaft position sensor and camshaft position sensor must remain in exact sync for the DME to time fuel injection and ignition correctly. P0016 is logged when the DME sees the camshaft position deviate from the crankshaft position by more than its permitted tolerance — typically a few degrees. Crucially, this is a correlation code, not a component failure code. The camshaft could genuinely be out of time (stretched chain, worn guides, failed tensioner), or a sensor could be reporting incorrect data, or oil pressure to the VANOS unit could be insufficient to hold the correct position. That distinction is the entire difference between a £150 solenoid and a £2,200 timing chain job. This code appears across the full range of BMW engines with VANOS, from the N47 diesel to the N54 twin-turbo inline-six, and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical regardless of which engine triggers it.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0016

  • Engine management light illuminated, often amber
  • Rough idle or misfire, especially on cold start — see misfire and rough idle
  • Reduced power and hesitation during acceleration
  • Hard starting, or extended cranking before the engine catches
  • Occasional rattle or metallic noise from the top end on N47 and N57 diesel engines
  • Increased fuel consumption as the DME attempts to compensate for mistimed events

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check oil level and service history

Low oil or an overdue service can cause VANOS to misbehave. Top up or change first.

2

Inspect and clean VANOS solenoids

Swap solenoids between banks or clean them. On N52/N54, this alone resolves a meaningful share of P0016 cases.

3

Live-data and sensor check

A specialist compares cam and crank signals on live data. A noisy or intermittent sensor reading points to the sensor itself.

4

Check VANOS oil pressure

Oil pressure to the VANOS unit tested. If pressure is low, the cause may be the oil pump or filter.

5

Timing chain inspection

If steps 1–4 are clear and the cam remains consistently out of sync, chain stretch or guide wear is the likely answer.

Can You Still Drive It?

Conditionally, yes — short journeys to get it diagnosed. P0016 on its own is not an engine‑destroying fault the way a loss of oil pressure is. However, if the DME has pulled timing significantly or the car has entered limp mode, prolonged driving risks further damage: a stretched chain can jump teeth, causing valve‑to‑piston contact on interference BMW engines. Get it looked at within a few days, not weeks. If the engine is making a heavy metallic rattle (especially on cold start on N47 or N57), do not drive it at all.

Related Fault Codes

P0016 rarely appears alone. Which codes accompany it materially changes the diagnosis.

P0012 — Camshaft Over‑Retarded

Often accompanies P0016 when the camshaft is genuinely out of time or VANOS is stuck.

P0021 — Camshaft Over‑Advanced

Points toward VANOS control issues or a failing solenoid in the over‑advanced direction.

P0340 — Camshaft Position Sensor Circuit

Strong supporting evidence that the sensor itself is at fault.

P0335 — Crankshaft Position Sensor Circuit

Points toward the crankshaft sensor rather than timing chain; often cheaper to fix.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0016 on a BMW?

For short journeys to a garage, generally yes — but not for normal use, and not at all if the engine is making a metallic rattle on cold start (especially N47/N57). Prolonged driving with a genuinely stretched chain or worn guides can cause the chain to jump teeth, leading to catastrophic engine failure.

Does P0016 always mean the timing chain has failed?

No. Across the six ranked causes, timing chain stretch is only the fourth most common. A sticking VANOS solenoid or a failed camshaft sensor are both far more likely and cost a fraction as much — always diagnose cheapest-first.

Will clearing the code fix it?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the DME sees the correlation error again, usually within a few drive cycles. Clearing it also destroys the freeze‑frame data a specialist would use to narrow the cause.

How much should diagnosis cost?

Expect one to two hours of diagnostic time at an independent BMW specialist. Many will offset that cost against the repair if you proceed with them.

Is P0016 more common on N47 diesel or N52 petrol?

It appears on both, but the N47 timing chain stretch is well‑documented and tends to be the costliest outcome. On N52 and N54, it is more often a solenoid or sensor issue.
