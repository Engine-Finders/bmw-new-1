The mirror image of P0401 – this code indicates the EGR system is flowing too much exhaust gas into the intake. Often a stuck-open valve or a faulty differential pressure sensor, not a catastrophic failure. Here's what to check and in what order.

What P0402 Actually Means on BMW Diesels

In a modern BMW common-rail diesel, the EGR system is carefully controlled by the ECU. The differential pressure sensor across the EGR cooler and the temperature sensor measure the actual flow. When the measured flow exceeds the commanded flow by a significant margin – typically more than 30% – the ECU logs P0402. This is the exact opposite of P0401 (insufficient flow). The most common cause is a valve that fails to close fully due to carbon fouling, or a sensor that reports incorrectly. Unlike P0401, a stuck-open EGR valve can cause rough idling, increased fuel consumption, and even engine stalling at low speeds. The code appears across all BMW diesel families – from N47 and B47 four‑cylinder units to N57 and B57 six‑cylinder engines – and the diagnostic approach is largely the same.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Engine management light illuminated (amber or yellow)
  • Rough idle or hesitation when pulling away – the engine may even stall at low RPM
  • Noticeable reduction in power, especially at low speeds
  • Increased fuel consumption (the ECU may inject extra fuel to compensate)
  • Occasional black smoke from the exhaust, though less common than with P0401
  • In some cases, a hissing or whistling noise from the EGR valve area

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Read live data

Compare commanded EGR position vs actual flow. If flow is high even at idle, suspect a stuck-open valve.

2

Test the differential pressure sensor

Check sensor output with a vacuum pump or substitute a known‑good unit. Many P0402 cases are misdiagnosed as valve faults when the sensor is the issue.

3

Inspect the EGR valve for carbon

Remove and inspect. If it doesn't close fully, clean it or replace if the shaft is worn.

4

Check wiring and connectors

Inspect sensor plugs for corrosion; perform continuity tests from sensor to ECU.

5

Consider ECU software

If all else is clear, check for software updates or tuning-related changes.

Can You Still Drive It?

Yes, but with caution. P0402 itself is not immediately engine‑destroying, but a stuck‑open EGR valve can cause rough running, stalling at low speeds, and increased fuel consumption. If the engine stalls repeatedly or the car enters limp mode, avoid motorway driving. The primary risk is that excessive EGR flow can lead to carbon buildup in the intake manifold over time, which may eventually cause performance issues. Get it diagnosed within a few days; a simple clean or sensor swap usually resolves it. If you notice significant power loss or stalling, stop driving and arrange recovery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0402 on a BMW?

Yes, for short trips to a garage. Avoid motorways if the engine stalls or limp mode is active. Prolonged driving with a stuck-open valve can lead to carbon buildup and reduced performance.

Does P0402 always mean the EGR valve needs replacing?

No. Often a clean is sufficient, and in many cases the differential pressure sensor is the real culprit – cheaper and easier to replace than the valve itself.

Will clearing the code fix P0402?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU sees the flow discrepancy again, typically within one drive cycle. Clearing it also erases freeze‑frame data that helps with diagnosis.

How much does a BMW P0402 diagnosis cost?

Expect 1–2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent BMW specialist, typically £100–£200. Many specialists will waive or offset this if you proceed with the repair.

Is P0402 covered under BMW warranty or recall?

Some BMW models had EGR cooler recalls (e.g., certain N47 and B47 engines). Check your VIN against BMW UK recalls or use our recall checker.
