P0670 BMW — Glow Plug Control Module Circuit Malfunction

This code indicates a fault in the glow plug control module circuit – typically a wiring issue, a failed relay, or a faulty module. It affects cold starting on diesel engines and is usually straightforward and inexpensive to fix.

P0670 on a BMW diesel means the ECU has detected a fault in the glow plug control module circuit – the module that supplies power to the glow plugs for cold starts. The most common cause is a blown fuse, a poor connection, or a failed relay – not a failed module itself.

  • Most common fix: Glow plug relay or fuse replacement – £80–£200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: Glow plug control module replacement – £300–£500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by glow plug warning light and hard starting in cold weather
  • May appear with P0671 to P0678 (individual glow plug circuit faults)

Do not replace the control module before checking the fuse, relay, and wiring – these are far more common and cheaper. Full diagnostic order below.

What P0670 Actually Means on BMW Diesels

The glow plug control module (sometimes called the glow plug relay) is responsible for supplying power to the glow plugs before and during cold starting. The ECU monitors the circuit's voltage and current draw. P0670 is set when the ECU detects an open circuit, short circuit, or excessive current draw in the control module's primary circuit – essentially, the module isn't responding or power isn't reaching the glow plugs. Crucially, the code describes an electrical fault, not a mechanical one. The module itself could be faulty, but it's far more common for a blown fuse, a corroded connector, or a failed relay to cause the same code. This code appears across all BMW diesel families – from N47 and B47 four‑cylinder units to the N57 and B57 six‑cylinder engines – and the diagnostic approach is essentially identical.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside It

  • Glow plug warning light illuminated or flashing on the dash
  • Extended cranking time before the engine starts, especially in cold weather
  • Rough idle or misfire immediately after starting when cold
  • Reduced fuel economy (the ECU may inject extra fuel to compensate)
  • In some cases, white smoke from the exhaust on cold starts
  • No other drivability issues once the engine is warm

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest First

1

Check fuses and relays

Locate the glow plug control module fuse and relay in the engine bay fuse box. A blown fuse is a simple fix.

2

Inspect wiring and connector

Visually check the harness to the module for chafing, breaks, or corrosion. Wiggle the connector while monitoring live data.

3

Test the module with a multimeter

Check for power supply and ground at the module. If they're present but no output, the module is likely faulty.

4

Replace the glow plug control module

If fuses, wiring, and relays are good, the module itself is the most likely failure point.

Can You Still Drive It?

Yes, but cold starts will be difficult. P0670 does not cause immediate engine damage, but if the glow plugs aren't heating, the engine may struggle to start in cold weather. Once started and warmed up, the car will run normally. However, prolonged hard starting can stress the battery and starter motor. Get it diagnosed before winter sets in, but it's not an emergency in warmer months. If the engine won't start at all, the fault may be more severe – have it recovered.

Related Fault Codes

P0670 often appears with other codes – the combination narrows the diagnosis significantly.

P0671 — Glow Plug Cylinder 1 Circuit

If you have individual cylinder glow plug codes, suspect a failed glow plug or wiring to that plug.

P0672 — Glow Plug Cylinder 2 Circuit

Same as above – individual glow plug faults may accompany the module fault.

P0673 — Glow Plug Cylinder 3 Circuit

Multiple cylinder codes suggest the module or common wiring is at fault.

Glow Plug Warning Light

If the light is flashing, it often indicates a serious glow system fault – check immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive with P0670 on a BMW?

Yes, but cold starts will be difficult. Once the engine is warm, it runs normally. Avoid repeated hard starting to protect the battery and starter. Get it diagnosed soon.

Does P0670 always mean the glow plug control module needs replacing?

No. A blown fuse or a corroded connector is far more common – both much cheaper than a new module. Always check those first.

Will clearing the code fix P0670?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU runs the glow plug system test, typically on the next cold start. Clearing it erases freeze‑frame data that helps with diagnosis.

How much does a BMW P0670 diagnosis cost?

Expect 1–2 hours of diagnostic time at an independent BMW specialist, typically £100–£200. Many specialists will waive or offset this if you proceed with the repair.

Is P0670 covered under BMW warranty or recall?

Glow plug systems are generally not covered by recalls, but some models had extended warranties on the glow plug module. Check your VIN against BMW UK recalls or use our recall checker.
