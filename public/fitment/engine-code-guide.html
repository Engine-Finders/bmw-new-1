The Short Answer

Your BMW engine code is a unique identifier that tells you the exact variant, power output, and generation of your engine. You can find it on the engine label (timing cover), in the VIN (8th character), or on your V5C logbook. The most common codes are N47, B47, N57, B57, N63, S63, and B58 — each with distinct characteristics and reliability profiles. Always confirm your code before ordering parts or a replacement.

How to Find Your BMW Engine Code

There are three reliable ways to locate your BMW engine code. Use the method that works best for your situation.

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and look for a sticker or metal tag on the timing cover, cylinder head, or front engine block. The code is usually printed as a combination of letters and numbers, e.g., N47D20C or B58B30. This is the most direct method.

  2. Decode the VIN (8th character).

    The 8th character of your BMW's VIN (Vehicle Identification Number) indicates the engine type. For example, an 'N' often means N-series diesel, while a 'B' means B-series. Use our BMW VIN decoder to get a full breakdown of your engine code and options.

  3. Check your registration document (V5C).

    The V5C logbook includes the engine number, which can be cross-referenced with BMW's engine list. While this is less direct, it's a useful backup if the engine label is damaged or missing.

Why Correct Engine Identification Matters

Using the wrong engine code can lead to costly mistakes when buying parts, ordering a replacement, or even doing routine maintenance. Here's why accuracy is critical:

  • ECU and emissions compatibility: Different engine variants have different engine control unit (ECU) calibrations, wiring looms, and emissions hardware (e.g., AdBlue, DPF). Installing the wrong code can trigger limp mode or fail MOT emissions tests.
  • Physical differences: Even within the same family, mounting points, sump shapes, and accessory drive layouts can vary. An N47 from an E90 is not a bolt-in for an F30.
  • Transmission and drivetrain integration: Some engine codes are paired with specific gearboxes or transfer cases. Mismatches can cause driveline issues.
  • Specialist support and warranty: Most vetted BMW specialists will not work on mismatched combinations, and any warranty on a replacement engine may be void if the code doesn't match the chassis.

Always confirm the engine code before purchasing parts or a replacement unit. Use our Engine Compatibility Checker to verify fitment.

Common Engine Code Mistakes

BMW's engine codes are logical but easy to confuse, especially across model years. Here are the most frequent errors:

N47 vs. B47

Both are 2.0-litre diesels, but the N47 is pre-2015 (roughly) with a rear-mounted timing chain. The B47 is the successor with a front-mounted chain, improved EGR, and better emissions. They are not interchangeable — the ECU, loom, and ancillaries differ. Always verify before ordering.

N57 vs. B57

The 3.0-litre diesel family: N57 is older (E60, F10, E70), B57 is modern (G30, G11, G01). The B57 has a front chain and is much more reliable. They do not share mounting points or electrical architecture. If you're replacing, ensure you get the correct generation.

N63 vs. S63

Both are 4.4-litre V8s, but the S63 is the M-performance variant (M5, M6, X5M). The S63 has stronger internals, different turbos, and a different ECU map. Fitting an N63 into an M car will not work without major re-engineering. Always check the full code.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the BMW engine code look like?

BMW engine codes are typically alphanumeric, e.g., N47D20C, B47D20A, B58B30. The first letter is the family (N or B), the next two numbers are the displacement (e.g., 47 = 2.0? Actually 47 is 2.0? It's a code), the next letter indicates fuel type or generation, and the last letters/numbers are variant-specific.

How can I decode my BMW engine code from the VIN?

The 8th character of the VIN is the engine code character. You can use our BMW VIN decoder to get a full breakdown, including the exact engine code, power output, and options.

Can I use a B-series engine to replace an N-series in my BMW?

Not without extensive modification. The ECU, wiring loom, emissions hardware, engine mounts, and even the subframe may differ. It's almost always more cost-effective to replace like-for-like or sell the car and buy one with the B-series engine already fitted.

What is the most reliable BMW engine code?

Based on our enquiry and failure pattern data, the B58 inline-six petrol is considered the most reliable modern BMW engine. The B47 and B57 are also strong. The N47 and N63 are the most problematic. For a full verdict, see the B58 Knowledge Centre.

How much does it cost to replace an N47 or B47 engine?

Based on our internal quote data [EM-QUOTE], a reconditioned N47D20C supply-only ranges from £3,200 to £5,500, while a B47D20A ranges from £2,800 to £4,500. Fitting costs (R&R labour) are similar at £650–£910 for an independent specialist. See the replacement cost guide for full details.

What does the 70% scrap threshold mean for BMW owners?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be financially wiser to scrap or sell the car rather than repair. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.
