B Series vs N Series: How to Identify Your BMW Engine

BMW's N-series (N47, N57, N63) and B-series (B47, B57, B58) engines are fundamentally different families. This guide shows you how to identify which one is under your bonnet, what the differences mean for reliability and cost, and how to confirm your engine code before buying a replacement.

The Short Answer

Your BMW has either an N-series (older) or B-series (newer) engine family. The quickest way to tell is to check the 8th character of your VIN or look at the engine code label on the timing cover. N-series engines (N47, N57, N63) are found in pre-2015–2018 models; B-series (B47, B57, B58) replaced them from around 2015 onwards with improved reliability and efficiency. The B-series is generally more reliable and cheaper to maintain long-term.

  • N47: 1,450 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED] — timing chain issues are common.
  • B47: 930 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED] — front-mounted chain, far fewer failures.
  • B58: 540 enquiries [EM-VERIFIED] — the modern petrol inline-six, highly regarded.
  • Scrap threshold: BMW uses 70% — if replacement cost exceeds 70% of vehicle value, scrapping may be the better decision.

How to Confirm Your BMW Engine Code

There are three reliable ways to identify whether your BMW has an N-series or B-series engine. Use the method that best suits your situation.

  1. Check the VIN (8th character).

    The 8th character of your BMW's VIN indicates the engine type. For example, N is common for N-series diesel (e.g., N47), B for B-series petrol/diesel (e.g., B58). Use the BMW VIN decoder for a full breakdown.

  2. Look at the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and locate the engine code on the timing cover or a sticker near the cylinder head. The code will be clearly stamped, e.g., N47D20C or B58B30. This is the most direct method.

  3. Check your vehicle registration document (V5C).

    The V5C logbook includes the engine number, which can be cross-referenced with BMW's engine list. This is a useful secondary check if the label is not accessible.

Why Correct Identification Matters

Getting the engine family wrong is a costly mistake. While an N47 and B47 are both 2.0-litre diesels, they are not interchangeable without significant modification. Here's why:

  • ECU and emissions: B-series engines have different ECU mappings, wiring looms, and emissions hardware (e.g., AdBlue on some) compared to N-series.
  • Physical layout: The timing chain is at the rear on N-series, front on B-series. This affects accessory drive, engine mounts, and even the sump design.
  • Turbo and fuel systems: B-series use newer generation turbochargers and fuel injection systems; they are not a direct swap.
  • Specialist support: Most vetted BMW specialists will not work on mismatched combinations — or they will charge significantly more for custom work.

Always verify the exact engine code against the chassis before buying a used or reconditioned unit. The Engine Compatibility Checker can confirm fitment.

Common BMW Engine Misidentifications

The B-series and N-series families are often confused, even by experienced owners. Here are the most frequent mistakes:

N47 vs. B47

Both are 2.0-litre diesel, but the N47 is pre-2015 (roughly) with a rear-mounted timing chain. The B47 is the successor with a front-mounted chain, improved EGR, and better emissions. They are not interchangeable — the ECU, loom, and ancillaries differ.

N47 vs. B47 comparison

N57 vs. B57

The 3.0-litre diesel family: N57 is older (E60, F10, E70), B57 is modern (G30, G11, G01). The B57 has a front chain and is much more reliable. They do not share mounting points or electrical architecture.

N57 vs. B57 comparison

N63 vs. S63

Both are 4.4-litre V8s, but the S63 is the M-performance variant (M5, M6, X5M). The S63 has stronger internals, different turbos, and a different ECU. Fitting an N63 into an M car will not work without major re-engineering.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between N-series and B-series BMW engines?

N-series engines are older (pre-2015–2018), with rear-mounted timing chains on many models and known reliability issues (e.g., N47 chain stretch). B-series are modern replacements with front-mounted chains, improved emissions, better fuel economy, and significantly higher reliability.

How can I tell if my BMW has an N47 or B47 engine?

The easiest way is to check the 8th character of your VIN. For N47, it will be a letter like 'N' or 'C' (depending on variant). For B47, it will be 'B'. You can also look at the engine code label on the timing cover; N47 codes start with 'N', B47 with 'B'.

Can I swap an N-series engine for a B-series in my BMW?

Not without extensive modification. The ECU, wiring loom, emissions hardware, engine mounts, and even the subframe may differ. It's almost always more cost-effective to replace like-for-like or sell the car and buy one with the B-series engine already fitted.

Which BMW engine is more reliable: N-series or B-series?

The B-series engines (B47, B57, B58) are significantly more reliable than their N-series predecessors. They have addressed the timing chain weaknesses and are designed with modularity and durability in mind. Based on our enquiry data, B-series engines have far fewer failure-related enquiries.
