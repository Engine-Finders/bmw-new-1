N47 vs N57: How to Identify Your BMW Engine

BMW's N47 and N57 are two of the most common diesel engines from the N-series family. The N47 is a 2.0-litre four-cylinder, while the N57 is a 3.0-litre six-cylinder. This guide helps you identify which one is in your car, understand the key differences, and avoid costly mistakes when ordering parts or a replacement.

The Short Answer

Your BMW has either an N47 (2.0-litre four-cylinder diesel) or N57 (3.0-litre six-cylinder diesel) engine. The quickest way to tell is to check the engine code label on the timing cover or use the 8th character of the VIN. N47 engines are found in 1, 3, 5 Series and X models with "20d" badges; N57 engines are in models with "25d", "30d", "35d" badges. The N57 is generally more powerful and more refined, but both share similar timing chain weaknesses.

How to Confirm Your BMW Engine (N47 vs N57)

There are three reliable ways to identify whether your BMW has an N47 or N57 engine. Use the method that works best for you.

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and look for a sticker or metal tag on the timing cover, cylinder head, or front engine block. The code will be printed as N47D20C or N57D30. This is the most direct method.

  2. Decode the VIN (8th character).

    The 8th character of your BMW's VIN indicates the engine type. For example, an 'N' often means N-series diesel, but the exact code is best confirmed with a BMW VIN decoder.

  3. Check the registration document (V5C).

    The V5C logbook includes the engine number, which can be cross-referenced with BMW's engine list. This is a useful backup if the engine label is damaged.

Why Correct Engine Identification Matters

Getting the engine family wrong can lead to expensive mistakes when ordering parts, buying a replacement, or even doing routine maintenance. Here's why accuracy is critical:

  • ECU and emissions: N47 and N57 have different engine control units, wiring looms, and emissions hardware (DPF, EGR). They are not compatible.
  • Physical dimensions: The N57 is a longer six-cylinder engine; mounting points, subframes, and ancillaries differ. An N57 will not fit in an N47 engine bay without significant modification.
  • Turbo and fuel systems: The N57 often has twin-turbo or variable geometry turbos, while the N47 uses a single turbo. Fuel injectors, pumps, and plumbing differ.
  • Specialist support: Most vetted BMW specialists will confirm the exact code before ordering parts. A mismatch can lead to return costs and wasted labour.

Common Engine Code Mistakes: N47 vs N57

These two engines are often confused, especially because they share the same "N" family and both appear in similar models. Here are the most frequent errors:

N47 vs. B47

The B47 is the successor to the N47, with a front-mounted timing chain and improved emissions. They are not interchangeable. If you have a post-2014 model, you likely have a B47, not an N47.

N57 vs. B57

The B57 is the modern replacement for the N57, with a front chain and better reliability. They are often mistaken because they share the 3.0L displacement, but they are different families.

N47 vs. N57 – same timing chain issue?

Both have rear-mounted timing chains prone to stretch, but the failure rates and costs differ. The N57 chain replacement is generally more expensive due to the larger engine and labour complexity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between N47 and N57 BMW engines?

The N47 is a 2.0-litre four-cylinder turbo diesel, while the N57 is a 3.0-litre six-cylinder turbo diesel. Both are from the N-series family and share a rear-mounted timing chain design. The N57 is more powerful and refined but also more expensive to repair.

How can I tell if my BMW has an N47 or N57 engine?

Check the engine code label on the timing cover; it will say N47D20C or N57D30. Alternatively, check the 8th character of your VIN with a VIN decoder or look at the engine number on the V5C logbook.

Can I swap an N57 engine into a car that originally had an N47?

Not without extensive modification. The N57 is physically larger, has different engine mounts, ECU, wiring, cooling system, and transmission compatibility. It is almost never cost-effective.

Which engine is more reliable: N47 or N57?

Both have similar timing chain weaknesses. The N57 has a higher failure rate due to its larger size and additional components (twin turbos). Based on our enquiry data, the N57 generates fewer requests (830 vs 1,450 for N47) but that may reflect vehicle parc size rather than reliability. Both require careful maintenance.
