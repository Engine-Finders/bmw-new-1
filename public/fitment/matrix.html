How to Confirm Your BMW Engine Code

Use these three methods to identify your BMW engine and cross-reference it with the fitment matrix.

  1. Check the engine code label.

    Open the bonnet and locate the engine code on the timing cover or a sticker on the cylinder head. The code will be clearly stamped, e.g., N47D20C or B58B30. This is the most direct method.

  2. Decode the VIN (8th character).

    The 8th character of your BMW's VIN indicates the engine type. Use our BMW VIN decoder to get a full breakdown of your engine code and options.

  3. Check your registration document (V5C).

    The V5C logbook includes the engine number, which can be cross-referenced with BMW's engine list. This is a useful backup if the label is damaged.

If you're still unsure, the Engine Compatibility Checker can help match your vehicle details to the correct engine.

Why Correct Engine Identification Matters

Getting the engine code right is critical — especially if you are considering a replacement. Installing the wrong engine variant can lead to:

  • ECU compatibility issues — The engine control unit is programmed for a specific power output and emissions setup.
  • Mounting and fitment problems — Even within the same family, slight variations in auxiliary components can prevent a direct fit.
  • Legal compliance failures — Installing an engine not originally homologated for your chassis can create MOT or insurance complications.
  • Voided warranty or specialist support — Most vetted BMW specialists will not work on mismatched engine/chassis combinations.

Always verify the exact engine code against the chassis before buying a used or reconditioned unit. The Engine Compatibility Checker is the fastest way to confirm fitment.

Common BMW Engine Misidentifications

BMW's engine naming conventions are logical but can be confusing across model years. Here are the most common mistakes:

N47 vs. B47

Both are 2.0-litre diesels, but the N47 is pre-2015 with a rear-mounted timing chain; the B47 is the successor with a front-mounted chain. They are not interchangeable — the ECU, loom, and ancillaries differ.

Compare N47 vs. B47

N57 vs. B57

The 3.0-litre diesel family: N57 is older (E60, F10, E70), B57 is modern (G30, G11, G01). The B57 has a front chain and is much more reliable. They do not share mounting points or electrical architecture.

Compare N57 vs. B57

N63 vs. S63

Both are 4.4-litre V8s, but the S63 is the M-performance variant (M5, M6, X5M). The S63 has stronger internals, different turbos, and a different ECU map. Fitting an N63 into an M car will not work without major re-engineering.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find my BMW engine code from the VIN?

The 8th character of the VIN is the engine type code. For example, if it's 'N', you likely have an N-series engine. For a full breakdown, use our BMW VIN decoder.

Can I swap a B-series engine into an N-series chassis?

Not without extensive modification. The ECU, wiring loom, emissions hardware, engine mounts, and even the subframe may differ. It's almost always more cost-effective to replace like-for-like.

What is the most reliable BMW engine family?

Based on our enquiry and failure pattern data, the B58 inline-six petrol is considered the most reliable modern BMW engine. The B47 and B57 are also strong. The N47 and N63 are the most problematic.

How much does a replacement N47 or B47 engine cost?

Based on our internal quote data [EM-QUOTE], a reconditioned N47D20C supply-only ranges from £3,200 to £5,500, while a B47D20A ranges from £2,800 to £4,500. Fitting costs (R&R labour) are similar at £650–£910 for an independent specialist.

What does the 70% scrap threshold mean for BMW owners?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be financially wiser to scrap or sell the car rather than repair. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.

Is there a difference between N47D20A, C, and D?

Yes, these are different power output versions: A = 116d/118d, C = 120d/320d, D = 123d/325d. The block is the same but the turbo, injectors, and ECU map differ. Always match the exact variant.
