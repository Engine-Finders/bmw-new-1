The Short Answer

BMW S55 crank hub failure is a known and potentially catastrophic weakness on the twin‑turbo straight‑six, where the crank hub slips on the crankshaft, causing timing to jump. It typically manifests as a sudden loss of power, engine warning lights, or complete engine failure. Repair costs can range from £3,000 to £8,000+ for a crank hub fix and timing reset, with full engine replacement potentially exceeding £12,000. Early diagnosis is essential to avoid a much larger bill.

  • Most common cause: The crank hub slipping due to high torque and inadequate clamping force from the factory.
  • Affects: BMW S55 engines in M3, M4 (F80/F82), and M2 Competition (F87) from 2014 onwards.
  • Typical repair cost: £3,000–£6,000 for a crank hub fix and timing reset; £8,000–£12,000+ for a new engine if catastrophic.
  • 70% scrap threshold: If repair exceeds 70% of vehicle value, scrapping may be the better option, but this is a rare scenario for this repair.

Symptom Progression — What Owners Notice, In Order

S55 crank hub failures rarely happen without warning. The symptoms can be subtle at first, but they escalate quickly if ignored.

  1. Stage 1 – Intermittent engine warning light. The check engine light may appear, often with timing-related fault codes (e.g., P0015, P0016). The car may still drive normally, but the ECU has detected a timing deviation.
  2. Stage 2 – Slight hesitation or rough running. You may notice a slight hesitation under hard acceleration or a lumpy idle. The engine may feel "off" without a clear reason.
  3. Stage 3 – Sudden loss of power / limp mode. The engine may suddenly go into limp mode, with a significant loss of power. This is often accompanied by a "Drivetrain Malfunction" warning.
  4. Stage 4 – No start or severe misfire. If the hub slips significantly, the timing jumps far enough that the engine either won't start or runs with severe misfires and a loud knocking noise.
  5. Stage 5 – Complete engine destruction. In the worst case, piston-to-valve contact occurs, bending valves and destroying the engine. This is catastrophic and requires a full engine replacement.

If you notice any of these signs, stop driving immediately. Continuing to drive with a crank hub issue can turn a £3,000 repair into a £12,000 engine replacement. For related timing issues, see our engine knocking noise guide.

Root Cause — Why S55 Crank Hubs Fail

The S55 engine uses a two-piece crank hub design. The hub is pressed onto the crankshaft and secured with a single central bolt. Under high torque loads, the hub can slip on the crankshaft, causing the timing chains to jump. The primary reasons are:

  • Insufficient clamping force. The single bolt design does not provide enough clamping force to handle the high torque output of the S55, especially when tuned.
  • Keyless design. Unlike traditional engines with a woodruff key, the S55 relies solely on friction to hold the hub in place. This is a fundamental design flaw.
  • High torque and aggressive driving. The hub is most likely to slip under hard acceleration, downshifts, or when the engine is under heavy load at low RPM.
  • Early production issues. Some early S55 engines (2014–2016) had marginal hub tolerances that made them more prone to failure.

The keyless crank hub design is the root cause – it's a well-known weakness that BMW never fully addressed. For a broader view of S55 issues, see our S55 timing chain guide.

What Makes Failure More or Less Likely

🏁

High Torque and Aggressive Driving

Frequent hard launches, aggressive downshifts, and sustained high‑RPM use place the most stress on the crank hub. Track use and tuned cars are at significantly higher risk.

⚙️

Engine Tuning

Aftermarket engine tuning increases torque output, which directly increases the load on the crank hub. Most catastrophic failures occur on tuned cars, especially those with increased boost.

📅

Mileage and Production Year

Failures can occur at any mileage, but most happen between 20,000 and 60,000 miles. Early‑production engines (2014–2016) have a higher incidence of failures.

How a Specialist Confirms It

1

Diagnostic Scan

A BMW‑compatible diagnostic tool will read fault codes from the engine control unit (ECU). Common codes include P0015 (camshaft timing over‑retarded) and P0016 (crankshaft/camshaft correlation).

2

Timing Verification

The specialist will manually verify the timing by checking the position of the camshafts relative to the crankshaft. A deviation of even a few degrees indicates hub slippage.

3

Visual Inspection

With the front timing cover removed, the specialist can inspect the crank hub for signs of slipping, such as witness marks or damage to the hub and crankshaft.

Prevention & Early Detection

While the crank hub is a design weakness, there are steps you can take to reduce the risk of failure.

  • Install a keyed crank hub or upgraded hub. Aftermarket solutions (e.g., VTT, Gintani, or OEM+ with a pin) eliminate the keyless design flaw and are the only permanent fix.
  • Keep the engine stock or with conservative tuning. Increased torque from tuning directly stresses the hub. If you tune, consider upgrading the hub at the same time.
  • Avoid aggressive launches and low‑RPM full‑throttle. The hub is most vulnerable at low RPM and high load – be gentle with the throttle in those conditions.
  • Listen for unusual noises and watch for warning lights. Any timing‑related fault code should be investigated immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of S55 crank hub failure?

The first sign is usually an intermittent engine warning light with timing‑related fault codes (P0015, P0016). You may also notice a slight hesitation under acceleration or a lumpy idle.

Can I fix an S55 crank hub myself?

This is a major job that requires removing the front timing cover, verifying timing, and installing a new hub. It is not a DIY task – specialist tools and experience are essential to avoid catastrophic engine damage.

How much does an S55 crank hub fix cost in the UK?

Preventative hub replacement (with a keyed or upgraded hub) typically costs £3,000–£4,500 at an independent specialist. If the hub has already slipped and caused damage, the cost can rise to £6,000–£9,000 or more.

Is it worth fixing an S55 crank hub on an older M car?

It depends on the car's overall condition and value. A preventative job is almost always worth it, as it preserves the car's reliability and value. However, if the engine has already failed, the repair may be uneconomical on a low‑value example.

What is the 70% scrap threshold for BMW?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be financially wiser to scrap or sell the car rather than repair. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.

Is there a recall for BMW S55 crank hub issues?

There is no official recall for the crank hub on the S55, as BMW considers it a design feature rather than a defect. However, many specialists recommend upgrading the hub as a preventative measure. Check with your dealer for any applicable service actions.
