BMW Glow Plug Light

The glow plug light on your BMW diesel is a dashboard warning that usually appears during cold starts. It can be a normal pre‑heating indicator, but if it stays on or flashes, it means the glow plug system or engine management has detected a fault. This guide covers the BMW-specific causes, costs, and whether you can drive.

The glow plug light on a BMW diesel is most commonly a normal pre‑heating indicator that turns off after a few seconds. However, if it stays on, flashes, or appears while driving, it signals a fault in the glow plug system, a failed glow plug, or a related engine sensor issue. The colour and behaviour determine the urgency.

  • Normal operation: Glow plug light on for 1–3 seconds during cold start — no action needed.

What the Glow Plug Light Means on a BMW

The glow plug light — a coiled yellow symbol — is the diesel‑specific pre‑heating indicator. When you turn the ignition on, the ECU activates the glow plugs to heat the combustion chambers, making cold starts easier. On a healthy BMW diesel, this light stays on for 1–3 seconds and then goes out.

If the light stays on longer, flashes, or comes on while the engine is already running, it indicates a fault. This could be a failed glow plug, a faulty glow plug relay, a wiring issue, or a problem with the engine management system that affects the glow plug circuit. On modern BMWs, it can also be triggered by DPF pressure sensor faults or EGR issues, especially on N47, B47, and N57 engines.

For engine‑specific fault patterns, see our N47, B47, and N57 Knowledge Centres.

Normal Operation vs Fault

The glow plug light is normal during cold starts, but you must know when it becomes a warning. Here’s how to tell the difference.

✅ Normal

  • Comes on briefly (1–3s) on ignition
  • Turns off before you start the engine
  • Does not appear while driving
  • No loss of power or rough running

❌ Fault

  • Stays on after ignition or while driving
  • Flashing light
  • Appears with engine management light
  • Difficult cold starts or smoke

If the glow plug light is flashing, it’s a clear indication of a fault that needs immediate attention. Do not ignore it — it often accompanies the engine management light and can lead to DPF or turbo damage. Our diagnostic wizard can help you confirm the urgency.

Can You Drive With the Glow Plug Light On?

Brief glow on cold start: yes — that’s normal. If the light goes out before you start the engine, there is no issue.

Stays on or flashing: do not drive. A glow plug light that stays on or flashes indicates a fault that can affect engine performance, emissions, and the DPF. Continued driving can lead to DPF blockage, turbo damage, or a non‑starting condition. If the light is accompanied by a loss of power or rough running, stop and recover the vehicle.

If the glow plug light appears alongside the engine management light or DPF warning light, treat it as more urgent — see our limp mode guide for further steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the glow plug light mean on a BMW?

It means the glow plug system is active during cold start, or if it stays on/flashes, there is a fault in the glow plug circuit or engine management. It’s most common on BMW diesel engines like N47, B47, and N57.

Can I drive with the glow plug light on?

If it’s the normal brief glow on cold start, yes. If it stays on or flashes, no — you risk DPF damage, poor performance, and even a non‑starting condition. Get it checked immediately.

How much does it cost to fix a glow plug light?

A single glow plug replacement is £80–£200. A full set is £150–£400. A control module or relay can cost £200–£600. The exact cost depends on the cause and your engine model.

Can a faulty glow plug cause my BMW not to start?

Yes — if the glow plugs are completely failed, especially in cold weather, the engine may struggle to start or may not start at all. If the light stays on and you have a hard start, it’s time to replace them.

Why does my glow plug light come on while driving?

This is not normal. It usually indicates a fault in the glow plug system (e.g., relay, wiring) or a sensor issue that triggers the warning. It can also be triggered by DPF pressure sensor or EGR faults. Stop and get a diagnosis.
