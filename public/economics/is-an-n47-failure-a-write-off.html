The N47 Failure Landscape

The N47 is BMW’s 2.0‑litre four‑cylinder diesel, fitted to countless 1 Series, 3 Series, X1, X3, and 5 Series models from 2007 to 2019. Its Achilles’ heel is the rear‑mounted timing chain – a design that makes replacement labour‑intensive and expensive. If the chain stretches or the tensioner fails, it can skip teeth, bend valves, and destroy the engine.

The N47 was the most‑enquired engine on our platform in 2025, with 1,450 quote requests [EM‑VERIFIED]. That’s more than any other BMW engine, reflecting both the size of the fleet and the frequency of failure. But a “failure” isn’t always a total engine death. Many owners face a chain replacement costing £1,200–£2,500 – a repair that’s often worth doing.

The real question is: when does the repair bill exceed the car’s worth? That’s where the 70% rule comes in. This page breaks down every scenario so you can decide with confidence.

The 70% Rule Applied to N47 Cars

We use the BMW‑specific 70% repair‑to‑value threshold. If a repair costs more than 70% of your car’s current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts.

Here’s how it works for the most common N47‑powered models, using typical 2025 UK values [THIRD-PARTY].

Example 1: 2008 E90 320d – Value £4,000

Repair: Timing chain replacement – ~£2,000

Ratio: £2,000 ÷ £4,000 = 50% → ✅ Safe (borderline but under 70%)

But if the same car needs a full engine replacement at £7,000:

Ratio: £7,000 ÷ £4,000 = 175% → 🔴 Scrap

Example 2: 2013 F30 320d – Value £9,000

Full engine replacement: £7,000

Ratio: £7,000 ÷ £9,000 = 78% → ⚠️ Watch (just over 70%)

At £10,000 value, the ratio drops to 70% – borderline. At £11,000, it’s 64% → ✅ Safe.

The verdict shifts with just a few hundred pounds in valuation – always check your own figure.

When the Honest Answer Is No – Scrap It

Sometimes the best financial decision is to walk away. Here are the N47 scenarios where we recommend scrapping.

🔴

E90 / E91 320d with Full Engine Failure

Typical value £2,500–£5,000. Recon engine + fitting often exceeds £6,000 – you’ll spend more than the car is worth. Unless it’s a rare manual or low‑mileage example, scrap it.

🔴

Combined repair bill of £3,000+ on a car worth £2,000–£4,000 pushes the ratio well above 70%. Sell for parts and put the money towards a newer BMW.

Even a £2,000 chain repair is 50% – but if anything more serious (turbo, engine) fails, you’re quickly over 70%. Consider your long‑term plan.

Frequently Asked Questions – N47 Write‑Off

Is an N47 engine failure always a write‑off?

No. It depends on the repair cost and your car’s value. A timing chain replacement is often worth it. A full engine replacement on a car under £9,000 is typically a write‑off.

What is the most common N47 failure that causes a write‑off?

Catastrophic timing chain failure – if the chain snaps, it bends valves and often requires a full replacement engine. This is the most expensive repair.

How much does a used N47 engine cost in the UK?

A used N47 engine supply‑only typically costs £1,800–£3,200, plus fitting (£650–£910). A reconditioned engine is £3,200–£5,500, plus fitting. Total fitted cost often exceeds £4,500–£6,500 [EM‑QUOTE].

Does the 70% rule apply to newer F30 N47 cars?

Yes, but the higher values of F30s (often over £7,000) mean that even a full engine replacement may still be safe (ratio under 70%). Always check your specific car’s value.
