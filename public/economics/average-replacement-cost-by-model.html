How to Use This Cost Data

These are not fixed quotes — they are typical ranges from our 2025 database. Here's what they mean for your decision.

1

Supply‑and‑Fit vs Supply‑Only

All costs above are supply‑and‑fit (reconditioned engine fitted by an independent specialist). Supply‑only is typically £1,000–£2,000 lower, but you'll need to arrange fitting separately.

2

Reconditioned vs Used

We show reconditioned costs because they are the most commonly requested condition (70% of enquiries). Used engines are cheaper but come without a warranty and with unknown history.

3

Verdict Flags

✅ Safe means the repair cost is well within the car's value. ⚠️ Watch means it's borderline — check your valuation. 🔴 Scrap means the repair is likely to exceed the car's worth; consider scrapping.

The 70% Rule Applied to These Models

The BMW threshold is 70% — meaning if a repair costs more than 70% of your car's current market value, we recommend scrapping or selling for parts.

Example: 2014 3 Series F30 (N47) – Value £6,000

Recon supply‑and‑fit cost: £4,500 (mid‑range).

Repair‑to‑value ratio: £4,500 ÷ £6,000 = 75% → exceeds 70% → 🔴 Scrap Threshold

If the same car were worth £8,000: ratio = 56% → ✅ Safe. The verdict depends heavily on your car's specific value.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest BMW model to replace an engine on?

In our 2025 data, the 3 Series G20 with the B47 engine typically has the lowest recon supply‑and‑fit cost at around £3,800–£5,500, but it's also a relatively new car, so the repair is well within its value. For older cars, the 1 Series F20 with B47 is similarly affordable.

Why is the N63 V8 so expensive to replace?

The N63's hot‑vee design makes labour intensive — many jobs require engine removal or extensive disassembly. Recon units themselves are also more costly due to the complexity of the twin‑turbo V8. In our data, N63 recon supply‑only is £6,000–£10,000, and fitting adds another £1,000–£1,500.

Is it worth putting a reconditioned engine in an E90 3 Series?

Usually no. Most E90 diesels are worth under £4,000, while a recon engine supply‑and‑fit is £3,800–£6,400. That puts the repair‑to‑value ratio well above 70% — you'd be spending more than the car is worth. Exceptions exist for low‑mileage or cherished examples, but they are rare.
