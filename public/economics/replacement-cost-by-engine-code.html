How to Use This Cost Data

These costs are a starting point. The final price depends on your chosen supplier, your car's platform, and the condition of the engine.

1

Supply‑Only vs Fitted

All figures are for the engine alone. Labour adds £650–£2,160 depending on hours and rate. Always ask for a supply‑and‑fit quote to get the full picture.

2

Condition Matters

Used – cheapest, but unknown history. Reconditioned – stripped, cleaned, worn parts replaced – the most popular option (70% of our enquiries). Rebuilt – full rebuild with new pistons, bearings, etc. – closest to new.

3

Warranty & Trust

Reconditioned and rebuilt engines usually include a 12‑24 month warranty. Used engines may come with 30 days or none. Always verify the warranty terms before purchase.

The 70% Rule Applied to These Costs

The replacement cost is only half the decision. The other half is your car's current market value. We use a 70% repair‑to‑value threshold – if the fitted cost exceeds 70% of your car's value, the honest verdict is usually to scrap it.

Let's apply it to a few common scenarios using real costs from the table above.

Example 1: 2012 F30 320d (N47D20C) – Value £7,500

Reconditioned supply: £4,500 (mid-range). Labour: 12 hrs × £75 = £900. Total fitted: £5,400.

Ratio: £5,400 ÷ £7,500 = 72% – ⚠️ Watch (slightly over 70%)

If the car were worth £8,500, the ratio drops to 64% – ✅ Safe.

Example 2: 2015 F10 530d (N57D30) – Value £9,000

Reconditioned supply: £5,750. Labour: 14 hrs × £80 = £1,120. Total fitted: £6,870.

Ratio: £6,870 ÷ £9,000 = 76% – 🔴 Scrap (over 70%)

At £11,000 value, ratio = 62% – ✅ Safe.

Example 3: 2018 G30 540i (B58B30) – Value £20,000

Reconditioned supply: £4,900. Labour: 12 hrs × £75 = £900. Total fitted: £5,800.

Ratio: £5,800 ÷ £20,000 = 29% – ✅ Safe

Always check your car's current trade value (e.g., Auto Trader, Parkers) and get a firm supply‑and‑fit quote. Our calculator can do this for you.

Cost Analysis: Which Engines Are Most Expensive?

Unsurprisingly, the V8s – S63 and N63 – are the most expensive to replace, often exceeding £10,000 supply. The 4‑cylinder N47 is the most requested and one of the cheapest to replace, but its popularity is driven by high failure rates, not low cost.

  • Most expensive: S63B44 – up to £14,000 supply; with labour can exceed £18,000.
  • Most requested: N47D20C – 1,450 quotes – a reflection of both a large vehicle parc and a known weakness.
  • Best value: B47D20A – newer, more reliable, and cheaper than N47 for a reconditioned unit (£2,800–£4,500).
  • Labour intensity: V8s take up to 24 hours; 4‑cylinders take 10‑14 hours – a significant cost difference.

Frequently Asked Questions – Replacement Costs

Which BMW engine is cheapest to replace?

The N47D20C is the most affordable reconditioned unit at £3,200–£5,500 supply, but its high failure rate makes it a common replacement. The B47 is slightly cheaper and more reliable.

Why is the S63 engine so expensive?

The S63 is a high‑performance twin‑turbo V8 with complex hot‑vee architecture, expensive internals, and low production volumes – all of which drive up the cost of replacement.

Are these prices from main dealers or independents?

These are typical independent specialist supply prices. Main dealers typically add 30‑50% to supply costs and charge higher labour rates.

How much does fitting add to the cost?

Labour ranges from 10–24 hours. At indie rates of £65–£90/hr, fitting adds £650–£2,160. Main dealer labour adds 40‑60% more.
