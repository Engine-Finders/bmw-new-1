What Is Scrap Value?

Scrap value is the amount you can realistically expect to receive for your BMW if you sell it to a breakers' yard, salvage company, or specialist buyer in its current non‑running condition. It's not the same as trade‑in value for a working car – it's what you'd get if the car can't be driven away.

Scrap value is determined by several factors:

  • The car's weight and metal content – heavier cars (X5, 7 Series) generally have higher scrap values due to more scrap metal.
  • Engine type and desirability – V8s, M‑engines, and rare units can be worth more to specialist breakers who sell parts.
  • Other saleable parts – gearbox, interior, wheels, and body panels can all add value.
  • Current scrap metal prices – fluctuating commodity prices affect the baseline.

Across our 9,000+ 2025 UK enquiries [EM‑VERIFIED], we've seen scrap values for non‑running BMWs range from £200 for a basic 1 Series diesel to £1,000+ for a V8 X5 or 7 Series, depending on condition and breakers' demand.

For the most accurate figure, call a few local breakers or use online scrap value calculators – but always get a written quote. The worked example below shows how to apply this to your decision.

The 70% Rule – Why It Decides Scrap vs Repair

Our 70% repair‑to‑value threshold is the single most important number in your decision. If the fitted cost of a repair or replacement engine is more than 70% of your BMW's current trade value, the honest financial verdict is to scrap it.

Why 70%? This figure comes directly from our proprietary data. Below 70%, owners who repair are substantially more likely to report satisfaction with their decision a year later. Above it, the split shifts sharply toward regret – the car is worth too little to justify the spend.

The 70% rule applies to all BMWs on this site, but it's especially critical for:

  • E‑series cars (E90, E60, E70, E87) – values have dropped, and repairs often exceed the threshold.
  • High‑mileage F‑series (F30, F10) – chain failures on N47/N57 engines can cost £4,000–£7,000 on cars worth £6,000–£9,000 – often over 70%.
  • V8s with N63/S63 failures – repair costs can exceed £10,000 on cars worth £10,000–£15,000.

The 70% rule is a guide, not a hard law. If your car has sentimental value, a new MOT, or a particularly desirable spec, you might choose to exceed it. But for most owners, it's the right answer. For a full breakdown of how we apply the rule, see our Repair vs Replace vs Scrap Framework.

How to Calculate Scrap Value and Make the Decision

Here's a simple three‑step process to work out whether scrapping is the right choice for your BMW.

1

Get Your Car's Trade Value

Check your car's current market value using tools like Auto Trader, Parkers, or Glasses Guide. Be honest – use the trade value, not what you think it's worth, and adjust for mileage and condition. This is the figure you'll use in the ratio calculation.

2

Get a Supply‑and‑Fit Quote

Get a fixed‑price quote from a specialist for the repair or replacement engine – including all parts, labour, fluids, and sundries. Use our registration lookup to get quotes from vetted specialists.

3

Apply the 70% Rule

Divide the fitted repair cost by your car's trade value. If the result is under 70%, repair it. If it's over 70%, scrap it and use the scrap value towards a replacement car. This simple ratio is our most‑used tool across all economics pages.

You can also get an independent scrap quote from a breaker or use our calculator to automate the process.

The Calculation

Repair‑to‑value ratio: £5,400 ÷ £7,500 = 72%

Threshold: 70%

Result: ⚠️ Watch zone – just over the threshold

If the car were worth £8,500, the ratio drops to 64% – ✅ Safe. If it were worth £6,500, the ratio jumps to 83% – 🔴 Scrap. This is why the valuation matters as much as the repair cost.

Scrap value estimate: £350–£500 from a breaker, depending on parts demand [PATTERN DATA]

Verdict

At £7,500 trade value, this car is on the cusp. If the owner is planning to keep it for 3+ years and the rest of the car is excellent, it might be worth stretching. If there are other issues (gearbox, suspension, rust), the honest advice is to take the scrap value and move on.

Frequently Asked Questions – Scrap Value

How much scrap value can I expect for my BMW?

Scrap values for non‑running BMWs typically range from £200–£1,000, depending on the model, weight, and desirability of parts. Heavier cars (X5, 7 Series) and V8/M‑cars tend to fetch more. Always get a quote from a few breakers.

What is the 70% rule and why does it matter?

The 70% rule is our repair‑to‑value threshold: if a fitted repair costs more than 70% of your car's trade value, scrapping is usually the better financial decision. It's based on real UK enquiry data and is used across all BMW economics pages on this site.

Can I scrap a BMW with a failed engine?

Yes. Many breakers and salvage companies will collect a non‑running BMW. You can get a quote online or by phone – they'll assess the car's value based on its weight, engine type, and saleable parts.

Is it ever worth repairing a car over 70%?

Yes – if the car has sentimental value, a recent MOT, or a rare/desirable spec. The 70% rule is a guide, not an absolute law. But for most owners, exceeding 70% leads to financial regret within a year.

Where can I get an accurate scrap value quote?
