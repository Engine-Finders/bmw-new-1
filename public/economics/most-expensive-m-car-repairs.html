Breakdown of Each Expensive M Car Repair

What makes these repairs so costly? Here’s the detail behind the numbers, including why each is expensive and what owners typically face.

S63B44 Engine Replacement – £8,000–£15,000 supply

The S63 is BMW’s high‑performance twin‑turbo V8 used in M5, M6, X5M and X6M. It suffers from similar hot‑vee heat issues as the N63, but with even higher performance parts. Failures often involve timing chain tensioner wear, oil pump issues, or catastrophic bearing failure [PATTERN DATA]. A full reconditioned engine is the only option – rebuilds are rarely attempted due to complexity. With fitting, total cost can reach £18,000, making it a write‑off on all but the newest, highest‑value M cars. The S63 generated 410 quote requests in 2025 [EM‑VERIFIED], indicating high demand but also high cost.

Tip: Always check full service history – these engines demand strict oil change intervals. See our S63 rod bearing failure page for more.

N63B44 Engine Replacement – £6,000–£10,000 supply

The N63 is the hot‑vee V8 found in the M550i, X5 M50i, and other M Performance models. It shares many characteristics with the S63 but is less extreme. It suffers from valve stem seal failure, turbo coolant line leaks, and battery drain from the auxiliary coolant pump [PATTERN DATA]. Valve seal repair alone costs £3,000–£6,000, but if ignored, a full replacement becomes necessary. With 440 quotes in 2025 [EM‑VERIFIED], this is a common but expensive issue. Fitted cost can exceed £12,500 – often more than the value of an older M550i.

For more detail, see our N63 oil consumption failure page.

Rod Bearing Replacement (S65/S85) – £3,000–£6,000

The S65 V8 (E9x M3) and S85 V10 (E60 M5) are iconic engines but notorious for rod bearing wear. The bearings are a known weak point and can fail catastrophically if not replaced preventatively. The repair involves replacing the bearings, often with upgraded aftermarket units, and requires engine removal. Labour is intensive, hence the high cost. Many owners consider it a "must‑do" maintenance item at around 60k–80k miles.

S55 Crank Hub Failure – £2,500–£5,000

The S55 engine in the F8x M3/M4 is a twin‑turbo inline‑six. One of its few weak points is the crank hub, which can spin or sheer, causing catastrophic timing issues. The repair requires significant disassembly and is labour‑intensive. Upgraded crank hub solutions are available, but the cost remains high. It's a well‑known issue and a common query among M car owners.

S63 Timing Chain Tensioner – £2,500–£5,000

The S63's timing chain tensioner can wear, leading to chain slack and potential engine damage. Replacing the tensioner and guides is a complex job due to the engine's layout. If caught early, it can prevent a full engine replacement, but the cost is still significant.

The 70% Rule – How It Applies to M Cars

Our 70% repair‑to‑value threshold is the key to deciding whether an expensive repair is worth it. The total fitted cost must be less than 70% of your car’s current market value; otherwise, scrap it.

Let’s apply it to a few common M car scenarios:

Example 1: 2010 E60 M5 (S85) – Value £12,000

Rod bearing replacement: £5,000 fitted

Ratio: £5,000 ÷ £12,000 = 42% – ✅ Safe (under 70%)

Worth it, as the car is still valuable and the repair is known preventative maintenance.

Example 2: 2011 F10 M5 (S63) – Value £15,000

S63 engine replacement: £14,000 fitted

Ratio: £14,000 ÷ £15,000 = 93% – 🔴 Scrap (over 70%)

Clearly not worth it – the repair exceeds the car’s value.

Example 3: 2018 F90 M5 (S63) – Value £45,000

S63 timing chain tensioner repair: £4,500 fitted

Ratio: £4,500 ÷ £45,000 = 10% – ✅ Safe

Well under 70%, so the repair is worth it.

Always get a full supply‑and‑fit quote and check your car’s current trade value before deciding. Our calculator can help.

Which Expensive M Car Repairs Are Worth It?

Not all expensive repairs are write‑offs. The value of the car and the nature of the repair matter.

✅

Rod Bearing on S65/S85 (Preventative)

If caught early or done as preventative maintenance on a well‑maintained M3/M5, it's worth it – the repair cost is a fraction of the car's value, and it prevents catastrophic failure.

✅

S55 Crank Hub on High‑Value F8x

On a £20,000+ M3/M4, the repair cost is around 15‑25% – well worth it to preserve the car's performance and value.

⚠️

S63 Engine Replacement on Older Models

On F10 M5 or E70 X5M, the cost often exceeds 70% – only consider if the car is exceptionally low‑mileage or has significant sentimental value.

🔴

N63 Engine Replacement on any E‑series

The cost of a full N63 replacement on an older M550i or similar almost always exceeds 70% – these are almost always write‑offs.

When to Walk Away – The Honest Verdict

Sometimes the most expensive repair is the one you choose to do. Here are the M car scenarios where we strongly recommend scrapping or selling for parts.

  • F10 M5 with S63 engine failure: A reconditioned engine supply is £8,000–£15,000, plus fitting, on a car worth under £15,000 – the ratio is over 70%.
  • E60 M5 with rod bearing failure (if engine is already damaged): If the engine has seized, a full replacement is needed – cost exceeds £10,000 on a car worth maybe £10,000 – borderline, but if there are other issues, it's not worth it.
  • E70 X5M with N63 or S63 failure: Values are low (£5,000–£10,000), and repair costs often exceed that – scrap.
  • Any high‑mileage M car with major engine damage: The cost of repair almost always exceeds the vehicle's residual value.

Frequently Asked Questions – M Car Repairs

What is the most expensive M car repair?

The S63 engine replacement is the most expensive, at £8,000–£15,000 supply, and with fitting can exceed £18,000. The N63 engine replacement is second, at £6,000–£10,000 supply.

Is it worth fixing an S63 engine on an F10 M5?

It depends on the car's value. On an F10 M5 worth under £15,000, the repair cost often exceeds 70% – not worth it. On a low‑mileage example or a newer F90, it may be worth it.

How much does rod bearing replacement cost on an E90 M3?

Typically £3,000–£6,000 supply and labour, depending on the shop. It's a well‑known preventative maintenance item.

Should I scrap my M car if the engine fails?

Apply the 70% rule: if the fitted repair cost exceeds 70% of your car's current trade value, scrapping is usually the better financial decision. See our framework page for a worked example.
