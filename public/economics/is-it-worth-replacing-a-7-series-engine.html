7 Series Generations and Engine Options

The BMW 7 Series has used a range of engines across its generations. The most common are the N57 six‑cylinder diesel (F01, early G11), N63 V8 petrol (F01, G11), B57 six‑cylinder diesel (G11/G12), B58 petrol (G11/G12), and the B47 diesel (G11/G12). Each has a different cost profile and write‑off risk.

The N47 is the most‑enquired engine on our platform, with 1,450 quote requests in 2025 [EM‑VERIFIED]. It was fitted to some early F01 7 Series models. Its Achilles' heel is the rear‑mounted timing chain – a repair that costs £1,200–£2,500 but can escalate to a full engine replacement if ignored.

The N57 six‑cylinder diesel is found in the 730d and 740d models. It has its own cost profile – higher repair bills but also higher values. It generated 830 quote requests [EM‑VERIFIED] in 2025.

The N63 V8 petrol is found in the 750i and 760i models. It is known for high running costs and expensive repairs, including valve stem seals and turbo coolant lines. It generated 440 quote requests [EM‑VERIFIED] in 2025.

The B57 and B58 are the latest generation engines, found in the G11/G12 7 Series. They are generally reliable, with fewer catastrophic failure modes than the N‑series engines.

The key question: does your car's value justify the repair? That's where the 70% rule comes in.

The 70% Rule Applied to 7 Series Models

We use the BMW‑specific 70% repair‑to‑value threshold. If a repair costs more than 70% of your car's current market value, the honest verdict is to scrap or sell for parts.

Here's how it works for the most common 7 Series generations, using typical 2025 UK values [THIRD-PARTY].

Example 1: 2010 F01 730d (N57) – Value £8,000

Repair: Timing chain replacement – ~£3,000

Ratio: £3,000 ÷ £8,000 = 37% → ✅ Safe

But if the same car needs a full engine replacement at £8,500:

Ratio: £8,500 ÷ £8,000 = 106% → 🔴 Scrap

Example 2: 2015 G11 730d (B57) – Value £22,000

Full engine replacement: £6,500

Ratio: £6,500 ÷ £22,000 = 30% → ✅ Safe

Even at £12,000 value, the ratio is 54% → ✅ Safe.

Example 3: 2012 F01 750i (N63) – Value £10,000

Valve stem seal repair: £4,000

Ratio: £4,000 ÷ £10,000 = 40% → ✅ Safe (just)

But if the car needs a full engine replacement at £10,000:

Ratio: £10,000 ÷ £10,000 = 100% → 🔴 Scrap

Frequently Asked Questions – 7 Series Write‑Off

Is it worth replacing an engine in a 7 Series?

It depends on the generation and engine. For a G11/G12 with a B57, B58, or B47 engine, yes – the car's value easily justifies it. For an F01 with an N57 or N63, a full engine replacement is often a write‑off, but a timing chain repair is usually worth it.

What is the most common 7 Series engine failure?

The N63 valve stem seal failure is the most common and most expensive V8 repair. It's the main reason 7 Series N63 enquiries are so high – 440 quote requests for the N63 in 2025 [EM‑VERIFIED].

How much does a used 7 Series engine cost?

A used N57 supply‑only costs £2,500–£4,500, plus fitting (£780–£1,040). A reconditioned N63 is £6,000–£10,000, plus fitting. Total fitted cost often exceeds £7,000–£11,000 [EM‑QUOTE].

Is the B57 engine in the G11 more reliable than the N57?

Yes. The B57 is a modern design with a front‑mounted timing chain and fewer known failure points. That's why G11/G12 7 Series models are almost always worth repairing.
