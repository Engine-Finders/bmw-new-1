What Do Reconditioned, Used, and Rebuilt Mean?

These terms are often used interchangeably, but they mean very different things – and the price and reliability vary accordingly.

🔧

Reconditioned

A reconditioned engine is a used engine that has been stripped, cleaned, inspected, and had worn parts replaced (e.g., bearings, rings, gaskets) to restore it to a "good as new" standard. It does not usually include a full rebuild of every component, but it's tested and comes with a warranty. This is the most popular option – 70.45% of our 2025 enquiries [EM‑VERIFIED].

🔄

Used

A used engine is taken from a donor vehicle and sold as-is, with minimal cleaning. It may have been tested for compression and checked for obvious faults, but there is no guarantee of internal condition. It's the cheapest option, but also the riskiest – you could be buying someone else's problems. Typically supplied with a 30‑day warranty at best.

🏗️

Rebuilt

A rebuilt engine is a full strip-down and rebuild with new parts – often including pistons, liners, crankshaft grinding, and upgraded components. It's the closest you can get to a brand-new engine without buying from BMW. It's the most expensive option, but offers the highest reliability and often a longer warranty.

The choice isn't just about price – it's about the age and value of your car, your planned ownership period, and your tolerance for risk.

Pros and Cons of Each Option

Here's a quick summary to help you weigh the trade-offs.

✅ Reconditioned

  • ✔️ Best balance of cost and reliability
  • ✔️ Typically 12‑24 months warranty
  • ✔️ Tested and inspected
  • ❌ Can still have underlying wear
  • ❌ Not as thorough as a full rebuild

💰 Used

  • ✔️ Cheapest option
  • ✔️ Quick availability
  • ❌ Unknown history – big risk
  • ❌ Limited or no warranty
  • ❌ May fail soon after fitting

🏗️ Rebuilt

  • ✔️ Highest quality – like new
  • ✔️ Upgraded parts possible
  • ✔️ Long warranty (2‑3 years common)
  • ❌ Most expensive
  • ❌ Longer lead time

Which Should You Choose?

The right choice depends on your car's value, your budget, and your plans.

🔰

High‑Value / Modern Car

If your car is worth over £15,000 and you plan to keep it for 3+ years, a rebuilt engine is the best investment – it preserves value and gives you peace of mind. A reconditioned unit is also a good choice.

⚠️

Mid‑Value Car (£5k–£15k)

A reconditioned engine is usually the sweet spot – it's affordable, reliable, and comes with a decent warranty. Used engines are risky; only consider if budget is extremely tight and you can inspect the donor car.

💸

Low‑Value Car (under £5k)

Even a used engine might cost more than 70% of the car's value – see the 70% rule. Often the best choice is to scrap the car and buy something else, unless it has sentimental value. If you must repair, a used engine is the only financially viable option.

For a full decision framework, see our Repair vs Replace vs Scrap guide, and remember to apply the 70% rule – if the fitted cost exceeds 70% of the car's trade value, walk away.

When to Walk Away – The Honest Verdict

Sometimes the best choice is not to replace the engine at all. Here's when to say no.

  • If the car is worth under £4,000 – even a used engine with fitting often exceeds 70% of value. Scrap it.
  • If the car has other major issues – rust, gearbox, suspension – stacking repairs is rarely worth it.
  • If you only need the car for a short time – a used engine might be a gamble, but a reconditioned one is overkill.
  • If you can't get a decent warranty – avoid used engines with no warranty; the risk is too high.

Frequently Asked Questions – Engine Condition

Which is better: reconditioned or rebuilt?

It depends on your budget and car value. Rebuilt is better in terms of quality and longevity, but it's significantly more expensive. Reconditioned offers a good balance and is the most popular choice in our data – 70.45% of enquiries.

Is a used BMW engine worth buying?

Used engines are risky but can be cost-effective if the donor car has low mileage and a known history. Always inspect it personally if possible, and insist on a compression test and a warranty (even 30 days). For high‑value cars, we recommend reconditioned or rebuilt instead.

What's the typical warranty on a reconditioned engine?

Most reconditioned engines come with a 12‑24 month warranty, depending on the supplier. Rebuilt engines often offer 2‑3 years. Used engines may have no warranty or a very limited 30‑day cover.

How much does it cost to fit an engine?

Labour rates vary: indie specialists £65–£90/hr, main dealers £120–£160/hr. Fitting time is typically 10–24 hours depending on engine and vehicle. Use our calculator to estimate total fitted cost.
