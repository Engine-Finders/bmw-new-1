What Does "Supply and Fit" Actually Mean?

In the engine replacement world, "supply and fit" is the all‑in price for the complete job – but not all quotes include the same things.

Supply refers to the engine itself: a used, reconditioned, or rebuilt unit supplied by the specialist. This is the core cost and varies widely by engine code and condition. For example, an N47D20C reconditioned supply is £3,200–£5,500, while an S63B44 reconditioned supply is £9,000–£14,000 [EM‑QUOTE]. The supply price typically includes a warranty (12‑24 months for reconditioned units, less for used).

Fit is the labour and sundries needed to remove the old engine, prepare the bay, and install the replacement. This includes the specialist's time, but also things like engine oil, coolant, filters, and any necessary gaskets or seals. It does not usually include the cost of repairing any ancillary parts that are found to be worn (e.g., clutch, turbo, injectors) – though a good specialist will warn you about these during the quote.

Always ask for a fixed‑price supply‑and‑fit quote that lists exactly what is included. A quote that says "supply and fit" without detail is a red flag – you need to know what's covered and what's not.

Hidden Costs and What to Ask Your Specialist

A supply‑and‑fit quote often hides the details. Here are the common extras and the questions to ask.

  • Oil and coolant – are they included? Some specialists add them as extras. Typically £100‑£200.
  • Filters and gaskets – new oil filter, air filter, and gaskets are often charged separately. Ask for a complete list.
  • Ancillary parts – if the old engine has damaged ancillaries (turbo, injectors, clutch), they may need replacement. A good specialist will inspect and warn you.
  • Diagnostic work – some specialists charge for diagnosis and coding after the fit. This can add £100‑£300.
  • Warranty – is it included? Reconditioned engines usually come with 12‑24 months; used engines may have little or none.

Questions to ask before committing:

  • "Is this a fixed‑price quote, or are there potential extras?"
  • "What's included in the labour – oil, coolant, filters, gaskets?"
  • "Do you inspect and replace ancillaries if needed?"
  • "What warranty do you offer on the supply and the fit?"
  • "Is diagnostic and coding work included?"

The 70% Rule – Why Fitted Cost Matters

Our 70% repair‑to‑value threshold is based on the total fitted cost, not just the supply price. Always include labour and sundries in the ratio.

Example: 2012 F30 320d (N47D20C) – Value £7,500

Supply: £4,500 (mid‑range). Labour: 12 hrs × £75 = £900. Sundries: £200. Total fitted: £5,600.

Ratio: £5,600 ÷ £7,500 = 75% – 🔴 Scrap (over 70%)

If the car were worth £9,000, the ratio drops to 62% – ✅ Safe. This is why the total fitted cost matters.

Frequently Asked Questions – Supply and Fit

What does "supply and fit" include?

It typically includes the engine itself (supply) plus the labour and sundries to remove the old engine and install the replacement. Sundries often include oil, coolant, filters, and gaskets. Always ask for a detailed breakdown.

How much does fitting a BMW engine cost?

Labour alone ranges from £650–£2,160 depending on hours and rate. Total fitted cost depends on the engine supply price. For a 4‑cylinder N47, total fitted is £4,100–£6,800; for a V8 S63, it's £10,650–£16,300 [EM‑QUOTE].

What hidden costs should I watch for?

Common extras include oil and coolant, filters and gaskets, ancillary parts (turbo, injectors), diagnostic work, and coding. Always get a fixed‑price quote that lists everything.

Is the 70% rule based on supply or fitted cost?

The fitted cost. The 70% rule compares the total cost of the job (supply + labour + sundries) to your car's current trade value. Labour can push a borderline case over the threshold.
