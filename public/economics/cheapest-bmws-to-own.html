How We Define "Cheapest to Own"

We don't just look at the purchase price. Our ranking is based on the true cost of keeping a BMW on the road — focusing on engine replacement viability, parts costs, labour hours, and the 70% rule.

1

Engine Replacement Cost

We use actual 2025 UK supplier quotes for reconditioned engines, including supply and independent fitting. This is the biggest single expense an owner may face, so it dominates the ranking.

2

Vehicle Value Retention

A car with strong residual values will have a lower repair‑to‑value ratio, making repairs more viable. We use current UK trade values [THIRD-PARTY] to assess this.

3

The 70% Threshold

We apply our 70% repair‑to‑value rule: if a recon engine costs more than 70% of the car's value, we consider it a scrap candidate. Models that stay safely below this line rank higher.

4

Known Reliability Patterns

We cross‑reference with known failure patterns (e.g., N47 timing chain, N63 valve seals) to adjust the ranking — a model with a catastrophic failure risk may be more expensive to own long‑term.

Verdict: The Cheapest BMW to Own

After analysing all data, the BMW 3 Series G20 with the B47 diesel engine takes the top spot. It combines low replacement costs, excellent residual values, and a modern reliable engine.

But if you're on a tighter budget, the 1 Series F40 is a very close second, offering the same engine in a smaller, slightly cheaper package. For SUV buyers, the X1 F48 is the most cost‑effective choice.

At the other end of the scale, avoid older N47‑powered E90/E87 models unless you're prepared for a potentially uneconomical repair. The 70% rule clearly shows they are often better scrapped than repaired.

Remember: these are averages. Your specific car's condition, mileage, and service history can shift the verdict. Always get a professional inspection and quote before making a final decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which BMW model has the cheapest engine replacement cost?

In our 2025 data, the BMW 1 Series F40 and 3 Series G20 with the B47 engine have the lowest recon supply‑and‑fit costs at around £3,800–£5,500. The B47 is cheaper to replace than the older N47 due to better parts availability and a more accessible chain design.

Is a BMW with a B47 engine always cheaper to own than an N47?

Generally, yes. The B47 is more reliable (fewer timing chain issues) and cheaper to replace because the chain is front‑mounted, reducing labour time. However, the overall cost also depends on the car's value — a high‑value B47 car will be safe, while a low‑value N47 car might still be a scrap candidate.

Why is the E90 3 Series so expensive to own?

The E90's low market value (often under £4,000) means that a recon engine costing £3,800–£6,400 exceeds 70% of its worth. Combined with the N47's timing chain issues, it's rarely worth repairing unless the car is a cherished low‑mileage example.

Does the 70% rule apply to M cars and petrol engines the same way?

The rule is constant at 70%, but the numbers differ. M cars (e.g., M3, M4) have higher repair costs but also higher values, so they can still be safe if the ratio stays below 70%. Petrol engines like the B58 are generally cheaper to maintain than V8s like the N63.
