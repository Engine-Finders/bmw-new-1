What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general coolant loss without leak guide covers several possible causes across the whole BMW range, on the N47 the picture narrows considerably. This diesel engine's dominant failure signature is a slow but persistent loss of coolant, requiring top-ups every few weeks or months, with no visible puddles under the car, no weeping hoses, and no obvious radiator leaks. It is distinct from the external leaks of a failed water pump or a split radiator hose. On the N47, coolant loss without visible leaks almost always points to internal consumption — and the most common culprits are a failing EGR cooler, a cracked cylinder head, or a head gasket failure — all of which are known issues on higher-mileage units.

Why This Engine In Particular

The N47's EGR (Exhaust Gas Recirculation) system includes a cooler that uses engine coolant to reduce the temperature of recirculated exhaust gases before they re-enter the intake. Over time, the EGR cooler's internal seals can fail, allowing coolant to leak into the exhaust stream where it is burned off, leaving no external trace. This is the most common cause of coolant loss without visible leaks on the N47. Additionally, the N47's cast-iron block and aluminium cylinder head can develop hairline cracks due to thermal stress, particularly in high-mileage engines. A cracked head or failed head gasket can also allow coolant to enter the combustion chamber, where it is burned as steam — again leaving no visible leak. This is a design characteristic of this particular engine family, and the same symptom on, say, a B47 diesel would point to a very different set of causes and is significantly less common.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. Check the coolant system for pressure — a pressure test can reveal slow external leaks that only occur under pressure. Also inspect the oil filler cap and dipstick for signs of oil in coolant or coolant in oil (a milky residue). If the coolant level is dropping but there's no external leak and the oil looks clean, the most likely culprits are the EGR cooler or the cylinder head. The definitive diagnostic step is to have a BMW specialist perform an EGR cooler test (using a cooling system pressure tester) and a cylinder leak-down test. Do not ignore this symptom; continued driving with an internal coolant leak can lead to overheating, hydro-lock, and catastrophic engine failure.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed immediately if you're losing coolant without visible leaks on your N47. This is a critical warning sign of an internal coolant leak. Early intervention can save you from a much larger bill. If it's an EGR cooler, the repair is straightforward and relatively affordable. If it's a head gasket or cracked cylinder head, the repair is more expensive, but catching it early can prevent catastrophic engine failure. Frequently Asked Questions

Is coolant loss without a visible leak on an N47 always the EGR cooler?

It's the most common cause, but not the only one. A cracked cylinder head, head gasket failure, or a faulty coolant expansion tank cap can also cause coolant loss without visible leaks. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a pressure test and leak-down test.

At what mileage does an N47 typically start losing coolant without visible leaks?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, though EGR cooler failure is more common on higher-mileage engines. Regular cooling system inspections can catch the issue early.

Can I drive an N47 with coolant loss but no visible leak?

It is strongly recommended not to. Driving with an internal coolant leak can lead to overheating, hydro-lock, and catastrophic engine failure. Have it inspected as soon as possible — ideally, don't drive it until a specialist has inspected it.

How much does it cost to fix coolant loss without a visible leak on an N47?

An EGR cooler replacement typically costs £600–£1,200. A head gasket replacement costs £1,500–£2,500. If the cylinder head is cracked, you're looking at £2,500–£4,500. If the engine has suffered catastrophic damage, expect £4,500–£6,500 for a replacement engine.

What is the 70% scrap threshold for BMW?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be financially wiser to scrap or sell the car rather than repair. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.
