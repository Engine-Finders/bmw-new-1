What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general engine knocking noise guide covers several possible causes across the whole BMW range, on the N47 the picture narrows considerably. This diesel engine's dominant failure signature is a persistent knocking or rattling noise from the engine, often most noticeable on cold start, under load, or at idle. The sound can range from a brief rattle to a deep, metallic knock. It is distinct from the clicking of sticking EGR valve or the whine of a failing turbocharger. On the N47, knocking noise is one of the most critical warning signs — it can indicate anything from a failing timing chain tensioner to catastrophic bottom-end bearing failure — and it requires immediate investigation.

Why This Engine In Particular

The N47's timing chain is mounted at the rear of the engine, making it inaccessible without removing the gearbox or the entire engine. When the chain stretches or the tensioner fails, the chain can slap against the guide rails, producing a distinct knocking or rattling noise, especially on cold start. Additionally, the N47's bottom end — specifically the crankshaft bearings — can fail due to oil starvation or high mileage, producing a deep, rhythmic knock that increases with engine speed. This is a design characteristic of this particular engine family, and the same symptom on, say, a B47 diesel — with its front-mounted chain and revised bearing design — would point to a very different set of causes and is significantly less common.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. A failing water pump or turbocharger actuator can sometimes produce a knocking-like noise. However, if the knocking is accompanied by a loss of power, a check engine light, or oil pressure warning, it is almost certainly a serious internal issue. The definitive diagnostic step is to have a BMW specialist perform an oil pressure test, a timing chain stretch measurement, and a stethoscope inspection to locate the source of the noise. Do not ignore this symptom; continued driving with an engine knock can destroy the engine.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed immediately if you hear a knocking noise on your N47. This is one of the most critical warning signs on this engine. Early intervention can save you from a £4,500+ engine replacement. If caught early, a chain and tensioner replacement is a straightforward (if labour-intensive) repair. If the knock is bottom-end bearing related, the repair is more complex but still manageable if caught early. If ignored, the engine will fail catastrophically. Frequently Asked Questions

Is an engine knocking noise on an N47 always the timing chain?

Not always — but it's one of the most common causes. A knocking noise can also indicate bottom-end bearing failure, a failing hydraulic lifter, or a worn injector. A specialist can pinpoint the source with a stethoscope and diagnostic equipment.

At what mileage does an N47 typically start knocking?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, though pre-2011 engines are at higher risk for timing chain issues. Regular oil changes with the correct BMW Longlife-04 spec oil can delay many of these issues.

Can I drive an N47 with an engine knocking noise?

It is strongly recommended not to. Driving with an engine knock risks catastrophic engine failure. Have it inspected as soon as possible — ideally, don't start it again until a specialist has inspected it.

How much does it cost to fix an engine knocking noise on an N47?

A timing chain replacement typically costs £650–£1,800. A bottom-end bearing replacement costs £3,000–£5,000. If the engine has suffered catastrophic damage, you're looking at £4,500–£6,500 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.

What is the 70% scrap threshold for BMW?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be financially wiser to scrap or sell the car rather than repair. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.
