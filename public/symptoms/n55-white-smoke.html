BMW N55 White Smoke

White smoke from the exhaust on the N55 is a critical warning sign. This guide explains exactly what it means on this twin‑scroll turbo inline‑six, the components most likely to be responsible, and what it will cost to put right.

On the BMW N55 specifically, white smoke from the exhaust is most often caused by coolant entering the combustion chamber — typically through a failing head gasket, a cracked cylinder head, or a leaking turbocharger coolant seal. It typically appears between 60,000 and 100,000 miles and, if ignored, can lead to catastrophic engine damage.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general white smoke guide covers several possible causes across the BMW range, on the N55 the picture narrows considerably. This 3.0‑litre twin‑scroll turbo inline‑six produces white smoke from the exhaust when coolant is burned in the combustion chamber. It is distinct from the black smoke of over‑fuelling or the blue smoke of oil burning. White smoke on the N55 almost always points to a head gasket failure, a cracked cylinder head, or a leaking turbocharger coolant seal. It is most noticeable at start‑up, often clearing as the engine warms up, or it can be a persistent cloud under load. On the N55, the most frequent diagnosis among BMW specialists is a head gasket that has failed between a coolant passage and a cylinder, allowing coolant to enter the combustion chamber. Catching it early is critical to prevent overheating and further engine damage.

Why This Engine In Particular

The N55's aluminium block and cylinder head are susceptible to head gasket failure if the engine has been overheated, even once. The head gasket sits between the cylinder head and the engine block, sealing the combustion chambers, oil passages, and coolant passages. When the gasket fails, it can allow coolant to leak into the combustion chamber, where it turns to steam and exits as white smoke. The N55's turbocharger is also water‑cooled, and a failed internal seal can allow coolant to enter the exhaust, producing white smoke. This is less common than a head gasket failure but is a known issue on higher‑mileage N55 units. The same symptom on a B58 (the N55's successor) is less common, as BMW revised the cooling system and head gasket design. On the N55, early diagnosis and repair are essential to prevent a much larger bill. Ignoring white smoke can lead to overheating, cylinder wall damage, and a seized engine.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out simpler possibilities. Condensation in the exhaust system can produce white smoke on a cold start, but it should clear quickly. If the white smoke persists, or if you see it under load, it is likely a coolant leak into the combustion chamber. Check the coolant level—if it is dropping and there is no external leak, the coolant is going into the engine. A combustion leak test (sniff test) will detect exhaust gases in the coolant, confirming a head gasket breach. If the turbocharger is suspected, a specialist can inspect the turbo for coolant leaks. Do not ignore white smoke—it can lead to catastrophic engine failure. If the smoke is accompanied by a sweet smell (coolant), a loss of power, or an overheating engine, stop driving immediately and have the car recovered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is white smoke on an N55 always a head gasket issue?

Not always, but it is the most common cause. A leaking turbocharger coolant seal can also produce white smoke. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a combustion leak test and a pressure test.

At what mileage does an N55 typically produce white smoke?

Most cases appear between 60,000 and 100,000 miles, often after an overheating event. Regular coolant changes and maintaining the cooling system can reduce the risk.

Can I drive my BMW N55 with white smoke?

No. White smoke indicates coolant is entering the combustion chamber. Continued driving can cause overheating and serious engine damage. Have the car recovered to a specialist.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on an N55?

A head gasket replacement typically costs £1,500–£3,000 at an independent specialist. A turbocharger coolant seal repair costs £800–£2,000. If the engine has suffered severe damage, a full rebuild or replacement can cost £4,000–£7,000.

Does the N55 white smoke issue affect all model years?

It is more common on earlier N55 engines (pre‑2013) that have been overheated. Later models (post‑2013) have some revisions, but any N55 with high mileage or a history of overheating remains susceptible.
