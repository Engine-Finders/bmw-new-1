What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general white smoke guide covers several possible causes across the whole BMW range, on the N47 the picture narrows considerably. This diesel engine's dominant failure signature is a thick, white cloud of smoke from the exhaust, often most noticeable on cold start or under acceleration, accompanied by a sweet, syrupy smell (the distinct odour of coolant). It is distinct from the black smoke of over-fuelling (injectors, MAF) and the blue smoke of oil burning (turbo seals, piston rings). On the N47, white smoke almost always points to coolant entering the combustion chamber, and the most common culprits are a failed EGR cooler, a cracked cylinder head, or a blown head gasket — all of which are known issues on higher-mileage units.

Why This Engine In Particular

The N47's EGR (Exhaust Gas Recirculation) system includes a cooler that uses engine coolant to reduce the temperature of recirculated exhaust gases before they re-enter the intake. Over time, the EGR cooler's internal seals can fail, allowing coolant to leak into the exhaust stream where it is vaporised as white smoke. This is the most common cause of white smoke on the N47. Additionally, the N47's aluminium cylinder head can develop hairline cracks due to thermal stress, particularly in high-mileage engines. A cracked head or failed head gasket can also allow coolant to enter the combustion chamber, where it is burned as steam — producing the characteristic white smoke. This is a design characteristic of this particular engine family, and the same symptom on, say, a B47 diesel — with its revised EGR cooler and cylinder head design — would point to a very different set of causes and is significantly less common.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. Check the coolant level — a dropping level often points to a leak. Also check the oil filler cap and dipstick for signs of oil in coolant or coolant in oil (a milky residue). If the white smoke is accompanied by a loss of power, a check engine light, or a coolant temperature warning, it is almost certainly a head gasket or EGR cooler issue. The definitive diagnostic step is to have a BMW specialist perform an EGR cooler test (using a cooling system pressure tester) and a cylinder leak-down test. Do not ignore white smoke; it will only get worse and can lead to catastrophic engine failure.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed immediately if you see white smoke on your N47. This is a critical warning sign of coolant burning. Early intervention can save you from a much larger bill. If it's an EGR cooler, the repair is straightforward and relatively affordable. If it's a head gasket or cracked cylinder head, the repair is more expensive, but catching it early can prevent catastrophic engine failure. Frequently Asked Questions

Is white smoke from an N47 always the EGR cooler?

It's the most common cause, but not the only one. A cracked cylinder head, head gasket failure, or a coolant leak into the combustion chamber can also cause white smoke. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a pressure test and leak-down test.

At what mileage does an N47 typically start producing white smoke?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, though EGR cooler failure is more common on higher-mileage engines. Regular cooling system inspections can catch the issue early.

Can I drive an N47 with white smoke?

It is strongly recommended not to. Driving with white smoke means coolant is being burned, which can lead to overheating, further engine damage, and catastrophic failure. Have it inspected as soon as possible — ideally, don't start it again until a specialist has inspected it.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on an N47?

An EGR cooler replacement costs £600–£1,200. A head gasket replacement costs £1,500–£2,500. If the cylinder head is cracked, you're looking at £2,500–£4,500. If the engine has suffered catastrophic damage, expect £4,500–£6,500 for a replacement engine.

What is the 70% scrap threshold for BMW?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be financially wiser to scrap or sell the car rather than repair. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.
