What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general oil in coolant guide covers several possible causes across the whole BMW range, on the N47 the picture narrows considerably. This diesel engine's dominant failure signature is a milky, brownish, or oily substance in the coolant expansion tank, often accompanied by a drop in coolant level and a rise in oil level (due to coolant leaking into the oil). It is distinct from the simple coolant loss of a failing water pump or the external leaks of a split hose. On the N47, oil in coolant almost always points to an internal breach — and the most common culprits are a failed oil cooler (which uses engine coolant to cool the engine oil), a blown head gasket, or a cracked cylinder head — all of which are known issues on higher-mileage units.

Why This Engine In Particular

The N47's oil cooler is mounted between the oil filter housing and the engine block, and it uses engine coolant to regulate oil temperature. Over time, the seals inside the oil cooler can degrade, allowing oil and coolant to mix. This is one of the most common causes of oil in coolant on the N47. Additionally, the N47's aluminium cylinder head can develop cracks due to thermal stress, particularly if the engine has ever been overheated. A cracked head or blown head gasket can also allow oil and coolant to mix. This is a design characteristic of this particular engine family, and the same symptom on, say, a B47 diesel — with its revised oil cooler and head design — would point to a very different set of causes and is significantly less common.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed immediately if you find oil in the coolant on your N47. This is one of the most critical warning signs on this engine. Early intervention can save you from a much larger bill. If it's an oil cooler, the repair is straightforward and relatively affordable. If it's a head gasket or cracked head, the repair is more expensive, but catching it early can prevent catastrophic engine failure. Frequently Asked Questions

Is oil in coolant on an N47 always the oil cooler?

Not always — but it's one of the most common causes. Oil in coolant can also indicate a blown head gasket or a cracked cylinder head. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a pressure test, chemical test, and inspection of the oil cooler.

At what mileage does an N47 typically get oil in the coolant?

Most cases in our data occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, though oil cooler seal failure is more common on higher-mileage engines. Regular cooling system and oil cooler inspections can catch issues early.

Can I drive an N47 with oil in the coolant?

It is strongly recommended not to. Driving with oil in coolant can lead to overheating, loss of lubrication, and catastrophic engine failure. Have it inspected as soon as possible — ideally, don't start it again until a specialist has inspected it.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on an N47?

An oil cooler replacement costs £600–£1,200. A head gasket replacement costs £1,500–£2,500. If the cylinder head is cracked, you're looking at £2,500–£4,500. If the engine has suffered catastrophic damage, expect £4,500–£6,500 for a replacement engine.

What is the 70% scrap threshold for BMW?

BMW's repair-or-scrap threshold is 70% of the vehicle's market value. If the fitted replacement cost exceeds 70% of what your BMW is worth, it may be financially wiser to scrap or sell the car rather than repair. Use the Repair vs. Replace Calculator to decide.
