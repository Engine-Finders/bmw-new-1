N47 Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

The BMW N47 four-cylinder diesel engine (used across the E90 3 Series, F10 5 Series, E84 X1, and many others) requires a specific oil capacity, grade, and BMW Longlife approval. This page gives you the factory figures, the real-world caveats, and the one service detail that directly affects timing-chain health.

The BMW N47 takes 5.2 litres with filter change (5.0 litres without) of 5W-30 oil meeting BMW Longlife-04 (LL-04) approval [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs OEM workshop data].

  • Manufacturer service interval: 18,000 miles or 24 months on approved oil, but BMW specialists routinely recommend 8,000-mile intervals for N47 engines to protect the timing chain guides [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Applies to: E81/E87/E88/E90/E91/E92/E93 1/3 Series, E60/E61 5 Series, E83 X3, E84 X1, and many other N47-powered models — see the variant table for capacity differences.
  • N47 timing-chain warning: extended oil-change intervals are a known contributor to chain guide wear. Our N47 Timing Chain Failure page explains the full mechanism.

Notes & Caveats

The factory 18,000-mile oil-change interval for the N47 is a known tension point in the BMW community. Independent specialists we've spoken to consistently recommend a shorter, 8,000-mile interval for N47 engines, regardless of what the service computer indicates [EM-INTERVIEW]. The reason is the timing-chain guide system: extended oil intervals allow the oil to shear and accumulate contaminants, which accelerates wear on the plastic chain guides. A full mechanical explanation of this failure mode is available on our N47 Timing Chain Failure page, where we cover the diagnostic sequence and replacement costs.

In real UK driving conditions, the 18,000-mile factory interval is often too long. Short urban journeys, cold starts, and stop-start traffic all degrade the oil more quickly than the BMW service schedule assumes. This is why specialist advice tends toward halving the interval — it adds a service cost, but it is cheap compared to a timing-chain replacement or a full N47 rebuild.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting the BMW service computer blindly. The condition-based service system does not know your driving pattern is mostly short urban trips with minimal warm-up time.
  • Using a non-LL-04-approved oil. The N47's timing chain tensioner and DPF system are sensitive to oil quality. Using an oil that does not meet LL-04 can cause chain guide wear, DPF issues, and turbocharger problems — see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained for why this matters.
  • Confusing N47 capacity with N57 or M47. The N47 is 2.0 litres, but the N57 (3.0) and M47 (2.0, older design) have different capacities — check your engine code via our Engine Code Guide before filling.
  • Using 0W-30 instead of 5W-30. While some later BMW engines use 0W-30, the N47 was designed for 5W-30 LL-04. Using 0W-30 without the correct LL-04 approval is not recommended for this engine family.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The N47's oil filler cap is marked with the correct grade and is accessible from above without removing panels. The dipstick is also located on the top of the engine, making checks straightforward. Confirm your exact build spec via the engine number stamp described on our Engine Number Location guide before assuming these figures apply — early and late N47 production runs occasionally carry minor specification changes that are not always reflected in aftermarket reference sources.

Why This Specification Matters

Getting the oil specification wrong on an N47 is not a minor mistake. The engine's timing-chain guides, turbocharger, and DPF all rely on the correct oil pressure, shear stability, and low-SAPS formulation. Using a non-LL-04-approved oil can lead to chain guide wear, DPF clogging, and ultimately timing-chain failure — a repair that typically costs £1,500–£3,000 at an independent specialist (see our Replacement Cost by Engine Code guide for up-to-date figures).

The viscosity grade matters similarly: 5W-30 is specified for cold-flow characteristics that directly affect how quickly oil reaches the chain guides and bearings on a cold UK morning. This is also why the N47 Timing Chain Failure page describes oil-change frequency as a primary preventative measure — not just a service suggestion.
