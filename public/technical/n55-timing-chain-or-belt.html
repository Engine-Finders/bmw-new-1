The BMW N55 (3.0-litre single-turbo inline-six) uses a timing chain, not a belt. This is a duplex (two-row) metal chain that drives the camshafts and is designed to last the life of the engine.

Does the N55 Have a Timing Chain or a Belt?

The BMW N55 engine has a timing chain, not a belt. It is a duplex (two-row) roller chain that drives the two overhead camshafts from the crankshaft. This chain is enclosed within the engine, running in oil, and is designed to be maintenance-free for the life of the engine. While the N55's chain is generally robust, the plastic chain guides and tensioner can still wear, particularly if oil changes have been neglected.

N55 Timing Chain Design Details

The N55 uses a robust duplex chain system, similar to the N54 but with a single-turbo layout and some refinements.

  • Duplex roller chain: Two rows of links for increased strength and reliability compared to a simplex (single-row) chain.
  • Hydraulic tensioner: Uses oil pressure to maintain tension on the chain, ensuring it doesn't slacken or jump teeth.
  • Plastic guides: The chain runs over plastic guide rails. These guides can become brittle and break over time, especially if oil changes are neglected [PATTERN DATA].
  • Front-mounted chain location: Like the N54, the N55's timing chain is located at the front of the engine, making replacement more straightforward than the N47's rear-mounted design.
  • VANOS system: The chain drives the intake and exhaust camshafts, which are equipped with BMW's variable valve timing (VANOS). Failure of the chain or guides can cause VANOS faults and serious engine damage.

A full technical breakdown of the VANOS system is available on our VANOS System Explained page.

Symptoms of N55 Timing Chain Wear

  • Cold-start rattle: A distinct metallic rattle for 1–2 seconds on a cold start. This is often the first and most important warning sign.
  • Rattle under acceleration: A rattle that occurs when accelerating from low RPM, particularly noticeable on a warm engine.
  • Check engine light: Often accompanied by VANOS-related fault codes (e.g., P0011, P0016).
  • Loss of power: If the chain has skipped a tooth, the engine may lose power, run roughly, or go into limp mode.
  • Engine noise: A general increase in top-end engine noise or a "clattering" sound.

If you notice any of these, do not drive the car. Continued driving can lead to complete chain failure, piston-valve contact, and a destroyed engine. Stop driving and have the car transported to a specialist. For more details on the diagnostic process, see our N55 Cold Start Rattle symptom page.

Notes & Caveats

Replacement interval: BMW does not specify a replacement interval for the N55 timing chain. It is officially a "life-of-the-engine" component. However, given the known wear issues on high-mileage engines, many specialists recommend an inspection at 100,000 miles and, if the car has a history of long oil-change intervals, preventative replacement at 120,000–150,000 miles [EM-INTERVIEW]. The Replacement Cost by Engine Code guide covers N55 chain replacement costs.

Oil quality is critical: The timing chain tensioner is hydraulically operated by engine oil pressure. Using the correct BMW Longlife-01 oil and changing it at regular 8,000-mile intervals is the single most important preventative measure you can take for the N55. Extended oil change intervals are a known contributor to chain guide wear and can also exacerbate other N55 issues like valve cover oil leaks and VANOS solenoid failure.

How to Verify the Timing Chain on Your N55

A definitive inspection requires specialist tools and experience, but there are a few checks you can do:

  • Listen carefully: Pay attention to any unusual rattle on a cold start. This is the most accessible and reliable indicator.
  • Check engine oil: Use the correct 5W-30 LL-01 oil. The correct grade is covered in our N55 Oil Capacity guide.
  • Diagnostic scan: Check for stored fault codes. VANOS-related codes (P0011, P0016) strongly indicate timing chain issues.
  • Specialist inspection: A BMW specialist can remove the valve cover to physically check the chain tension and guide condition. They can also inspect the condition of the VANOS solenoids and camshaft adjusters.
