N57 Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

The BMW N57 3.0-litre inline-six diesel (used across the E90 330d, F10 530d, E70 X5 30d, and many others) requires a specific oil capacity, grade, and BMW Longlife approval. This page gives you the factory figures, the real-world caveats for a high‑torque diesel, and the service details that matter most.

The BMW N57 takes 6.5 litres with filter change (6.0 litres without) of 5W-30 oil meeting BMW Longlife-04 (LL-04) approval [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs OEM workshop data].

  • Manufacturer service interval: 18,000 miles or 24 months on approved oil, but BMW specialists routinely recommend 8,000-mile intervals for N57 engines to protect the timing chain guides [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Applies to: E90/E91/E92/E93 330d/335d, F10/F11 530d/535d, E70 X5 30d/35d, E83/F25 X3 30d, and many other N57-powered models — see the variant table for capacity differences.
  • N57 timing-chain warning: Like the N47, the N57 has a rear-mounted timing chain; extended oil-change intervals are a known contributor to chain guide wear. Our N57 Injector Seal Failure page covers related diesel issues, but for chain specifics see our N57 Timing Chain or Belt guide.

Notes & Caveats

The factory 18,000-mile oil-change interval for the N57 is one of the most debated topics in the BMW community. Independent specialists we've spoken to consistently recommend a shorter, 8,000-mile interval for N57 engines, regardless of what the service computer indicates [EM-INTERVIEW]. The reason is the rear-mounted timing chain design, shared with the N47: extended oil intervals allow the oil to shear and accumulate contaminants, which accelerates wear on the plastic chain guides. A full mechanical explanation of this failure mode is available on our N47 Timing Chain Failure page (the same principle applies to the N57).

In real UK driving conditions, the 18,000-mile factory interval is often too long. Short urban journeys, cold starts, and stop-start traffic all degrade the oil more quickly than the BMW service schedule assumes. This is why specialist advice tends toward halving the interval — it adds a service cost, but it is cheap compared to a timing-chain replacement or a full N57 rebuild.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting the BMW service computer blindly. The condition-based service system does not know your driving pattern is mostly short urban trips with minimal warm-up time.
  • Using a non-LL-04-approved oil. The N57's timing chain tensioner, DPF, and injector system are sensitive to oil quality. Using an oil that does not meet LL-04 can cause chain guide wear, DPF clogging, and injector issues — see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained for why this matters.
  • Confusing N57 capacity with N47 or M57. The N57 is 3.0 litres, but the N47 (2.0) and M57 (3.0, older design) have different capacities — check your engine code via our Engine Code Guide before filling.
  • Using 0W-30 instead of 5W-30. While some later BMW engines use 0W-30, the N57 was designed for 5W-30 LL-04. Using 0W-30 without the correct LL-04 approval is not recommended for this engine family, especially given the high torque and DPF regeneration heat.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The N57's oil filler cap is marked with the correct grade and is accessible from above without removing panels. The dipstick is also located on the top of the engine, making checks straightforward. Confirm your exact build spec via the engine number stamp described on our Engine Number Location guide before assuming these figures apply — early and late N57 production runs occasionally carry minor specification changes that are not always reflected in aftermarket reference sources.

Why This Specification Matters

Getting the oil specification wrong on an N57 is not a minor mistake. The engine's rear-mounted timing chain, DPF, and injector system all rely on the correct oil pressure, shear stability, and low-SAPS formulation. Using a non-LL-04-approved oil can lead to chain guide wear, DPF clogging, and ultimately timing-chain failure — a repair that typically costs £1,500–£3,000 at an independent specialist (see our Replacement Cost by Engine Code guide for up-to-date figures).

The viscosity grade matters similarly: 5W-30 is specified for cold-flow characteristics that directly affect how quickly oil reaches the chain guides and bearings on a cold UK morning. This is also why the specialist community treats the 8,000-mile oil-change interval as a primary preventative measure — not just a service suggestion.
