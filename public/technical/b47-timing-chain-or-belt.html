B47 Timing Chain or Belt? The Definitive Answer

The BMW B47 2.0-litre diesel uses a timing chain, not a belt. This page explains the chain design, location, service life, common issues, and what to look for to avoid expensive repairs.

The BMW B47 engine uses a timing chain, not a timing belt. Unlike belt-driven engines, the B47 chain is designed to last the life of the engine, but it can still suffer from tensioner wear or stretch, particularly on early or high-mileage examples.

  • Engine: B47D20A / B47D20B (2.0-litre diesel)
  • Timing system: Chain-driven (not a belt)
  • BMW official interval: No scheduled replacement — but specialist advice suggests inspection at 80,000–100,000 miles [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Key warning: Cold-start rattle is the most common early symptom — don't ignore it
  • Cost: Chain replacement: £1,200–£2,500 at independent specialists [EM-QUOTE]

Design & Location: Why B47 Is Different from N47

The B47 engine was a significant redesign from the earlier N47 diesel. One of the most important changes was the relocation of the timing chain.

The N47 engine had a notorious rear-mounted timing chain located at the flywheel end of the engine. This made chain replacement a major job requiring engine removal or a transmission-out process. The B47 moved the timing chain to the front of the engine, making it significantly more accessible. It also uses a more robust chain design with improved tensioners and guides, greatly reducing the catastrophic failure rate seen on the N47.

The B47 chain is driven by the crankshaft via a chain sprocket. It uses a hydraulic tensioner that relies on engine oil pressure to maintain correct tension. On some variants (e.g., X3 G01, 5 Series G30), there is also a secondary balancer shaft chain that should be inspected when the primary chain is being serviced.

Service Life: How Long Does the B47 Timing Chain Last?

BMW does not specify a service interval for the B47 timing chain. The official line is that the chain is designed to last the lifetime of the engine. However, independent specialists who work on these engines regularly report that chain wear and tensioner fatigue can occur from around 80,000 miles, particularly on cars that have had extended oil change intervals or have been used for short urban journeys.

The B47 is far more reliable than the N47, but it is not immune to timing chain issues. The most common cause of premature wear is oil degradation—if the oil is not changed regularly, sludge can build up in the tensioner, reducing its ability to maintain tension. This is why many specialists recommend reducing the oil change interval to 8,000–10,000 miles rather than relying on the 18,000-mile CBS system. Symptoms of Timing Chain Wear

Early detection is the key to avoiding a catastrophic engine failure. The following symptoms should prompt an immediate inspection:

  • Cold-start rattle — a metallic clattering sound that lasts 1–2 seconds on startup, then disappears. This is the most common and earliest warning sign.
  • Engine warning light — camshaft position sensor codes (e.g., P0016) or timing correlation faults.
  • Rough idle or misfire — if the chain has stretched enough to affect timing, the engine may run unevenly.
  • Loss of power / hesitation — particularly noticeable under acceleration as valve timing becomes less precise.
  • Oil sludge or metallic particles in the oil filter — a sign of chain guide wear.
  • Check engine light with cam/crank correlation codes — P0016, P0017, P000A, P000B are common.

If you hear a cold-start rattle, do not ignore it. A chain that fails while driving can cause catastrophic valve-to-piston contact, resulting in a full engine replacement. For a full diagnostic guide, see our cold-start rattle symptom hub.

Prevention and Early Detection

While the B47 is significantly more reliable than its predecessor, the N47, there are still steps you can take to minimise the risk of timing chain failure.

  • Shorten oil change intervals — reduce from 18,000 miles to 8,000–10,000 miles, especially if the car is used for short journeys or sporty driving.
  • Use the correct oil — BMW Longlife-04 low-SAPS oil is essential to prevent sludge buildup in the tensioner. See our B47 oil capacity page for full specs.
  • Listen for cold-start rattle — the single most important early warning sign. If you hear it, get the car inspected immediately.
  • Monitor the oil level and quality — low oil or dirty oil can starve the tensioner of pressure.
  • Inspect at 80,000–100,000 miles — even if there are no symptoms, a preventative inspection by a specialist can catch wear before it becomes catastrophic.
  • Avoid extended idling — prolonged idling can cause oil pressure drops that may affect the tensioner.
