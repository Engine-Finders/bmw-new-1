BMW M57 Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

Complete reference for the BMW M57 3.0L diesel engine – oil capacity with and without filter change, correct viscosity grade (5W-30), BMW Longlife-04 specification, and variant-specific differences across E46, E60, E90, X5, and other models.

The BMW M57 3.0L diesel engine takes approximately 6.5 litres of engine oil with filter change, and 6.0 litres without [THIRD-PARTY — BMW TIS]. The correct specification is BMW Longlife-04 (5W-30 or 0W-30) for most applications, with a change interval of 18,000 miles or 24 months under normal conditions, though many specialists recommend shorter intervals for high-mileage or heavy-use vehicles.

  • Capacity (with filter): 6.5 litres – always use a new filter and crush washer.
  • Capacity (without filter): 6.0 litres – change the filter every other service at minimum.
  • Specification: BMW Longlife-04 (for Euro 4/5) – do not use older LL-98 or LL-01.
  • Variant differences exist: early M57 (pre-2004) may have slightly different capacities; check your specific engine code.

Notes & Caveats

The M57 engine is known for its robustness and smoothness, but oil choice is critical. BMW Longlife-04 is a low-SAPS oil designed for diesel particulate filters (DPF) – using an older LL-01 or non-approved oil can lead to DPF clogging and increased ash build-up. Many specialists recommend reducing the interval to 10,000–12,000 miles for high-mileage cars or those used primarily for short journeys [EM-INTERVIEW]. Additionally, the oil level should be checked regularly; some M57 variants are prone to oil consumption, especially with higher mileage.

For detailed information on timing chain and other common issues, see our M57 timing chain guide.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong oil spec. Many owners mistakenly use LL-01 (old spec) which does not protect the DPF – always use LL-04 for M57 engines with DPF.
  • Overfilling. Adding too much oil can damage the turbocharger and cause excessive crankcase pressure – always check the dipstick and follow the correct level.
  • Ignoring the filter change. Changing oil without the filter leaves old contaminants – always replace the filter and crush washer.
  • Confusing M57 with N57. The N57 engine has different capacity and spec – see our N57 oil capacity page if you're unsure which engine you have.
  • Using 10W-40 or other viscosities. BMW specifies 5W-30 (or 0W-30) for cold start protection – using thicker oil can cause timing chain tensioner issues on higher-mileage engines.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick on the M57 is located on the left side of the engine block (driver's side in UK cars). The oil filler cap is on the top of the engine, usually marked with the oil specification. To confirm the exact capacity for your specific build, check the engine code stamp – it will be on a flat pad near the starter motor on the left side of the block. Use our engine number location guide to find it. Additionally, the BMW parts system using your VIN will provide the exact oil capacity and type for your vehicle – you can use our VIN decoder to get started.

Why This Specification Matters

Using the correct oil capacity and spec on the M57 is not just about performance – it affects the longevity of the timing chain, turbocharger, and DPF. Overfilling can lead to oil aeration, which reduces oil pressure and can starve the chain tensioner – a known failure point on high-mileage M57 engines. Underfilling can cause oil starvation, especially during cornering. Additionally, the LL-04 spec includes additives that protect the DPF and reduce ash accumulation, extending its life. This is why we always recommend using the correct oil and sticking to the recommended change intervals – or even shortening them for peace of mind.
