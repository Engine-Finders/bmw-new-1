S55 Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

The BMW S55 3.0‑litre twin‑turbo straight‑six (M3/M4) uses approximately 6.5 litres of 5W‑30 oil meeting BMW Longlife‑01, with a condition‑based service interval that often translates to 10,000–12,000 miles in road use, but track use demands shorter intervals. This is the dry technical reference — no opinions, just the numbers and the variant‑by‑variant differences you need to get the spec right.

Variant Differences

The S55 appeared in three main variants — the core oil capacity is consistent, but the oil cooler layout and dry‑sump reservoir size vary slightly, which affects the fill procedure.

Notes & Caveats

The S55 is a high‑performance engine that generates significant heat, especially on track. The standard LL‑01 oil is formulated to handle these temperatures, but extended intervals beyond 12,000 miles are not recommended even for road use. Specialists we've spoken to [EM‑INTERVIEW] note that many S55s that come in for crank‑hub or rod‑bearing work have oil that has sheared out of grade, contributing to the well‑known crank‑hub failure that plagues this engine family. Shortening the interval to 5,000 miles for track‑use cars is a common preventative measure, but that is owner‑choice, not factory guidance.

The capacity figure is approximate; always check the electronic dipstick (or physical dipstick on some models) after filling and run the engine to full temperature before topping up. Overfilling can cause aeration and oil foaming, which is especially harmful on this engine due to the high oil temperatures.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a non‑LL‑01 oil. Many 5W‑30 oils meet the viscosity but not the BMW Longlife‑01 approval – they lack the high‑temperature shear stability required for the S55. Always check the container for LL‑01.
  • Assuming the S55 shares capacity with the N55. The N55 (earlier 3.0) has a different capacity and oil spec – see our N55 oil capacity page. They are not interchangeable.
  • Trusting the CBS interval blindly. The condition‑based service system may indicate 15,000+ miles under ideal conditions – but track use and the high‑output design mean the oil degrades faster. Many specialists recommend a fixed 5,000‑8,000 mile interval for track cars regardless of what the computer says.
  • Overfilling. The electronic dipstick on some models is sensitive; overfilling can cause high‑oil‑level warnings and aeration that damages bearings. Never exceed the max mark.
  • Ignoring oil consumption. The S55 is known to consume oil – that's not necessarily a fault, but if you ignore topping up and let the level drop, the hydraulic tensioners and variable valve timing can suffer.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The oil filler cap is on the left‑hand side of the engine and is clearly marked with the oil grade. The electronic oil level can be accessed via the iDrive menu (or dipstick on earlier models). To verify the exact capacity for your specific model, check the engine number stamp (described on our engine number location guide) and confirm the variant code. The workshop manual specifies that after an oil change, the engine must be run to operating temperature, then switched off and left for a few minutes before the final level check – this is critical for the dry‑sump system.

Why This Specification Matters

Getting the oil wrong on an S55 isn't a minor inconvenience – it's a direct contributor to the engine's notorious reliability issues. The LL‑01 specification guarantees a specific level of high‑temperature shear stability and anti‑wear additives that protect the turbocharger bearings and the variable valve lift system (Valvetronic). Using a non‑approved oil can lead to sludge formation, which blocks oil galleries and starves the turbos, eventually causing the crank‑hub failure that so many owners experience. The 6.5‑litre capacity is large for a straight‑six, but that's because of the large oil cooler and the dry‑sump system – under‑filling by even a litre can cause pressure drops at high RPM, and over‑filling can aerate the oil and cause hydraulic lifter noise. This is why having the exact specification, verified against your variant, is the first step to keeping an S55 alive.
