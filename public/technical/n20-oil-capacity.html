BMW N20 Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

Complete reference for the BMW N20 2.0L turbo petrol engine – oil capacity with and without filter change, correct viscosity grade (5W-30), BMW Longlife-01 FE / LL-04 specification, and variant-specific differences across F30, F10, X1, X3, and other models.

The BMW N20 2.0L turbo petrol engine takes approximately 5.0 litres of engine oil with filter change, and 4.8 litres without [THIRD-PARTY — BMW TIS]. The correct specification is BMW Longlife-01 FE (5W-30) or Longlife-04 for most applications, with a change interval of 12,000 miles or 12 months under normal conditions, though many specialists recommend shorter intervals for turbocharged engines.

  • Capacity (with filter): 5.0 litres – always use a new filter and crush washer.
  • Capacity (without filter): 4.8 litres – change the filter every other service at minimum.
  • Specification: BMW Longlife-01 FE (or LL-04) – do not use older LL-98 or non-approved oils.
  • Variant differences exist: early N20 (pre-2013) may have slightly different capacities; check your specific engine code.

Notes & Caveats

The N20 engine is a turbocharged direct-injection petrol, and oil choice is critical. BMW Longlife-01 FE is a low-ash oil designed for turbocharger protection – using an older LL-98 or non-approved oil can lead to turbo bearing failure and sludge formation. Many specialists recommend reducing the interval to 8,000–10,000 miles for high-performance or frequently driven cars [EM-INTERVIEW]. Additionally, the oil level should be checked regularly; some N20 variants are prone to oil consumption, especially with higher mileage.

For detailed information on timing chain and other common issues, see our N20 timing chain guide and N20 timing chain tensioner failure page.

Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong oil spec. Many owners mistakenly use LL-04 (diesel spec) which is not optimised for the N20's turbocharger – always use LL-01 FE for petrol N20 engines without DPF.
  • Overfilling. Adding too much oil can damage the turbocharger and cause excessive crankcase pressure – always check the dipstick and follow the correct level.
  • Ignoring the filter change. Changing oil without the filter leaves old contaminants – always replace the filter and crush washer.
  • Confusing N20 with B48. The newer B48 engine has different capacity and spec – see our B48 oil capacity page if you're unsure which engine you have.
  • Using 10W-40 or other viscosities. BMW specifies 5W-30 (or 0W-30) for cold start protection – using thicker oil can cause timing chain tensioner issues on higher-mileage engines.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick on the N20 is located on the left side of the engine block (driver's side in UK cars). The oil filler cap is on the top of the engine, usually marked with the oil specification. To confirm the exact capacity for your specific build, check the engine code stamp – it will be on a flat pad near the starter motor on the left side of the block. Use our engine number location guide to find it. Additionally, the BMW parts system using your VIN will provide the exact oil capacity and type for your vehicle – you can use our VIN decoder to get started.

Why This Specification Matters

Using the correct oil capacity and spec on the N20 is critical for turbocharger longevity, timing chain health, and overall engine performance. Overfilling can lead to oil aeration, which reduces oil pressure and can starve the chain tensioner – a known failure point on higher-mileage N20 engines. Underfilling can cause oil starvation, especially during hard cornering. Additionally, the LL-01 FE spec includes additives that protect the turbocharger bearings and reduce sludge formation, extending the life of both the engine and turbo. This is why we always recommend using the correct oil and sticking to the recommended change intervals – or even shortening them for peace of mind.
