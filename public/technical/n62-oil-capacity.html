N62 Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

The BMW N62 4.4-litre and 4.8-litre V8 (used across the E60 5 Series, E63 6 Series, E65 7 Series, E70 X5, and many others) requires a specific oil capacity, grade, and BMW Longlife approval. This page gives you the factory figures, the real-world caveats for the hot-vee V8, and the service details that matter most.

The BMW N62 takes 8.0 litres with filter change (7.5 litres without) of 5W-30 oil meeting BMW Longlife-01 (LL-01) approval [PLACEHOLDER — confirm vs OEM workshop data].

  • Manufacturer service interval: 15,000 miles or 24 months on approved oil, but BMW specialists routinely recommend 8,000-mile intervals for N62 engines to protect the hot-vee turbo system and valve seals [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Applies to: E60/E61 545i/550i, E63/E64 645Ci/650i, E65/E66 745i/750i, E70 X5 4.8i, and many other N62-powered models — see the variant table for capacity differences.
  • N62 oil health warning: the hot-vee design generates significant heat, which degrades oil faster than in naturally aspirated engines. Frequent oil changes are the single most important preventative measure for this engine.

Variant Differences

The N62 was fitted across several BMW models and chassis codes, with two primary displacements: 4.4L (N62B44) and 4.8L (N62B48). While the core specification is consistent, sump capacity varies slightly by application.

Notes & Caveats

The factory 15,000-mile oil-change interval for the N62 is one of the most debated topics in the BMW community. Independent specialists we've spoken to consistently recommend a shorter, 8,000-mile interval for N62 engines, regardless of what the service computer indicates [EM-INTERVIEW]. The reason is the hot-vee architecture: the exhaust manifolds and catalytic converters sit in the 'V' between the cylinder banks, generating intense heat that degrades oil much faster than in conventional V8 layouts. This accelerated degradation directly affects the hydraulic valve lifters, the VANOS system, and the valve stem seals — a well-documented N62 weakness.

In real UK driving conditions, the 15,000-mile factory interval is often too long. Short urban journeys, cold starts, and stop-start traffic all degrade the oil more quickly than the BMW service schedule assumes. This is why specialist advice tends toward halving the interval — it adds a service cost, but it is cheap compared to a valve stem seal replacement or a full N62 rebuild. For a full breakdown of N62 failure patterns, see our N62 Alternator Access page and Valve Stem Seal Wear page.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting the BMW service computer blindly. The condition-based service system does not know your driving pattern is mostly short urban trips with minimal warm-up time.
  • Using a non-LL-01-approved oil. The N62's hydraulic valve lifters, VANOS system, and valve stem seals are sensitive to oil quality. Using an oil that does not meet LL-01 can cause valve seal wear, VANOS faults, and chain guide wear — see our VANOS System Explained for why this matters.
  • Confusing N62 capacity with N63 or M62. The N62 is 4.4/4.8 litres, but the N63 (twin-turbo V8) and M62 (older V8) have different capacities — check your engine code via our Engine Code Guide before filling.
  • Ignoring oil level sensor warnings. The N62's electronic oil level sensor can fail, giving false readings. Many specialists recommend periodically checking the oil level via the iDrive system and verifying with a manual dipstick if fitted.
  • Using 0W-30 instead of 5W-30. While some later BMW engines use 0W-30, the N62 was designed for 5W-30 LL-01. Using 0W-30 without the correct LL-01 approval is not recommended for this engine family, especially given the high operating temperatures.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The N62's oil filler cap is marked with the correct grade and is accessible from above without removing panels. However, like many modern BMWs, the N62 does not have a traditional dipstick — oil level is checked via the iDrive system. Confirm your exact build spec via the engine number stamp described on our Engine Number Location guide before assuming these figures apply — early and late N62 production runs occasionally carry minor specification changes that are not always reflected in aftermarket reference sources.

Why This Specification Matters

Getting the oil specification wrong on an N62 is not a minor mistake. The engine's hot-vee architecture, VANOS, and hydraulic valve lifters all rely on the correct oil pressure, shear stability, and thermal resistance. Using a non-LL-01-approved oil or extending the change interval too far can lead to:

  • Valve stem seal wear: Heat causes valve stem seals to harden and fail, leading to blue smoke on startup — a repair that typically costs £2,500–£4,500 at an independent specialist (see our Replacement Cost by Engine Code guide).
  • VANOS issues: Contaminated oil can clog VANOS solenoids, causing rough idle, loss of power, and poor fuel economy.
  • Timing chain guide wear: While less common than on the N47, chain guide wear can occur on high-mileage N62 engines with poor oil change history.
  • Alternator bracket oil leak: The N62 is known for oil leaks from the alternator bracket gasket — a failure exacerbated by degraded oil.

The viscosity grade matters similarly: 5W-30 is specified for cold-flow characteristics that directly affect how quickly oil reaches the camshaft bearings and valve train on a cold UK morning. This is also why the specialist community treats the 8,000-mile oil-change interval as a primary preventative measure — not just a service suggestion.
