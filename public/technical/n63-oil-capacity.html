N63 Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

The BMW N63 4.4‑litre twin‑turbo V8 uses approximately 8.5 litres of 5W‑30 oil meeting BMW Longlife‑01, with a condition‑based service interval that often translates to 10,000–12,000 miles in real UK use. This is the dry technical reference — no opinions, just the numbers and the variant‑by‑variant differences you need to get the spec right.

Variant Differences

The N63 appeared in three main revisions — the core oil capacity is consistent, but the oil cooler layout and sump design vary slightly, which affects the fill procedure.

All variants require the same viscosity and BMW LL‑01 approval. The dry‑sump system in later cars holds the same capacity but the level must be checked with the engine running at operating temperature per the workshop manual.

Notes & Caveats

The N63 is a "hot‑vee" engine — the turbochargers sit inside the V, so the oil and coolant are subjected to significantly higher temperatures than in a conventional layout. This makes oil degradation a real concern. The standard LL‑01 oil is formulated to handle these temperatures, but extended intervals beyond 12,000 miles are not recommended in UK stop‑start traffic. Specialists we've spoken to [EM‑INTERVIEW] note that many N63s that come in for turbo or valve‑seal work have oil that has sheared out of grade, contributing to the well‑known oil consumption issue that plagues this engine family. Shortening the interval to 8,000 miles is a common preventative measure, but that is owner‑choice, not factory guidance.

The capacity figure is approximate; always check the dipstick (or electronic level) after filling and run the engine to full temperature before topping up. Overfilling can cause aeration and oil foaming, which is especially harmful on this engine due to the high oil temperatures.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a non‑LL‑01 oil. Many 5W‑30 oils meet the viscosity but not the BMW Longlife‑01 approval – they lack the high‑temperature shear stability required for the N63. Always check the container for LL‑01.
  • Assuming all 4.4 V8s share the same capacity. The N62 (earlier 4.4) has a different capacity and oil spec – see our N62 oil capacity page. They are not interchangeable.
  • Trusting the CBS interval blindly. The condition‑based service system may indicate 15,000+ miles under ideal conditions – but UK traffic and the hot‑vee design mean the oil degrades faster. Many specialists recommend a fixed 8,000‑10,000 mile interval regardless of what the computer says.
  • Overfilling. The electronic dipstick on some models is sensitive; overfilling can cause high‑oil‑level warnings and aeration that damages bearings. Never exceed the max mark.
  • Ignoring oil consumption. The N63 is known to consume oil – that's not necessarily a fault, but if you ignore topping up and let the level drop, the hydraulic tensioners and variable valve timing can suffer.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The oil filler cap is on the left‑hand cylinder bank (passenger side in RHD cars) and is clearly marked with the oil grade. The electronic oil level can be accessed via the iDrive menu (or dipstick on earlier models). To verify the exact capacity for your specific model, check the engine number stamp (described on our engine number location guide) and confirm the variant code. The workshop manual specifies that after an oil change, the engine must be run to operating temperature, then switched off and left for a few minutes before the final level check – this is critical for the dry‑sump system on later cars.

Why This Specification Matters

Getting the oil wrong on an N63 isn't a minor inconvenience – it's a direct contributor to the engine's notorious reliability issues. The LL‑01 specification guarantees a specific level of high‑temperature shear stability and anti‑wear additives that protect the turbocharger bearings and the variable valve lift system (Valvetronic). Using a non‑approved oil can lead to sludge formation in the hot‑vee, which blocks oil galleries and starves the turbos, eventually causing the oil consumption and valve‑seal failure that so many owners experience. The 8.5‑litre capacity is large for a V8, but that's because of the large oil cooler and the internal oil passages – under‑filling by even a litre can cause pressure drops at high RPM, and over‑filling can aerate the oil and cause hydraulic lifter noise. This is why having the exact specification, verified against your variant, is the first step to keeping an N63 alive.
