Does the N47 Have a Timing Chain or a Belt?

The BMW N47 engine has a timing chain, not a belt. It is a duplex (two-row) roller chain that drives the two overhead camshafts from the crankshaft. This chain is enclosed within the engine, running in oil, and is designed to be maintenance-free for the life of the engine. However, the plastic chain guides and tensioner have proven to be a weak point on early production N47 engines, leading to a known failure pattern that can cause the chain to skip teeth or break.

N47 Timing Chain Design Details

The N47 uses a robust duplex chain system, but its vulnerability lies in the guides and tensioner.

  • Duplex roller chain: Two rows of links for increased strength and reliability compared to a simplex (single-row) chain.
  • Hydraulic tensioner: Uses oil pressure to maintain tension on the chain, ensuring it doesn't slacken or jump teeth.
  • Plastic guides: The chain runs over plastic guide rails. These guides can become brittle and break over time, especially if oil changes are neglected [PATTERN DATA].
  • Rear-mounted chain location: The N47's timing chain is located at the rear of the engine, making replacement a labour-intensive job that requires the engine or transmission to be removed.

The rear-mounted chain location is a key reason the N47 is more expensive to repair than the later B47 engine, which has a front-mounted chain.

Symptoms of N47 Timing Chain Wear

  • Cold-start rattle: A distinct metallic rattle for 1–2 seconds on a cold start. This is often the first and most important warning sign.
  • Rattle under acceleration: A rattle that occurs when accelerating from low RPM, particularly noticeable on a warm engine.
  • Check engine light: Often accompanied by timing-related fault codes (e.g., P0011, P0016).
  • Loss of power: If the chain has skipped a tooth, the engine may lose power, run roughly, or go into limp mode.
  • Engine noise: A general increase in top-end engine noise or a "clattering" sound.

If you notice any of these, do not drive the car. Continued driving can lead to complete chain failure, piston-valve contact, and a destroyed engine. Stop driving and have the car transported to a specialist. For more details on the diagnostic process, see our N47 Cold Start Rattle symptom page.

Notes & Caveats

Replacement interval: BMW does not specify a replacement interval for the N47 timing chain. It is officially a "life-of-the-engine" component. However, given the known guide wear issues, many specialists recommend an inspection at 80,000 miles and, if the car has a history of long oil-change intervals, preventative replacement at 100,000–120,000 miles [EM-INTERVIEW]. The N47 Timing Chain Failure page covers the replacement cost in full.

Oil quality is critical: The timing chain tensioner is hydraulically operated by engine oil pressure. Using the correct BMW Longlife-04 oil and changing it at regular 8,000-mile intervals is the single most important preventative measure you can take. Extended oil change intervals are a known contributor to chain guide wear.

How to Verify the Timing Chain on Your N47

A definitive inspection requires specialist tools and experience, but there are a few checks you can do:

  • Listen carefully: Pay attention to any unusual rattle on a cold start. This is the most accessible and reliable indicator.
  • Check engine oil: Use the correct 5W-30 LL-04 oil. The correct grade is covered in our N47 Oil Capacity guide.
  • Diagnostic scan: Check for stored fault codes. Timing-related codes (P0011, P0016) strongly indicate timing chain issues.
  • Specialist inspection: A BMW specialist can use a bore-scope to inspect the guides through the oil filler cap or remove the valve cover to physically check the chain tension and guide condition.
